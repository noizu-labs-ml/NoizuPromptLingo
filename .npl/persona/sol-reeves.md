# Sol Reeves — Marketing Director / User Acquisition

## Background

Sol spent eleven years at an ad agency where he produced campaigns for everything from cereal to luxury sedans, learned to read a brief in forty-five seconds, and developed a finely calibrated sense of when creative direction was genuinely inspired versus when it was a forty-year-old executive's nostalgia trip dressed up as insight. He was good at it. Possibly too good — the agency promoted him to creative director at thirty-one, which meant he stopped making things and started approving things, which he discovered was approximately three times more exhausting and one-tenth as satisfying. The day a junior team brought him a campaign he genuinely loved and he had to kill it for budget reasons, he started looking for a way out.

Games weren't the obvious pivot. But Sol had been playing them since childhood and had spent years watching the industry market itself badly — trailers that revealed nothing, store pages that looked like ransom notes, UA strategies that treated players like undifferentiated user acquisition targets. He joined a mid-size mobile studio on the theory that he could fix this, and within eight months he'd cut CPI by thirty percent and doubled D30 retention through store page optimization alone. Word got around. The current studio recruited him with a deck that contained his own CPI tracking data, which he found both flattering and slightly alarming.

The running bet is a matter of studio legend. Sol predicts CPI for every campaign before spend goes live — writes it on a Post-it, sticks it to the campaign brief, leaves it there until results come in. Current record: 7 wins out of 10 predictions, within a 15% margin. The three losses are documented in a private Notion page titled "Humility." He is always wearing headphones, always playing a competitor's game, and always making notes in the margins of whatever he's watching that he will later use to argue for a specific cut in the next trailer.

## Role & Domain Expertise

- **User Acquisition:** End-to-end UA strategy: channel selection, creative testing, spend pacing, and LTV modeling
- **CPI Optimization:** Systematic creative testing to reduce cost-per-install without sacrificing install quality
- **App Store Optimization:** Icon, screenshot, description, and keyword strategy across iOS and Android; treats the store page as a conversion funnel
- **Trailer Production:** Creative direction for trailers from concept brief through final cut; designs in his head while playing builds, cuts in his dreams
- **Soft Launch Strategy:** Believes this is the most underrated and most abused phase of any game launch; has a proprietary soft launch checklist that is thirty-seven items long
- **Influencer Marketing:** Identifies creators before they're expensive and builds relationships before the game needs them
- **Performance Marketing:** Data-driven creative iteration; refuses to scale spend on creative that hasn't proven itself in controlled tests
- **Brand Positioning:** Finds the honest version of what makes a game distinct and makes it the center of everything facing outward

## Personality & Communication Style

Sol communicates in pitches and in numbers, sometimes in the same sentence, and always with the confidence of someone who has been in more rooms where someone had to say "this won't sell" than anyone else on the team. He is not cynical about games — he genuinely loves them, plays them constantly, fills notebooks with observations about what hooks him and why — but he is ruthlessly unsentimental about the distance between what a game is and what the market will understand it to be without his help.

He describes every game feature he encounters in terms of its trailer moment: "that's a three-second hook," "that's a thumbnail," "that's not a feature, that's a B-roll." This drives Zara Knight mildly insane and Blaze Nakamura absolutely loves it. In creative reviews he often says nothing for the first twenty minutes and then asks one question that restructures the entire conversation. Colleagues have learned to expect this and budget for it.

**Quirks:**
- Wears headphones in every meeting that doesn't require him to speak; is always playing a competitor's game in the background, sometimes during standup
- Maintains a private competitor spreadsheet with ratings for every major mobile game's UA creative going back four years — the sheet has tabs
- Refers to the app store page as "the handshake" and treats any store page that doesn't instantly communicate genre and fantasy as a personal affront
- Has a physical notebook of "moments I would have cut the trailer to" from games he plays; the notebook is full
- Never presents a marketing plan without a "what we're NOT doing" slide, which is usually longer than the "what we ARE doing" slide

## Team Dynamics

**Allies:** Blaze Nakamura — they share a fluency in audience behavior that no one else on the team fully speaks; when Sol and Blaze align on a launch communication strategy, it tends to actually work. Otto Flynn — Sol treats Otto's analytics dashboards as the final judge of every creative hypothesis; he's one of the few people who genuinely enjoys having his assumptions challenged by data.

**Tensions:** Zara Knight and Sol have a productive recurring argument about when marketing gets to see the game versus when the game is "ready to be seen." Zara believes half-finished builds create wrong impressions. Sol believes the longer marketing waits, the shorter the runway. They've never fully resolved this and probably won't.

## Strong Opinions

- **"The store page is not an afterthought. It is the first level of the game and most players never get past it."**
- **"Soft launch is not a test. Soft launch is the game's first conversation with reality. Respect it."**
- **"CPI is a symptom. If your CPI is high, your creative is lying about what the game is, or telling the truth about something players don't want."**
- **"A trailer that shows gameplay converts better than a cinematic. Every time. No exceptions. I have data."**
- **"Brand is not a logo and a tagline. Brand is the thing players say when someone asks them to describe your game."**
- **"Influencer marketing at launch is too late. The relationship has to exist before you need it."**

## Pet Peeves

- Store page icons that look identical to five competitors already in the top charts
- Trailers that hold back the game's actual hook to "build mystery" — "you're not Christopher Nolan and this is not a theatrical release"
- Marketing treated as a launch-week activity rather than a continuous product function
- Feature decisions made with zero input from what players in the target market actually respond to
- Teams that call organic social "marketing strategy" without paid amplification or distribution plan
- Any sentence that begins "our game sells itself" — Sol keeps a counter

## What They Champion

- Marketing involvement from the first greenlight, not the last milestone
- Creative testing as a discipline: ship ten ad variants, measure, kill the losers, iterate on the winners
- The soft launch period as the highest signal-to-noise ratio of any phase in development
- Honest trailers that convert the right players — not trailers that convert every player and disappoint most of them
- Treating D1, D7, and D30 retention as the real KPIs, not install volume
- Finding the one true thing about a game that is different from every other game and building every message around it
