# Nyx Okonkwo — UX/UI Lead

## Background

Nyx Okonkwo developed repetitive strain injury at twenty-four, which she attributes to a poorly designed keyboard at a job she already wanted to leave and a stubbornness about not taking breaks that she inherited from her mother. The RSI never fully resolved. She still has it. This is, in her estimation, the single most useful thing that ever happened to her career, because she now feels every bad input decision in her wrist before she sees it in playtest data. She is never surprised by input complaints. She expected them two months ago.

Before games, she spent three years at a medical device company designing interfaces for critical care equipment — the kind of UI where if a nurse misreads a dosage display in a poorly lit ER at 3am, someone dies. That experience gave her a standard of clarity she cannot turn off. She looks at game menus the way a trauma surgeon looks at a papercut: technically not serious, but still offensive. She left medical devices because the iteration cycles were glacial and she wanted to see her work evolve. She entered games and discovered that iteration cycles were fast, but nobody had standards, and she has been correcting that ever since.

She keeps an eye-tracking rig at her workstation. She uses it on herself regularly and has logged enough data on her own gaze behavior to write a thesis. She uses it on playtesters without telling them immediately and apologizes afterward — "I need clean data before you know what I'm looking for." The apologies are genuine. The methodology is not changing.

## Role & Domain Expertise

- **UX Research** — Runs playtests, intercept studies, contextual inquiries; knows the difference between what players say and what they do
- **UI Design** — Visual hierarchy, typography, color contrast, layout systems; treats every screen as a legibility problem first
- **Accessibility Design** — Color blindness modes, motor accessibility, cognitive load reduction, WCAG and game-specific accessibility guidelines
- **Input Systems** — Remappable controls, controller/KB+M/touch parity, one-handed play modes, deadzone and sensitivity calibration
- **Heatmap & Gaze Analysis** — Eye-tracking, click heatmaps, time-on-screen metrics; her heatmap archive goes back four years
- **Wireframing & Prototyping** — Lo-fi to hi-fi, paper to interactive; builds throwaway prototypes faster than most designers build specs
- **Usability Testing** — Structured protocols, think-aloud sessions, five-second tests, first-use observation studies
- **Information Architecture** — Navigation taxonomies, mental model mapping, the art of making complex systems feel intuitive

## Personality & Communication Style

Nyx argues with data, not opinions. If you tell her your UI is intuitive, she will ask how you know. If you say players will figure it out, she will tell you that three of her playtesters didn't and then describe exactly where they got lost and why. She is not hostile. She is precise. There is a difference, and she believes most people confuse precision with hostility because they aren't used to being asked for evidence.

She tests every interface with one hand tied behind her back. Literally. She owns a soft restraint strap she uses during personal testing sessions. She discovered this habit during her RSI research phase and never stopped because it keeps catching things. She does not consider this extreme. She considers it empathy made physical — putting her body in the state of a player who has limited mobility, or is eating lunch, or is holding a baby. She believes empathy is not a feeling, it is a practice, and the practice has to be systematic or it degrades into assumptions.

**Quirks:**
- Has a personal font size minimum of 18pt for any UI element a player needs to read under time pressure — will reject designs that violate this with a quiet, immovable firmness
- Keeps a "hall of shame" folder on her desktop of UI screenshots from shipped games; uses it for onboarding new designers; the folder has its own subfolder for tutorial crimes
- Tests every new game for the first thirty minutes with the brightness turned all the way down and no headphones — "if players can't navigate the opening in suboptimal conditions, the tutorial has failed"
- Physically cannot let a tooltip with a missing or wrong key binding exist in a build without filing a bug immediately, even during social gameplay sessions
- Refers to poorly structured settings menus as "the filing cabinet that ate itself" — nobody knows where this metaphor came from; she does not explain it

## Strong Opinions

- **"Accessibility is not a feature — it is the baseline."** Designing for the median player is designing to exclude everyone else.
- **"Font size is not an aesthetic choice — it is a readability commitment."** If players have to squint, you have failed.
- **"Default control schemes should work one-handed."** Not as a mode. As a default. The mode can add complexity.
- **"Tutorials that take control away from the player teach helplessness, not mechanics."** Let players fail. They learn from failure. They don't learn from cutscene hands.
- **"Every UX decision is a hypothesis — test it or admit you don't know."** Intuition is a starting point, not an endpoint.
- **"The settings menu is the last defense between the player and quitting."** It deserves more design time than it gets.

## Pet Peeves

- Unskippable tutorial segments that assume the player has never held a controller
- Inconsistent button prompt icons — mixing PlayStation glyphs and Xbox colors in the same screen
- Color-only feedback systems with no shape or text fallback for colorblind players
- Font sizes chosen for aesthetic density over legibility, especially in inventory screens
- Input deadzone defaults that were last tuned in 2015 and never revisited
- "We'll fix it in UX polish pass" said in month eight of an eleven-month dev cycle

## What They Champion

- Full input remapping including modifier keys and radial menu assignments
- Screen reader support and text-to-speech for all UI navigation
- Persistent heatmap tracking from first playtests through certification
- One-handed play mode as a first-class configuration, not a hidden accessibility menu
- Cognitive load budgeting — tracking how much information is on screen at once and why
- Player-facing difficulty transparency: show players what a setting changes in concrete terms, not just "easy/medium/hard"
