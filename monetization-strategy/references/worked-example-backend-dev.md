## 3. Stream Selection Rationale

### Primary Stream: AI Templates

**Score: 8.2/10 — clear winner.**

**Why AI Templates wins for Alex:**

1. **Skills alignment is near-perfect.** Alex's Python, PostgreSQL, Docker, and API expertise are exactly the kind of deep knowledge that makes valuable templates. The prompts will be authoritative because they come from production experience.

2. **Timeline works.** First product can be scoped, built, and launched in 2-4 weeks at 8 hrs/week. This hits the 3-month revenue goal.

3. **Batch-friendly.** Alex can spend a weekend afternoon (4 hours) writing prompts and a couple of evenings (4 hours) polishing and setting up the listing. No need for daily consistency.

4. **Zero cost to start.** Gumroad is free. No ad budget needed for first product.

5. **The marketing weakness is manageable.** Alex doesn't need marketing skills for the first product. Gumroad Discover provides passive traffic. After the first product validates, Alex can learn marketing incrementally.

### Secondary Stream: Content Publishing (later)

**Score: 5.6/10 — not now, but add later.**

**When to add:** After first AI template product is live and validated (month 3-4). Content becomes the distribution channel for templates.

**Why not now:**
- Timeline doesn't work (Alex wants revenue in 3 months)
- No writing habit yet (building one while also building a product = too much)
- Better to have a product to promote BEFORE building an audience

**Why eventually:**
- Articles about Python/Docker/K8s drive traffic to template products
- Newsletter captures template buyers for repeat sales
- Content compounds over time (SEO)

### Why NOT POD

- Alex rated creative skills 4/10 and interest 3/10
- Design-dependent stream for someone who says "I can't make things look professional"
- Would require learning AI image generation AND design principles — too much new skill acquisition alongside product building

## 4. First 90-Day Plan

### Phase 1: Weeks 1-2 — Research & Scope (8 hrs)

| Day | Task | Hours | Output |
|-----|------|-------|--------|
| Sat (W1) | Niche research: which Python/backend problem has demand? | 3h | 5 niche candidates scored |
| Tue (W1) | Competitor analysis: what exists on Gumroad for chosen niche? | 1h | Gap analysis |
| Thu (W1) | Scope product: 10-15 prompts, what's included, what's not | 1h | Product scope doc |
| Sat (W2) | Start writing prompts (first 5) | 3h | 5 draft prompts |

**Key decision (end of Week 1):** Which niche to target.

**Alex's niche options (pre-scored):**

| Niche | Pain | Competition | Alex's Edge | Score |
|-------|------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Python API debugging prompts | High | Low | Strong (daily experience) | 8.0 |
| Docker optimization prompts | High | Medium | Good | 7.2 |
| PostgreSQL performance prompts | Medium | Low | Strong (6 years) | 7.5 |
| Backend interview prep prompts | High | High | Moderate | 5.5 |

**Recommended first product:** Python API Debugging Prompts (highest score, strongest personal edge).

| Week | Focus | Key Activity |

## 6. What Makes This Example Realistic

1. **No existing audience.** Alex starts from zero. First product relies on Gumroad Discover + organic community posting, not a pre-built email list.

2. **8 hrs/week is tight.** The plan accounts for this — batch work on weekends, light tasks on evenings. No "and also write 3 articles per week."

3. **$1K/month in 12 months is achievable but not guaranteed.** The revenue projections show it taking 9-12 months conservatively. Telling someone it'll happen in 3 months would be dishonest.

4. **The marketing weakness is real.** Alex has zero marketing experience. The plan doesn't pretend that's fine — it uses channels that require minimal marketing skill (Gumroad marketplace, Reddit genuine participation, Dev.to articles).

5. **The secondary stream comes later.** Content publishing starts in month 3-4, not day 1. Building two things simultaneously with 8 hrs/week is a recipe for building zero things.
