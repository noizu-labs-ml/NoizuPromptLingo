# Wrath of the Golden Serpent

**Narrative Story / Metroidvania**

## Core Loop
You are an immortal summoner whose soul was fractured by a golden serpent -- a leviathan that devours memories. Each area of the interconnected world you explore restores a fragment of your past, granting you a new summon ability. You summon elementals to fight, phase through barriers, and solve environmental puzzles. The world branches and loops back on itself, with previously inaccessible areas opening up as you recover more memories. Combat is deliberate and tactical: you summon creatures in real-time but each summon drains from a shared memory pool, forcing you to choose between powerful single summons or swarms of weaker allies. The narrative unfolds through environmental storytelling, memory-flash cutscenes triggered by exploring hidden rooms, and NPC encounters that remember you differently depending on which memories you have restored.

## Features
- **Memory Fragment Summons**: Each recovered memory unlocks a new summon creature and simultaneously reveals a piece of the narrative -- gameplay progression IS story progression
- **Shared Memory Pool**: All summons draw from one resource bar, creating strategic tension between fielding a few powerful allies versus many weak ones
- **Recursive World Design**: The map loops and interconnects like a fractal -- areas you explore early reveal hidden passages once you recover specific memories, rewarding thorough backtracking

## Why This Is Awesome
**Story-Driven Explorer** players will be hooked by the central mystery: your own fragmented identity. Every memory fragment is both a gameplay upgrade and a story revelation, making exploration feel doubly rewarding. The question "who was I before the serpent?" drives the entire experience.

**Challenge Seeker** players will find deep combat satisfaction in optimizing summon loadouts against boss encounters that demand specific elemental counters, and the shared memory pool creates genuine resource-management tension during the hardest fights.

**Flow State** players will appreciate the seamless world design -- no loading screens between areas, no level select menus. You simply walk from one zone to the next, and the map folds back on itself so naturally that navigation becomes instinctive.
