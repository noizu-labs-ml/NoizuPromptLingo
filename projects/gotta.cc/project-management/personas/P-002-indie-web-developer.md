# Theo Blackwell — The Builder Seeking Daylight

## Demographics
| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 29 |
| **Role** | Freelance developer + indie hacker |
| **Technical Level** | High |
| **Industry** | Software / SaaS |
| **Location** | Berlin, Germany |

## Bio
Theo has shipped six personal projects in three years — some tools, some essays, one micro-SaaS that earns $200/month. He posts on Hacker News, writes about what he builds, and genuinely cares about the quality of his work. His frustration is that his most thoughtful projects get less attention than low-effort viral posts. He wants to be discovered by people who care about quality, not just engagement.

## Goals
1. Get his sites in front of people who actually care about the work, not just the metrics
2. Understand how his sites are perceived by an objective quality rubric
3. Use quality scores as social proof to embed on his own project pages

## Frustrations
1. Show HN is a lottery — timing and framing matter more than quality
2. Product Hunt rewards polish and launch theatrics over depth
3. SEO is a game he doesn't want to play; he'd rather write well and be found

## Behaviors
- Submits his own sites and monitors how AI scores them
- Reads the AI-generated summary critically to see if the system "gets" what he built
- Follows other builders' sites via gotta.cc category pages (Tools, Developer Blogs)
- Embeds gotta.cc quality badge on his projects (Creator tier feature)

## Job to Be Done
> "I want my site found by 100 people who love what I do — not 10,000 who scroll past it."

## Relationship to Product
Theo is a Creator tier subscriber ($10/mo). He submits his own work, monitors scores, and uses the API to pull quality data into his personal site's footer. He is a vocal advocate — if gotta.cc gives fair scores, he'll post about it. If the scores feel off or arbitrary, he'll be the first to write a critical thread about it.

## Scenarios
1. **Post-Launch Submission** — Theo ships a new tool and immediately submits it to gotta.cc. He waits for the AI scoring pipeline to run, then checks where it landed in the Tools category ranking. He screenshots the score card and includes it in his launch post.
2. **Score Audit** — His site scores low on Freshness. He updates his blog regularly but suspects the crawler isn't detecting recent posts. He files a re-review request via the Creator dashboard.
