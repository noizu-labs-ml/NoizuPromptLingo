# Persona 07: The Dad Gamer

**Name:** Marcus Williams
**Age:** 38
**Location:** Denver, CO
**Occupation:** Project manager at a construction firm
**Device:** Samsung Galaxy S23, rarely updated
**Income:** $85K/year

---

## Profile

Marcus used to play StarCraft competitively in college. He still has the strategic mind but zero hours to devote to real-time games — he has two kids (ages 6 and 9), coaches weekend soccer, and gets maybe 15 minutes of phone time before bed. He needs a game that respects his time: no daily login rewards that punish absence, no real-time battles that require uninterrupted focus, no grinding that makes him feel behind after a week off.

AI Fighter's async model is perfect for him. Design a fighter in 5 minutes, queue battles, check results tomorrow. The strategic depth scratches the same itch StarCraft did, but the time commitment is 10 minutes per day instead of 2 hours.

---

## Goals

- Enjoy strategic depth in extremely short play sessions (5-10 minutes)
- Feel competitive without needing to play daily — his fighter competes while he coaches soccer
- Understand the game well enough to play with his 9-year-old (who's interested in "the robot game dad plays")
- Progress at a reasonable pace despite playing 1/10th the time of a hardcore player

## Frustrations

- FOMO mechanics: daily login streaks, time-limited events he always misses, energy systems that cap play
- Games that require 30+ minutes of uninterrupted attention
- Steep learning curves that require YouTube research before you can be competitive
- Being matched against players who play 20x more and are 20 ranks higher

## Behaviors

- Plays 1-2 sessions per day, always under 10 minutes
- Prefers templates with modifications over building from scratch — efficient, not lazy
- Checks battle results and reads decision overlays more than he watches full replays
- Will buy the Fighter Pass if it saves time (extra training generations, analysis tools) — time is his scarcest resource
- Might introduce his kids to the game if the content is appropriate

---

## Key Scenarios

1. **First session:** Plays through onboarding during kids' bedtime. Picks a template, makes one change, runs a battle. Thinks "this is exactly what I need." Sets an alarm to check results tomorrow morning
2. **Week 2:** Has a routine: morning coffee = check last night's battle results, tweak one thing in the graph, queue 2 new battles. Total time: 7 minutes. Climbed to Silver III
3. **Month 2:** His 9-year-old is now playing on Marcus's old phone. They compare fighters at dinner. Marcus is Gold I despite playing less than almost everyone at his rank

---

## Design Implications

- Async battle resolution is this persona's killer feature — preserve and emphasize it
- Avoid punitive time-gating: no "your energy refills in 4 hours" or "login within 24 hours or lose your streak"
- Post-battle summary needs to be scannable in 30 seconds — key decisions, win/loss, suggested improvements
- Matchmaking should factor in play frequency, not just ELO — Marcus shouldn't face someone who plays 25 sessions/day at the same rank
- Family-friendliness matters: no violent imagery, no inappropriate UGC, age-appropriate content moderation
- "Quick tweak" suggestions after a loss ("swap this node and retrain") reduce the time cost of improvement
