# Zoe Nakamura — AI Newcomer

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 19–23 |
| **Role** | Undergraduate Student (Computer Science, second year) |
| **Technical Level** | Novice |
| **Industry** | Education |
| **Location** | Chicago, IL |

## Bio

Zoe is a second-year CS student who started using ChatGPT for homework help last year and is now genuinely curious about how AI actually works. She can write basic Python but has never worked with the OpenAI API directly. She knows the names Claude, GPT-4, Gemini, and Llama but not their architectural differences or capability profiles. She follows AI on TikTok and YouTube, where she's seen impressive demos, but her real-world results with AI tools are inconsistent — sometimes magical, often mediocre — and she doesn't know why. A professor mentioned "prompt engineering" in passing, which sent her down a rabbit hole that led to Meat Brains.

## Goals

1. Understand what "good" prompting actually means — not just generic tips like "be specific," but concrete techniques with examples.
2. Find a community where she can ask beginner questions without being made to feel stupid.
3. Gradually develop enough fluency to contribute her own findings, not just consume others'.

## Frustrations

1. Meat Brains content often assumes knowledge of model-specific behavior (e.g., "this works on Sonnet 3.5 but not 3.0") she doesn't have — she doesn't know which model to even use, let alone the difference between versions.
2. Jargon is everywhere and inconsistently explained: "few-shot," "chain-of-thought," "temperature," "RLHF" — she encounters these terms without definitions and feels perpetually behind.
3. She's afraid to post because she doesn't know if her observations are interesting or already well-known — impostor syndrome keeps her a lurker far longer than necessary.

## Behaviors

- Visits Meat Brains several times a week but almost exclusively reads, rarely comments.
- Clicks through to linked papers or tutorials but often abandons them when the reading level becomes inaccessible.
- Uses browser bookmarks to save posts she doesn't fully understand but wants to return to.
- Tries prompts she finds on the platform in ChatGPT free tier — which means she doesn't always see the same results as posts tested on GPT-4o or Claude API.
- Asks clarifying questions in post comment threads, usually prefaced with "sorry if this is a dumb question."

## Job to Be Done

> "When I encounter an AI capability I don't understand but find compelling, I want to find an approachable explanation and a concrete example I can try myself, so I can build a real mental model of how prompting works instead of just collecting tips that I can't explain."

## Relationship to Product

Zoe finds Meat Brains through a TikTok that screenshots a top-voted post about chain-of-thought prompting. She visits the site expecting it to be like Reddit and is initially overwhelmed by the volume of technical content. She doesn't create an account for two weeks — just reads. Eventually, she creates an account to upvote a post that finally explained few-shot prompting in a way that clicked for her, and she leaves a comment saying so. That comment gets 12 upvotes and a warm reply from the original poster. That interaction converts her from lurker to participant. Features that matter most: a "beginner" or "explainer" post tag she can filter to, post vocabulary tooltips or a glossary sidebar, and a culture of welcoming beginner questions in comments. She churns if her first real question gets a dismissive or sarcastic response.

## Scenarios

1. **First Successful Prompt** — Zoe finds a Meat Brains post explaining "role prompting" with a worked example. She copies the template, adapts it for a study guide she needs for her algorithms exam, and gets dramatically better output than her usual "summarize this for me" attempts. She screenshots the result and shares it in her class Discord, mentioning where she found the technique.

2. **Posting for the First Time** — After three months of lurking, Zoe notices that a technique tagged "GPT-4o" also works on Claude Haiku — which nobody in the comments has tried. She writes a short post documenting her experiment: the original prompt, her modification, and two side-by-side outputs. The post gets 45 upvotes and a comment from a senior community member confirming her finding is genuinely novel. She's hooked.
