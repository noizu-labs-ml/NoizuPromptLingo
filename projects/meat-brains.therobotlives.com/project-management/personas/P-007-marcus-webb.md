# Marcus Webb — Enterprise AI Lead

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 38–52 |
| **Role** | Director of AI Innovation, Fortune 500 Financial Services firm |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate (strong business context, moderate hands-on) |
| **Industry** | Financial Services / Enterprise |
| **Location** | New York, NY |

## Bio

Marcus leads a team of eight AI engineers and data scientists responsible for evaluating and deploying AI capabilities across a 20,000-person financial services company. He came from a product management background and retains strong technical literacy without being a daily coder. His world is governed by compliance requirements, change management cycles, and executive-level justification for every tool evaluation. He's genuinely excited about AI's potential but operates under constraints — data privacy, model governance, vendor risk assessments — that practitioner communities rarely discuss. He uses Meat Brains sparingly but purposefully: to understand what's technically possible before commissioning formal evaluations internally.

## Goals

1. Understand what techniques are currently state-of-the-art so he can brief his technical team on what to evaluate next quarter.
2. Find real-world evidence of enterprise-appropriate use cases (document processing, regulatory text analysis, meeting summarization) being solved with specific models and prompts.
3. Gauge the community's consensus on model reliability and vendor trajectory before recommending a platform to senior leadership.

## Frustrations

1. Most Meat Brains content is written for developers and researchers — enterprise constraints like PII handling, on-premise deployment requirements, and audit logging are almost never addressed.
2. Community enthusiasm for a technique doesn't tell him anything about whether it would survive a security review or hold up in a regulated environment.
3. Finding the business-use-case content requires a lot of filtering through developer and creative content that isn't relevant to his work.

## Behaviors

- Visits the platform 3–4 times per month, usually triggered by a specific research question his team is investigating.
- Reads more than he posts; when he does post, it's to ask whether a technique has been tested in document-heavy, structured-output contexts.
- Shares relevant Meat Brains posts in his team Slack with the note "worth evaluating" — his team then does the hands-on testing.
- Never posts anything containing company-specific information or use case details.
- Uses saved posts as a briefing list for his monthly team meeting on AI strategy.

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm evaluating whether an AI technique is worth commissioning a formal internal pilot, I want to find evidence that it works reliably for business document tasks at production scale, so I can justify the evaluation effort to my team and make a confident recommendation to leadership."

## Relationship to Product

Marcus finds Meat Brains through a LinkedIn post shared by an AI engineer he respects. He bookmarks the site, creates an account, and builds a saved post collection tagged "enterprise candidates." He visits when he has a specific question — usually around document processing, contract analysis, or meeting intelligence. He occasionally leaves high-quality comments with enterprise context that other users find valuable. Features that matter most: robust search and filtering by model and use case, ability to see a post's full comment thread with practitioner feedback, saved post collections he can share privately with his team. He churns (stops visiting) if the enterprise/business-use-case content doesn't grow proportionally with the rest of the platform.

## Scenarios

1. **Pre-Evaluation Briefing** — Marcus's team is considering piloting Claude for contract review. Before scheduling the internal evaluation, he searches Meat Brains for posts about legal document analysis and long-context comprehension. He finds three detailed posts with prompt templates and failure cases. He shares them with his two senior AI engineers as "starting points for the eval harness" — saving two weeks of prompt research.

2. **Asking a Governance Question** — Marcus posts a question asking whether anyone has successfully deployed a prompt-based classification system in an environment where all data must remain on-premise (no cloud API calls). The responses reveal two practitioners who've done exactly this with locally-hosted models, and they share their prompt adaptation strategies for smaller context windows.
