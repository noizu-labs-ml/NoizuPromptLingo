# Suki Tanaka — Hobbyist / Therapeutic Painter

**Type:** Secondary  
**Age:** 38 | **Location:** Vancouver, BC  
**Occupation:** Software engineer, paints evenings and weekends  
**Platform:** MacBook Air M2, no external tablet (trackpad + mouse)  

> "I don't need professional output. I need the *feeling* of pushing paint around after a long day of code."

## Goals

- Relaxing, tactile painting experience without setup or cleanup
- Explore color mixing intuitively — no color theory knowledge assumed
- Try media she can't afford or store physically (oils, pastels)
- Share casual work on social media, not print production

## Frustrations

- Professional tools are intimidating (too many panels, modes, settings)
- Simple painting apps (like Tayasui Sketches) lack satisfying paint physics
- Doesn't own a drawing tablet; needs the app to work with mouse/trackpad
- Tutorials assume art school vocabulary she doesn't have

## Usage Context

- 30-60 minute evening sessions, low pressure
- Wants to open the app and start painting in under 10 seconds
- Would appreciate guided experiences ("try watercolor on rough paper")
- Screen recording for sharing, not professional export

## Key Features She'd Use

- Simple brush selection (media type as primary choice)
- Canvas material presets (not manual parameter tuning)
- Satisfying paint physics (visible flow, drying, color mixing)
- Minimal UI that doesn't overwhelm

## Design Implications

- First-run experience must be zero-friction (no tutorial walls)
- Mouse/trackpad input must produce good results (pressure simulation via speed/distance)
- UI should have a "simple mode" that hides simulation parameters
- Guided prompts or starter canvases lower the blank-canvas barrier
