# Alex Chen — The Prompt Artisan

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|

| **Role** | Senior Prompt Engineer at AI Startup |

## Bio

Alex has spent the last 4 years mastering the craft of prompt engineering. They work at a rapidly growing AI startup, building and refining prompts for production systems. Their workflow involves constant iteration, prompt libraries that have grown to thousands of variations, and a reputation internally as the person to ask when an LLM isn't performing. They're active in 12 Discord servers dedicated to various AI tools, maintain a GitHub repo of curated prompts with 3k+ stars, and have lost countless hours to the friction of scattered knowledge across platforms.

## Goals

1. Build a reputation as a thought leader in prompt engineering through shared resources
2. Discover and incorporate proven patterns from other practitioners to accelerate iteration cycles
3. Reduce the time spent re-solving the same prompt engineering problems

## Frustrations

1. Knowledge is scattered across Discord, Twitter, GitHub, and Reddit — no centralized repository
2. Can't easily track version history of prompt iterations or see what changed between versions
3. Difficulty finding prompts for specific use cases without knowing the right terminology

## Behaviors

- Maintains personal prompt library in Notion (synced from scattered sources)
- Participates in 3-4 AI Discord servers daily, lurking more than speaking
- Runs A/B tests on prompt variations, documents results in personal workflow system
- Shares high-quality prompts on Twitter/X when they yield standout results

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm trying to solve a new prompt engineering challenge, I want to search a centralized repository of community-vetted prompts, so I can find proven patterns and adapt them instead of starting from scratch."

## Relationship to Product

Alex is the archetypal power user — heavy contributor, early adopter, and community tastemaker. They'll join Spaces relevant to their domains (engineering, workflow automation, code review), fork existing resources to create variants, and become a go-to source for high-quality contributions. Features that matter most: resource versioning, search/filter by domain, fork tracking, and reputation signals. They'll churn if the content quality declines or if they can't easily credit contributors.

## Scenarios

1. **Prompt Archaeology** — Alex remembers seeing a JSON-mode validation prompt 6 months ago but can't find it. They search TheRobotLives Resources, discover it was forked 14 times with documented improvements, and grab the latest stable variant.

2. **Contribution Spiral** — Alex shares their chain-of-thought verification prompt. Three other engineers fork it, add domain-specific refinements for legal, medical, and code contexts. Each fork references Alex's original, building a visible contribution graph.

3. **Space Governance** — Alex creates a "Production-Ready Prompts" Space, invites known contributors, and establishes norms for documented performance metrics. The Space becomes a trusted signal for resources ready for use in production systems.
