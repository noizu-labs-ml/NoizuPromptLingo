## Bio

Marcus quit his job at a tech giant 18 months ago to become a full-time AI educator. He runs "Prompt Weekly" (15k Substack subscribers), a YouTube channel (120k subscribers) explaining prompt engineering in plain terms, and streams weekly prompt engineering sessions. His biggest challenge is keeping his content original and valuable — the AI space moves fast, and if he's not discovering new patterns, he's irrelevant. He actively mines communities for prompts and workflows, but it's scattered and time-consuming. He needs a centralized source of high-quality, vetted resources he can build content around.

## Goals

1. Maintain content freshness by discovering cutting-edge prompt patterns early
2. Build authority by teaching from real, tested resources (not just theory)
3. Grow his audience indirectly through contributions that get recognized

## Frustrations

1. Can't easily discover the latest high-quality prompts without scrolling 50 Discords
2. Worrying about crediting sources properly when building derivative content
3. Audience demands deep expertise but maintaining it across all AI tools is exhausting

## Behaviors

- Spends 2-3 hours daily in communities hunting for prompt gems
- Maintains massive Notion database of prompts categorized by tool/use case
- Streams prompt engineering experiments with audience interaction
- Tweets prompts with explanations, monitors engagement to gauge quality

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm researching prompts for my next video/article, I want to discover vetted, trending, and well-documented resources, so I can build content from proven examples and credit contributors properly."

## Relationship to Product

Marcus is the amplifier — his content can drive user acquisition to TheRobotLives. He'll discover Resources, fork them with his annotations/explanations, share links back to the platform, and participate as a thought leader. Features that matter most: trending/discovery algorithms, clear contributor attribution, Resource embeddability (can he share a visual card linking back?), and discussion threads attached to Resources. He'll churn if the content quality isn't curated enough for his audience standards.

## Scenarios

1. **Content Research** — Marcus is planning a video on "JSON Mode Patterns." He searches TheRobotLives Resources, finds 8 JSON-related prompts sorted by recency and reputation, studies the top 3 for common patterns, and credits the original contributors in his video links.

2. **Audience Contribution** — After his video drops, 500 of Marcus's followers flood TheRobotLives, joining Spaces he mentioned and forking Resources. He becomes a de facto community ambassador and creates a "Prompt Weekly" Space where he curates resources for his newsletter subscribers.

3. **Collaborative Experiment** — Marcus invites Alex Chen (P-001) to a live stream where they experiment with a new prompt pattern. They document the iteration in real-time, capture the final prompt as a Resource, and the discussion becomes a reference for others studying prompt engineering methodology.
