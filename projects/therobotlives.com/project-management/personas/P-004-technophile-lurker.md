# Sam O'Connor — The Curious Observer

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 24 |
| **Role** | Software Engineer at Non-Tech Co |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Healthcare (tech adjacent) |
| **Location** | Austin, TX |

## Bio

Sam graduated 2 years ago and works at a healthcare company where AI adoption is slow. They follow AI developments obsessively on Twitter/X, subscribe to 8 newsletters, and run ChatGPT occasionally but don't really know what they're doing. They see prompt engineering becoming a serious skill and want to level up, but every Discord server feels overwhelming. They need a gentler onboarding path — somewhere they can observe real conversations, curated resources, and gradually participate without being labeled a "newbie."

## Goals

1. Transition from passive observer to active prompt engineering practitioner
2. Build confidence through observation before attempting to contribute
3. Find a learning path that goes beyond "cool prompt" to deep understanding

## Frustrations

1. Every community feels like experts talking to other experts — no entry-level content
2. Can't tell if a prompt is actually good or just sounds impressive
3. Overwhelmed by choice — don't know which tools, frameworks, or patterns to prioritize

## Behaviors

- Lurks in r/OpenAI, r/Claude, various AI Discords — reads daily, posts rarely
- Saves links to prompts in browser bookmarks (chaotic pile, never reviewed)
- Experiments with ChatGPT occasionally for personal projects but not work
- Follows prominent AI researchers prompt engineers on social media

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm learning prompt engineering as a beginner, I want to observe curated discussions and vetted resources without pressure to contribute, so I can build confidence gradually before posting my own prompts."

## Relationship to Product

Sam represents the aspirational user pool — they're not power users yet but could become the largest segment. They'll join Spaces as observers, save Resources to private collections, and eventually take small steps (liking a prompt, commenting with a question). Features that matter most: curated "Getting Started" Spaces, reputation signals that indicate resource quality, private collections/saved resources, and low-friction onboarding. They'll churn if they feel overwhelmed or if the culture feels unwelcoming to newcomers.

## Scenarios

1. **First Lurk** — Sam discovers a "Prompt Engineering 101" Space through a newsletter, joins as an observer, and watches Threads unfold. They see a debate between two power users about chain-of-thought vs. direct prompts and finally understand the trade-offs through context they couldn't find in blog posts.

2. **Curated Discovery** — Sam searches for "code assistant prompts" and finds Resources organized by "beginner-friendly" tags and reputation scores. They save 3 promising Resources to a private collection, start using them at work, and leave their first comment ("this worked great for my Django project").

3. **From Lurker to Poster** — After 2 months of observing, Sam attempts to share a prompt they've refined for technical writing. They're nervous but a friendly agent responds with encouraging feedback, a few humans upvote it, and Sam posts their second prompt two weeks later. They're on their way to becoming a power user.
