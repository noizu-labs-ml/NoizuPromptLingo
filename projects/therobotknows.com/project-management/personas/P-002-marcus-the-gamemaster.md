# Marcus Chen — The Veteran Game Master

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 28-35 |
| **Role** | Software engineer by day, GM by passion |
| **Technical Level** | Advanced |
| **Industry** | Tech (day job) / TTRPG community |
| **Location** | Austin, Texas |

## Bio

Marcus has been running a weekly D&D 5e homebrew campaign for two years. His world started from a published module but has diverged so far that it's entirely his own creation. He has 89 lore entries spread across Notion, a Discord channel of session recaps, and his own memory. Players regularly ask questions about the world that require him to improvise — and then remember what he improvised.

## Goals

1. Answer player questions about his world instantly and consistently, even when improvising
2. Turn session notes into permanent canon without spending hours after each game
3. Keep track of NPCs, locations, and faction politics across a 100+ session campaign

## Frustrations

1. He improvised that a merchant guild controls the docks in session 12, but forgot about it by session 40 — a player caught the contradiction
2. Notion is flexible but has no concept of a "knowledge graph" — he can't see how his entries connect
3. Pre-game prep takes 2-3 hours because he has to re-read old notes to maintain consistency

## Behaviors

- Runs 3-4 hour sessions weekly, preps for 1-2 hours before each
- Takes rough notes during sessions, cleans them up within 48 hours
- Uses Notion for persistent notes, D&D Beyond for mechanics, Discord for session recaps
- Frequently Googles "random NPC name generator" and "tavern description generator"

## Job to Be Done

> "When a player asks about the political structure of a kingdom I mentioned three sessions ago, I want to pull up a consistent answer in under 30 seconds, so I can keep the game flowing without breaking immersion."

## Relationship to Product

Marcus would discover the Knowledge Base through TTRPG subreddits or YouTube GM advice channels. The Session Companion mode is his killer feature — quick search, improvise mode, session logging. He'd pay for Creator tier for the generation features. He'd churn if the tool is too slow for live-session use or if the UI feels like it's designed for novelists, not GMs.

## Scenarios

1. **Mid-session improvisation** — A player asks about customs in a port city Marcus hasn't fleshed out. He opens Session Companion, types "port city customs," and gets a canon-consistent generated answer in 10 seconds.
2. **Post-session canon capture** — After a session where the party uncovered a conspiracy, Marcus pastes his session notes and the system extracts new entries: two NPCs, a faction, and a location. He reviews and promotes them to canon.
