# Sofia Reyes — UX Designer ("Design-to-Code Bridge")

**Type:** Secondary
**Segment:** Technical-Adjacent — Designer
**Pipeline entry:** Phase 2 (Draft)
**Likely exit:** Phase 3 (Ink) — wants to see her designs become real

---

## Demographics

- **Age:** 30
- **Role:** Senior UX/UI designer at a product studio
- **Location:** Mexico City, Mexico (remote for US clients)
- **Income:** $95K
- **Tech comfort:** Moderate — proficient in Figma, basic HTML/CSS, can't write backend code

## Quote

> "I design pixel-perfect mockups and then watch developers butcher them. I want to see my designs running, not approximated."

## Goals

1. Turn her designs into working prototypes without waiting for a developer sprint
2. Validate interaction patterns with real, clickable code — not just Figma prototypes
3. Bridge the gap between her Figma deliverables and what actually ships
4. Build side projects (a design tool, a portfolio generator) without learning backend

## Frustrations

- Figma prototypes fool stakeholders into thinking the product is further along than it is
- Developer handoff always loses the details — spacing, animation, responsive behavior
- Learning React feels like a massive detour from her core competency
- No-code tools (Webflow, Framer) are fine for marketing sites but can't handle real app logic

## Behaviors

- Maintains a personal design system in Figma with 200+ components
- Follows design Twitter/X and Dribbble for trends
- Has tried Framer, Webflow, and Bubble — none handle her use cases fully
- Evaluates tools by fidelity of output, not speed of generation

## Scenario

Sofia has a side project: a design critique tool where designers upload screens and get structured AI feedback. She opens noizu.ink, describes the concept, and the Sketch phase generates personas and stories. In Draft, she's delighted — the style guide generation produces something close to her aesthetic, and she can refine the mockups. She approves and enters Ink. The agents generate a Next.js app that actually matches her design tokens. She adjusts CSS in the exported code, deploys a beta, and starts collecting feedback from her design community.
