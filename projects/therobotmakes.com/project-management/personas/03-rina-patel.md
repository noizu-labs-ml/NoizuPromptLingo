# Rina Patel — Small Team Lead ("Alignment Tool")

**Type:** Secondary
**Segment:** Technical — Team Lead
**Pipeline entry:** Phase 1 (Sketch)
**Likely exit:** Phase 3 (Ink) — team takes over from the generated codebase

---

## Demographics

- **Age:** 36
- **Role:** Engineering lead at a 5-person startup
- **Location:** Austin, TX
- **Income:** $160K + equity
- **Tech comfort:** Advanced — codes less now, reviews PRs and sets architecture

## Quote

> "We spent three sprints arguing about what we're building. A shared spec with mockups would have saved us a month."

## Goals

1. Get the whole team looking at the same spec, mockups, and running scaffold
2. Replace Google Docs + Figma + Jira with one tool for the 0-to-1 phase
3. Ship the first version fast so the team can iterate on real code, not abstractions
4. Reduce her own bottleneck as the only person who can translate vision into tickets

## Frustrations

- PM left 6 months ago — she's doing product + engineering + hiring
- Design decisions made in Slack threads get lost and re-litigated
- Junior devs need more structure than "here's a Jira ticket, figure it out"
- Every tool in the stack solves one phase but nothing connects them

## Behaviors

- Creates detailed technical specs but nobody reads past page 2
- Uses Figma for rough mockups — functional but not polished
- Evaluates tools based on team adoption friction, not personal preference
- Willing to pay for seat-based tools if they reduce meetings

## Scenario

Rina's team is starting a new internal tool. She opens noizu.ink and walks through Sketch with her CTO in a screen share. They refine the PRD together, approve personas and stories. She moves into Draft alone, generates a style guide and wireframes, then shares the mockup link with the team. Two developers fork the generated scaffold from Ink phase and start implementing against the approved stories. Sprint planning takes 15 minutes instead of 2 hours because everyone already knows what they're building.
