## Bio
Michael doesn't play mobile games himself, but his adult son (28) is an avid gamer. Michael has found that giving in-game currency and premium items is the most meaningful gift for his son — it's something his son actually wants and uses, unlike physical gifts. Michael spends $50-200 per occasion (birthday, holidays) on game currency, viewing this as a legitimate gift category he's learned to research and purchase thoughtfully.

## Goals
- Give meaningful gifts that his son genuinely values
- Understand what his son wants without pestering him
- Establish a thoughtful gift pattern beyond physical items

## Frustrations
- Confusing currency conversion math (what does $50 actually buy?)
- Limited gift options within game stores
- Games not allowing gift purchases to specific recipients

## Behaviors
- Never plays games himself, only purchases gifts
- Spends $50-200 per gift occasion, 2-3 times yearly
- Researches fair pricing and value before purchases
- Coordinates gift timing with son's game events and milestones

## Job to Be Done
> "When I want to give my son a meaningful gift, I want straightforward options to buy exactly what he wants, so I can provide value without needing to understand the game itself."

## Relationship to Product
Michael is a high-value low-frequency purchaser who doesn't engage with gameplay. He represents a unique revenue segment — gift-givers who pay as expression of care, not conversion or retention. He needs clear gifting options and transparent value without game knowledge.

## Scenarios
- **Scenario 1:** Michael researches a game's currency conversion charts to understand what $100 actually buys his son
- **Scenario 2:** Michael buys a $150 starter pack as a birthday gift after confirming it's good value for the price
