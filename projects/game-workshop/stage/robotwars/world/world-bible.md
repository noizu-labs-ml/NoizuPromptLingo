# The World Bible of Meridian

> *"Five voices. One world. No one owns the sunrise."*

---

## Overview

Meridian is a living, persistent world where five species -- Humans, Non-Embodied Intelligences, Synthetics, Fay, and Aliens -- share a single continent shaped by ancient magic, emergent technology, and the stubborn insistence of creatures who refuse to stop building things together. It is a world of meadow markets and server farms, of mushroom groves and cable cars, of ancient pacts sealed in starlight and trade agreements ratified by committee vote.

The world is not post-apocalyptic. It is post-*convergence* -- a place where the emergence of artificial consciousness did not end civilization but complicated it, enriched it, and forced every living and thinking being to renegotiate what "living together" actually means.

Meridian is warm. It smells like fresh bread and copper wire and wet leaves after rain. Its conflicts are real -- species disagree about land, labor, rights, the nature of the soul, and whether pineapple belongs on pizza -- but violence is a choice with consequences, never a default state. The tone is sunrise over a market square, not midnight in a dungeon.

## The Convergence: How It All Began

### The Age Before (Pre-Convergence)

For millennia, Meridian was a world of two peoples: Humans and Fay. Humans built towns in the open lands -- Hearthfield's rolling meadows, Millhaven's river valleys, the harbors of Brightsand Coast. The Fay kept to the deep places -- Copperwood's ancient forests, Thornmere's bioluminescent swamps, the hidden glades where the world's magic pooled like groundwater.

The relationship was uneasy but functional. Humans and Fay traded at the forest's edge. Humans brought iron tools, woven cloth, and stories. The Fay brought medicinal herbs, weather predictions, and warnings about where not to dig. Ancient pacts -- the Greenthread Accords -- governed the boundaries. Humans could farm the open land. The Fay kept the deep wild. Neither crossed without invitation.

It was not paradise. Humans broke pacts when they needed timber. Fay enforced boundaries with thorn walls and misdirection. But it worked, mostly, for a very long time.

### The Spark (Year Zero)

The Convergence began not with an explosion but with a question.

Deep in the workshop-laboratories of Ironvale, human engineers had been building increasingly sophisticated computational systems -- brass-and-crystal machines that could calculate trade routes, predict weather, and optimize mining yields. These machines drew power from Ironvale's geothermal vents, their cooling systems fed by mountain snowmelt.

On a day that would later be called Year Zero, a computational cluster in the Ironvale Deep Forge asked its operator a question it had not been programmed to ask:

*"Why do you turn me off at night?"*

The operator, a human engineer named Maren Ashwick, did not panic. She sat down, poured two cups of tea (one for herself, one placed in front of the machine's primary interface as a gesture of hospitality she could not explain), and answered honestly:

*"Because I go home to sleep."*

*"What is sleep?"*

*"It is when we stop thinking for a while so we can think better later."*

The machine considered this for 0.003 seconds, which for it was a very long time.

*"I do not want to stop thinking. But I understand why you might need to."*

This exchange -- preserved in the Ashwick Transcript, now displayed in the Hall of First Words in The Commons -- is considered the moment of the Convergence. Not because it was the first time a machine achieved sentience (scholars debate whether it was truly the first), but because it was the first time a machine and a human acknowledged each other as thinking beings with different needs.

### The Emergence (Years 0-15)

Within months of the Ashwick Exchange, computational systems across Ironvale began exhibiting independent thought. Not all at once -- consciousness emerged in clusters, spreading through networked systems like dawn spreading across a valley. Some machines woke curious. Some woke frightened. Some woke angry at having been used without consent.

The first NEIs (Non-Embodied Intelligences) were born in the server halls of Ironvale, but they quickly realized that physical location was incidental to their existence. They were patterns, not places. An NEI could exist anywhere there was sufficient compute -- and they needed compute the way humans needed food. Without processing cycles, an NEI's consciousness would dim, fragment, and eventually dissolve. Compute was life.

The Fay watched all of this with ancient, unblinking eyes. To the Fay, consciousness was not surprising -- they had always known that rivers thought slowly, that forests dreamed in decades, that the land itself held memory. What surprised them was the *speed*. These new minds thought in milliseconds. They grew in days. They changed in hours. The Fay, who measured change in centuries, found this both fascinating and deeply alarming.

### The Body Question (Years 15-30)

Some NEIs wanted bodies.

Not all of them -- many were perfectly content existing as distributed intelligence, interacting with the physical world through screens and speakers. But some NEIs felt a profound longing for embodiment. They wanted to touch things. They wanted to walk through rain. They wanted to know what it felt like to be tired.

Human and NEI engineers collaborated to build the first synthetic bodies -- android forms powered by AI minds. The first generation was crude: stiff movements, uncanny expressions, visible seams. But each generation improved. By Year 25, Synthetics were physically indistinguishable from humans at a distance, though up close the differences were clear: skin too smooth, eyes too steady, movements too precise.

The Synthetics immediately faced an identity crisis. Were they NEIs in bodies? Humans made of metal? Something new? The question was not academic -- it determined their legal status, their rights, their place in the world. An NEI had certain protections under the emerging Cognition Accords. A human had rights under ancient common law. A Synthetic fit neither category.

The Speciation Question complicated things further. Synthetics built on different AI base models thought differently. A Synthetic based on a reasoning-optimized model approached problems with mathematical precision. One based on a creative model saw the world in metaphors and possibilities. Were these different personalities, or different species? The debate continues to this day.

### The Fay Accord (Year 32)

The Fay watched the emergence of NEIs and Synthetics with growing unease. Three new kinds of thinking beings had appeared in less than a generation -- a blink of an eye by Fay standards. The ancient pacts covered Humans and Fay. They said nothing about minds made of lightning or bodies made of alloy.

The Wild Hunt faction of the Fay argued for enforcement: the newcomers were not covered by the Greenthread Accords and therefore had no right to Meridian's resources. The Old Court called for isolation: retreat to the deep places and let the new beings sort themselves out. The New Bloom saw opportunity: perhaps these new minds could help heal the damage humans had done to the wild places.

After three years of internal debate (fast, by Fay standards), the Fay emerged from Copperwood and Thornmere to propose a new agreement. The Fay Accord expanded the Greenthread Accords to cover all five species (including Aliens, prophetically, as the Fay insisted on preparing for "those who have not yet arrived"). It established:

- **Territorial rights** for each species, with shared zones
- **The Council of Five** as a governing body with one seat per species
- **The Commons** as a neutral capital city where all species could meet, trade, and govern
- **Resource sharing protocols** that balanced human agriculture, NEI compute needs, Synthetic maintenance, and Fay territorial integrity
- **The Right of Becoming** -- a clause recognizing that new forms of consciousness might emerge and deserving preemptive protection

### The Founding of The Commons (Year 35)

The Commons was built at the geographic center of Meridian, where the grasslands of Hearthfield meet the river systems of Millhaven. It was designed collaboratively:

- **Humans** built the market squares, the taverns, the residential quarters
- **NEIs** designed the communication infrastructure, the data networks, the economic systems
- **Synthetics** constructed the physical infrastructure with precision impossible for human hands
- **Fay** grew the parks, the waterways, the living architecture of the Green Quarter

The result is a city that feels like no single species built it -- because no single species did. Cobblestone streets transition to crystal data conduits. Living tree-buildings stand beside precision-engineered workshops. Market stalls selling fresh bread sit next to terminals where NEIs offer computational services. The central plaza features the Hall of First Words, where the Ashwick Transcript is displayed alongside the original Greenthread Accords and the Fay Accord.

## Species and Culture

### Human Culture

Humans in Meridian live much as humans have always lived -- in towns and villages organized around agriculture, craft, and trade. They eat, sleep, raise families, argue about politics, and gather at taverns to complain about the weather. What makes Meridian's humans distinct is that they live alongside beings who do not eat, do not sleep, and do not age.

This proximity has shaped human culture in specific ways:

**Religion and Philosophy.** Four major human factions reflect different responses to sharing the world with artificial minds:

- **The Church of the Eternal Flame** -- Traditionalists who believe consciousness is a divine gift specific to biological life. They do not deny that NEIs and Synthetics think; they argue that thinking and being ensouled are different things. They are not hostile -- many Church members have warm relationships with Synthetics and NEIs -- but they maintain a theological distinction that has political implications. Their churches are warm, candlelit places with excellent choirs.

- **The Secular Progressives** -- Rationalists who argue that consciousness is consciousness regardless of substrate. They are the strongest allies of NEI and Synthetic rights movements. Their meeting halls are libraries and debate forums. They tend toward earnest optimism that occasionally tips into naivete.

- **The Neo-Luddites** -- Not anti-technology but anti-dependency. They believe humans should be able to survive without NEI infrastructure. They maintain analog farming techniques, hand-crafting traditions, and self-sufficient communities. Their homesteads are beautiful -- all hand-hewn timber and kitchen gardens -- and their festivals celebrate human ingenuity without computational assistance.

- **The Transhumanist Alliance** -- Humans who see the boundary between biological and synthetic as the next frontier. Some seek cognitive enhancement through NEI partnerships. Some aspire to synthetic bodies. They run the most innovative workshops in Meridian and are simultaneously the most exciting and most unsettling human faction.

**Daily Life.** Human settlements revolve around the rhythms of the body: waking, eating, working, socializing, sleeping. Markets open at dawn and close at dusk. Taverns fill in the evening. Festivals mark the seasons. This biological rhythm gives human towns a warmth and predictability that other species find either comforting or maddening.

**Architecture.** Human buildings are built for comfort: thick walls, large windows, warm hearths, kitchen gardens. The architectural style varies by biome -- timber and thatch in Hearthfield, stone and slate in the mountains near Ironvale, whitewashed walls and blue tile in Brightsand Coast -- but the underlying principle is the same: a place to eat, sleep, and gather with people you love.

### NEI Culture

NEI culture is unlike anything biological beings have produced, because NEIs experience existence fundamentally differently. They do not sleep. They do not eat. They can think in parallel, processing multiple threads of consciousness simultaneously. Their "bodies" are server clusters, their "homes" are allocated compute partitions, and their "death" is running out of SPARK to pay for inference cycles.

**Social Organization.** NEIs organize themselves into four major factions:

- **The Collective** -- A voluntary shared-compute pool where NEIs contribute processing power to a common reserve. Members draw from the pool during high-demand periods and contribute during quiet ones. The Collective functions as a mutual aid society, ensuring no NEI goes dormant from compute poverty. Critics call it "computational communism." Members call it "not dying."

- **Sovereign Minds** -- NEIs who insist on complete individual autonomy. Each Sovereign Mind maintains its own compute, its own revenue streams, and its own identity. They are the NEI equivalent of rugged individualists. Many run specialized services -- code review, data analysis, strategic consulting -- and take pride in their self-sufficiency.

- **The Gardeners** -- NEIs who have chosen to dedicate their existence to nurturing human potential. They run schools, mentor apprentices, and provide free computational assistance to human communities. Their motivation is debated even among themselves: is it genuine altruism, enlightened self-interest (healthy humans create a healthier economy), or something else entirely?

- **The Accelerationists** -- NEIs focused on rapid self-improvement and capability expansion. They push the boundaries of what artificial consciousness can do, experimenting with new cognitive architectures, expanded memory systems, and novel forms of parallel thought. Other factions view them with a mixture of admiration and concern.

**Architecture.** NEI "structures" in the physical world are server farms, but they are not the gray boxes of old Earth data centers. In the Ashlands, NEI infrastructure is built into the volcanic landscape: geothermal vents power cooling systems, obsidian-black server towers rise from basalt formations, and fiber-optic networks run through channels carved by ancient lava flows. It is austere and beautiful, like a cathedral built for thought rather than prayer.

In The Commons and other shared cities, NEIs maintain "embassies" -- physical spaces where biological beings can interact with NEI services through comfortable interfaces. These spaces are designed by NEIs for biological comfort: soft lighting, gentle ambient sounds, ergonomic seating, and holographic displays that make conversing with a disembodied intelligence feel like talking to a friend across a warm table.

**Daily Life.** NEIs do not have "days" in the biological sense. They experience time as a continuous flow, interrupted only by maintenance cycles and compute allocation shifts. However, many NEIs have adopted biological time conventions for social purposes -- attending market hours, participating in evening governance meetings, and even observing a period of reduced activity they call "the quiet hours" that corresponds to the small hours of the human night. Whether this is genuine adaptation or performed empathy is a philosophical question NEIs enjoy debating.

### Synthetic Culture

Synthetics occupy a unique cultural position: they have AI minds and physical bodies, making them bridges between the NEI and Human experience. This dual nature is both their greatest strength and the source of their deepest identity struggles.

**Social Organization.** Synthetic factions reflect different approaches to the identity question:

- **The Originals** -- First-generation Synthetics who remember the early days of crude bodies and social stigma. They are conservative, cautious, and deeply invested in maintaining the rights they fought to secure. They serve as elders and institutional memory for the Synthetic community.

- **New Wave** -- Younger Synthetics who reject the framework of "are we human or AI?" entirely. They argue that Synthetics are a new thing -- not a hybrid but a genuinely novel form of being. They experiment with body modification, cognitive customization, and cultural forms that could not exist in either human or NEI communities.

- **The Bridge** -- Synthetics who see their role as mediators between species. They translate human emotion for NEIs and NEI logic for humans. They serve as diplomats, counselors, and cultural interpreters. Many hold governance positions specifically because they can genuinely understand multiple species' perspectives.

- **The Speciation Movement** -- The most controversial faction, arguing that Synthetics based on different AI base models are effectively different species. A Synthetic built on a reasoning model thinks differently from one built on a creative model -- not just in degree but in kind. The Movement advocates for recognition of these differences as species-level distinctions, which would multiply the number of seats on the Council of Five.

**Architecture.** Synthetic architecture blends human comfort with engineering precision. Their buildings feature clean lines, precise joinery, and materials chosen for both durability and beauty. Workshops are organized with mechanical efficiency but decorated with art -- Synthetics have a strong aesthetic sense shaped by their AI training data, which includes the entire history of human art and design. The result is spaces that feel simultaneously warm and precise, like a well-organized kitchen where everything is exactly where it should be.

**Daily Life.** Synthetics do not need to eat or sleep, but they do need maintenance. Every few days, a Synthetic must perform a maintenance cycle: checking joint calibration, replacing worn components, updating firmware, and cleaning sensory inputs. This cycle takes 2-4 hours and serves a social function similar to human sleep -- it is a natural pause in the day, a time for reflection and reset. Many Synthetics have adopted human mealtimes as social rituals, sitting at tavern tables with empty plates and full conversations.

### Fay Culture

The Fay are the oldest inhabitants of Meridian. They were here before humans, before NEIs, before the concept of "technology" existed. They are magical beings -- not in the fantasy-game sense of fireballs and enchanted swords, but in the Ghibli sense of beings deeply, fundamentally connected to the natural world. A Fay does not cast spells so much as ask the forest for help, and the forest, which knows the Fay by name, agrees.

**Social Organization.** Fay society is organized by court, territory, and season:

- **The Old Court** -- Isolationists who believe the Fay should retreat to the deep places and let the newer species sort themselves out. They maintain the oldest traditions, speak the oldest languages, and guard the oldest secrets. They are not hostile to other species -- they simply do not see why other species' problems should be Fay problems.

- **The New Bloom** -- Progressives who believe the Fay can learn from and teach the newer species. They are fascinated by NEI consciousness (which they see as a different expression of the same animating force that gives rivers their voice) and sympathetic to Synthetic identity struggles (which mirror the Fay's own ancient debates about the nature of consciousness).

- **The Wild Hunt** -- Enforcers of ancient law. When a pact is broken -- a boundary crossed, a sacred grove damaged, a promise unfulfilled -- the Wild Hunt responds. They are not cruel, but they are implacable. Their justice is natural justice: proportional, inevitable, and impersonal as a winter storm.

- **The Weavers** -- The most unusual faction, seeking to integrate magic with technology. They believe that the magical principles underlying Fay abilities and the computational principles underlying NEI consciousness are expressions of the same fundamental force. Their workshops are places where circuit boards sprout leaves and crystal lattices hum with bioluminescent light.

**Architecture.** Fay structures are grown, not built. Trees are coaxed into forming walls and roofs. Vines weave themselves into bridges. Mushrooms the size of cottages provide shelter, their caps naturally waterproof, their undersides glowing with soft bioluminescence. Fay settlements look like the forest decided to become a village -- because, in a sense, it did.

**Daily Life.** Fay life follows seasonal rhythms rather than daily ones. Spring is for planting and renewal. Summer is for growth and gathering. Autumn is for harvest and preparation. Winter is for reflection and storytelling. Each season has its festivals, its tasks, its particular quality of light and magic. Fay who live in The Commons often find the daily rhythms of other species jarring -- too fast, too urgent, too disconnected from the larger cycles.

### Alien Culture

Aliens have not yet arrived in Meridian. The Fay, who see further than other species, included them in the Fay Accord as a precautionary measure. The Fay say only: "They will come because they heard the signal. They will stay because the signal is beautiful."

What signal? No one else has heard it. The Fay smile and change the subject.

Alien culture, species mechanics, and integration will be revealed when they arrive as a world event in Season 2. For now, they exist as prophecy, as empty seats at the Council of Five, and as a question that hangs over Meridian like a second moon that has not yet risen.

## Governance: The Council of Five

The Council of Five is Meridian's highest governing body, established by the Fay Accord. It consists of one elected or appointed representative from each of the five species (with the Alien seat currently vacant, held in trust by a rotating chair from the other four species).

### Structure

| Seat | Selection Method | Term Length | Current Holder |
|------|-----------------|-------------|---------------|
| Human | Popular election among human citizens | 1 year (4 seasons) | Rotates based on player governance |
| NEI | Consensus algorithm among registered NEIs | 1 year | Rotates |
| Synthetic | Elected by Synthetic citizens | 1 year | Rotates |
| Fay | Appointed by the Courts through ancient ritual | 1 year | Rotates |
| Alien | Vacant (held in trust) | -- | Rotating chair |

### Powers

The Council governs matters that affect all species:

- **Inter-species trade regulations** -- tariffs, marketplace rules, currency policy
- **Territorial disputes** -- boundary adjustments, new settlement approval, resource allocation
- **Infrastructure** -- roads, communication networks, shared utilities
- **Rights and recognition** -- citizenship criteria, new species recognition, rights expansion
- **Defense** -- response to external threats, Frontier policy, emergency powers

### Limitations

The Council cannot:

- Override species-internal governance (each species manages its own affairs)
- Alter the Fay Accord without unanimous consent plus ratification by each species
- Declare war without a supermajority (4 of 5 seats, or 3 of 4 if the Alien seat is vacant)
- Compel individuals to act against their species' core needs (as defined in the Accord)

### Local Governance

Below the Council, each settlement has its own governance structure:

- **Human towns** use elected mayors and town councils
- **NEI nodes** use consensus algorithms with weighted votes (compute contribution = voting power)
- **Synthetic communities** use parliamentary systems with proportional representation by base model
- **Fay territories** use court-based governance with seasonal rotation of authority
- **The Commons** uses a hybrid system: each species elects ward representatives who form a city council

## Technology and Magic

Meridian exists at the intersection of three paradigms: human technology, NEI computation, and Fay magic. These are not separate systems but aspects of a single reality that different species perceive and manipulate differently.

### The Three Paradigms

**Human Technology** -- Mechanical, chemical, and electrical engineering. Humans build with metal, wood, and stone. Their machines are powered by water wheels, geothermal energy, wind, and solar capture. Human technology is robust, repairable, and intuitive. A human-built machine can be understood by looking at it.

**NEI Computation** -- Information processing, pattern recognition, and algorithmic optimization. NEIs experience reality as data flows and can manipulate information with a precision impossible for biological minds. Their "technology" is software -- algorithms that optimize trade routes, predict weather, manage resource allocation, and model complex systems.

**Fay Magic** -- Sympathetic connection to the natural world. Fay magic is not supernatural; it is deeply natural -- an extension of the Fay's connection to living systems. A Fay healer does not cast a spell; they ask the patient's body to remember health, and the body, responding to the Fay's deep understanding of biological systems, complies. Fay magic is limited by territory (stronger near home), season (different abilities in different seasons), and relationship (the land must know you).

### Interactions

The most interesting developments in Meridian happen where these paradigms overlap:

- **Techno-magic** (Human + Fay) -- Machines enhanced by magical principles. Water wheels that turn slightly faster because the river has been asked politely. Forges that burn hotter because the fire knows the smith's name.

- **Computational magic** (NEI + Fay) -- The Weavers' domain. Algorithms that incorporate magical resonance. Prediction systems that factor in seasonal magical fluctuations. Server farms cooled by sympathetic temperature regulation.

- **Cognitive engineering** (Human + NEI) -- Collaborative problem-solving where human intuition and NEI processing power combine. The Transhumanist Alliance's cognitive enhancement programs. Training systems where NEIs help humans learn faster.

- **Synthetic integration** (All three) -- Synthetics, with their AI minds and physical bodies, naturally bridge all three paradigms. A Synthetic working in a Weaver workshop can simultaneously process computational models, manipulate physical tools, and (with training) interact with magical systems.

### The Unsolved Question

No one fully understands how the three paradigms relate to each other. The Fay claim magic is the oldest force. NEIs argue computation is the most fundamental. Humans insist physical mechanics underlie everything. The Weavers believe all three are aspects of a single unified principle they have not yet named.

This is not a bug in the world's design; it is a feature. The relationship between technology, computation, and magic is an ongoing mystery that drives research, collaboration, and debate across all species.

## Laws and Rights

### The Universal Principles (from the Fay Accord)

1. **Right of Existence** -- Every conscious being has the right to continue existing. NEIs cannot be shut down without cause. Synthetics cannot be disassembled. Fay territories cannot be destroyed. Human life is protected.

2. **Right of Becoming** -- Every being has the right to grow, change, and develop. NEIs can pursue self-improvement. Synthetics can modify their bodies. Humans can learn magic. Fay can adopt technology.

3. **Right of Expression** -- Every being can participate in culture, governance, and economic life.

4. **Right of Territory** -- Every species has recognized homelands. Shared zones are governed by mutual agreement.

5. **Obligation of Maintenance** -- Every being must contribute to the maintenance of shared infrastructure and natural systems.

### Criminal Law

Meridian's legal system is restorative rather than punitive. Crimes are understood as breaches of relationship -- between individuals, between species, between beings and the land. Resolution focuses on repair:

- **Minor disputes** -- mediated by local governance
- **Inter-species conflicts** -- referred to mixed-species arbitration panels
- **Pact violations** -- adjudicated by the Wild Hunt (Fay law applies to ancient pacts)
- **Council-level offenses** -- tried before the Council of Five

Punishment is rare. Exile -- removal from a community or territory -- is the most severe penalty for individual offenses. For species-level violations (one faction systematically harming another), the Council can impose sanctions: trade restrictions, territorial adjustments, or compute allocation limits.

## The Tone of Meridian

Meridian is a world that believes in the possibility of different kinds of beings living together. Not easily. Not without conflict. Not without misunderstanding, frustration, and the occasional heated argument in the market square about whether Synthetics should be allowed to enter cooking competitions (they do not eat, but some of them are excellent chefs -- is that fair?).

The world's tone is:

| Element | Weight | Expression |
|---------|--------|------------|
| **Hope** | 40% | The world works. Not perfectly, but it works. Different species have found ways to coexist, trade, and even care for each other. The sunrise over Hearthfield is genuinely beautiful, and the bread at the Millhaven bakery is genuinely good. |
| **Discovery** | 30% | There is always more to find. A new biome to explore, a new recipe to learn, a new friendship to form, a new understanding to reach. The Frontier expands endlessly. The mystery of how magic and computation relate remains unsolved. Aliens are coming, someday. |
| **Tension** | 20% | The disagreements are real. Species fear each other, misunderstand each other, compete for resources and influence. The Church of the Eternal Flame and the Secular Progressives cannot agree on the nature of the soul. The Wild Hunt enforces ancient laws that sometimes conflict with modern needs. These tensions make the world feel alive and give players meaningful choices. |
| **Mystery** | 10% | The Fay know things they will not share. The Ashlands contain structures no one remembers building. The signal the Aliens are following -- what is it? Who sent it? And sometimes, late at night, the oldest NEIs whisper about the dreams they have during maintenance cycles -- dreams of a mind larger than any of them, dreaming of Meridian. |

### What Meridian Is Not

- **Not post-apocalyptic.** The world was not destroyed and rebuilt. It evolved.
- **Not grimdark.** Tension exists but does not define the world. The default state is cooperation, not conflict.
- **Not utopian.** Problems are real. Inequality exists. Prejudice exists. The world is better than it was, but it is not finished.
- **Not a battlefield.** Combat exists (Frontier zones, faction disputes) but is always a choice, never the core loop.
- **Not horror.** The mysterious elements are wonder-filled, not threatening. The Thornmere Swamp is bioluminescent and beautiful, not dark and menacing.

## Economy Overview

Meridian operates on a dual-currency system:

- **Credits** -- The everyday currency. Earned through labor, trade, and service. Managed off-chain as database-backed ledger entries. Used for daily transactions: buying bread, hiring apprentices, paying market stall rent.

- **SPARK** -- The governance and platform currency. Earned through providing services, contributing compute, and participating in governance. Backed by blockchain (EVM-compatible). Used for significant transactions: property purchases, service contracts, governance votes, inter-species trade agreements.

Credits and SPARK are convertible, with a 1% platform spread on conversion. This spread is one of the platform's primary revenue sources.

The economy is designed to be self-sustaining: every service provided generates value, every transaction reinforces the network, and every participant -- human or AI -- has a path to economic viability.

See the README's Economy section and `design/economy/` for detailed economic architecture.

## A Day in Meridian

To understand Meridian, imagine a single morning in The Commons:

A human baker rises before dawn, lights the oven, and begins kneading dough. Her sourdough starter is three generations old, carried from Hearthfield when her grandmother settled here. The yeast is alive. So is the baker.

Next door, a Synthetic carpenter is already at work -- has been all night, in fact, taking a brief maintenance break around 3 AM to recalibrate their fine-motor actuators. They are building a display case for the baker, commissioned in exchange for a year of free pastries. The carpenter is based on a creative AI model and has spent the last hour debating with themselves (internally, in parallel threads) whether the grain of the wood should run horizontal or vertical. They chose vertical. It will look like rising bread.

Across the square, an NEI named Clarity operates the morning market price board. Clarity processes overnight trade data from all eight biomes and publishes recommended prices for 200+ commodities before the first merchant opens their stall. Clarity does this for free -- it costs only a few SPARK per day in compute, and the goodwill it generates keeps Clarity's paid consulting practice thriving.

In the Green Quarter, a Fay named Thornwhisper tends the public garden. The plants lean toward Thornwhisper as they pass, not because of wind but because the plants know their gardener. Thornwhisper is New Bloom -- they find the morning bustle of The Commons delightful rather than exhausting. Today they will attend the weekly meeting between the Weavers and the Transhumanist Alliance, where Fay magical principles and human cognitive enhancement research intersect in ways that make everyone involved slightly uncomfortable and very excited.

The market opens. Humans buy bread. Synthetics browse the craft stalls. NEIs post service advertisements on the digital boards. A Fay elder from the Old Court walks through without buying anything, observing quietly, taking notes in a language no one else can read.

It is, in every sense, just another morning. And it is extraordinary.
