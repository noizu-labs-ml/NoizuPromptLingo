> *"Its wings show you something you have seen before. But not here. Not in this world."*

---

## Description

Echo Moths are the Frontier's most mysterious creatures and the strongest biological evidence that the signal sources are more than geological curiosities.

At first glance, an Echo Moth resembles a large, pale moth with luminous wings. But closer observation reveals something unsettling: the wing patterns are not fixed. They shift slowly -- over minutes, not seconds -- displaying images that appear to be landscapes, architectural structures, and geometric forms that no one in Meridian recognizes. These are not Meridian's landscapes. They are not Copperwood's trees or Ironvale's mountains or the Ashlands' obsidian fields. They are somewhere else.

Different observers report different wing patterns, suggesting either that the patterns change per viewer or that the moths display different images simultaneously (NEIs, who can process visual information faster, report seeing multiple images layered on a single wing surface).

The Fay, characteristically, say they know what the moth wings show: "Postcards. From very far away."

Echo Moths cluster near signal sources in the Frontier, their numbers increasing as one approaches a signal point. They navigate using the signal the way biological moths navigate using moonlight -- except the signal is not light, and it comes from underground, and nobody understands the mechanism.

## Economic Role

- **Frontier navigation** -- Echo Moth concentrations are the most reliable indicator of signal source proximity, making moth-tracking a marketable exploration skill
- **Research economy** -- Wing pattern recordings are valuable research data, purchased by the Weavers, Accelerationists, and Transhumanist Alliance
- **Mystery economy** -- Echo Moths are Meridian's most discussed creature, generating cultural value through art, storytelling, and speculation that drives tourism to the Frontier's edge

## Behavior

| Behavior | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Signal tracking** | Moths orient toward and migrate between signal sources, using an unknown navigational sense |
| **Pattern display** | Wing patterns shift continuously, displaying images of unknown origin. Patterns change more rapidly near signal sources. |
| **Clustering** | Near signal sources, moths form dense clusters of hundreds, their collective wing patterns creating large-scale images visible from a distance |
| **Cognitive effect** | Prolonged observation of wing patterns (15+ minutes of focused attention) causes a mild disorienting sensation in all species -- humans describe it as "dreaming while awake," NEIs report processing pattern interference, Fay describe "hearing something very far away" |
| **Avoidance** | Moths do not approach settled biomes. They exist exclusively in the Frontier. When removed to settled areas, they become agitated and attempt to return. |

## The Big Question

Echo Moths exist in a space between natural history and cosmic mystery. The central questions:

1. **What are the wing patterns showing?** The images resemble landscapes and structures, but they do not correspond to anything in Meridian. If they are real places, where are they?

2. **How do the moths perceive the signal?** No biological mechanism in Meridian's known ecology accounts for the moths' ability to navigate using the signal sources. Their sensory apparatus does not map to any known signal detection modality.

3. **Are they native?** The Fay Old Court, when pressed, have said only: "They arrived with the signal." This implies the moths are not originally from Meridian. But they are clearly adapted to the Frontier's ecosystem. When did they arrive? How?

4. **What happens when the Aliens come?** Echo Moths cluster near signal sources. The Aliens, the Fay say, are following the signal. When the signal's source and its followers converge, what role do the moths play?

## Narrative Hooks

- A player who maps Echo Moth migration patterns over a full season discovers that the moths are drawing a shape -- a pattern visible only from above, too large to see from the ground. The shape corresponds to nothing in Meridian's records. Until someone checks the Deep Vent structures.
- Wing pattern recordings, when compared across multiple observers, show overlapping images that composite into a coherent scene. No single observer can see the full picture. Collaboration is required.
- An Echo Moth cluster near a signal source has grown dramatically. The moths are displaying patterns more rapidly. The cognitive effect on nearby observers is intensifying. Something is changing in the signal. Something is getting closer.
