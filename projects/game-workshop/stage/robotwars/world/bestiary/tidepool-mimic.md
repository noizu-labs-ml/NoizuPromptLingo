# Tidepool Mimic

> *"It looks like a rock. Then it looks like a shell. Then it looks like a very old coin. Then you realize it is looking at you."*

## Description

Tidepool Mimics are small, rubbery cephalopods with an extraordinary ability: they can alter their surface texture, color, and shape to resemble objects in their environment. A Mimic resting on a barnacle-covered rock becomes indistinguishable from the surrounding barnacles. One sitting on a sandy bed looks like a pebble. And -- this is the part that delights and frustrates beachcombers -- a Mimic that has seen a coin, a button, a shell, or any small interesting object can reproduce its appearance with startling accuracy.

This camouflage serves two purposes. First, predator avoidance. Second, prey attraction -- small creatures investigating what appears to be a shiny object find a Mimic instead. But the Mimics seem to enjoy their talent beyond its survival utility. They will sometimes mimic objects they have no practical reason to copy: a carved figurine dropped by a tourist, a data crystal left by an NEI researcher, a flower petal blown in by the wind.

Sea Fay, who maintain the Tidal Gardens where Mimics are most abundant, claim that Mimics are "the ocean playing dress-up." Human fishers consider them pests (they mimic fishing lures). Synthetics find them philosophically interesting ("a being defined by what it pretends to be -- is that so different from us?").

## Drops and Resources

| Resource | Method | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|--------|-----|
| **Mimic ink** | Harvested from ink deposits in tidepool crevices (Mimics spray ink when startled; it settles) | Uncommon | Ink for writing (color-shifting), dye (unique iridescent properties), alchemical ingredient |
| **Shed mimic skin** | Found in tidal pools after molting | Common | Flexible crafting material with residual color-shifting properties |
| **Salvage indicator** | Mimics congregate near underwater objects of interest | Free | Beachcombers follow Mimic clusters to find salvage, as Mimics are attracted to unusual objects |

---

## Economic Role

- **Salvage economy** -- Mimic clusters near shoreline mark locations where interesting objects have settled, making them involuntary guides for the Brightsand Coast salvage industry
- **Ink production** -- Mimic ink is a specialty crafting material prized for its color-shifting properties in writing and textile dyeing
- **Tourism** -- Mimic-spotting is a popular coastal activity. Guided tours teach visitors to identify Mimics among the rocks.
- **Fishing nuisance** -- Mimics that learn to copy fishing lures create minor economic friction for commercial fishers (and entertainment for everyone else)

## Behavior

| Behavior | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Camouflage** | Default state. Mimics match their immediate environment in texture, color, and shape. |
| **Object mimicry** | After observing a novel object, a Mimic can reproduce its appearance for hours to days |
| **Curiosity** | Mimics investigate new objects in their environment, approaching slowly and touching with tentacles |
| **Ink defense** | When startled, Mimics release a cloud of iridescent ink that obscures vision for 5-10 seconds |
| **Display** | During mating season, Mimics perform rapid shape-shifting displays, cycling through dozens of forms in seconds. Spectacular to witness. |

## Narrative Hooks

- A Mimic in the Salvage Yards has been copying an artifact that no one recognizes -- an object from before the Convergence, or possibly from before human settlement. What did the Mimic find?
- The Sea Fay use Mimic behavior as a coastal health indicator. When Mimics stop mimicking and go still, something is wrong with the water.
- A comedic quest: a Mimic has learned to copy SPARK tokens. The copies are convincing but worthless. A merchant in Port Brightsand has been fooled three times and wants the player to relocate the offending Mimic.
