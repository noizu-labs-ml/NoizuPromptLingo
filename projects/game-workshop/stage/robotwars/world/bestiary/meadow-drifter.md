## Description

Meadow Drifters are the first creatures most players see in Meridian, and they set the tone for everything that follows: this is a world that is beautiful, alive, and not trying to kill you.

Drifters resemble luminous dandelion seeds -- spherical bodies of soft golden light surrounded by trailing filaments that catch the breeze. They drift in loose swarms across Hearthfield's meadows, rising and falling with air currents, occasionally clustering around wildflower patches. Each Drifter produces a faint, warm glow that intensifies at dawn and dusk, creating meadow-wide light shows during golden hour.

Up close, a Drifter's body is translucent -- a delicate membrane filled with a bioluminescent fluid that shifts between gold, amber, and soft orange. The filaments are not tentacles; they are sensory structures that detect pollen, moisture, and (the Fay claim) emotional states.

Drifters are not intelligent in any way that the Council of Five recognizes. But the Fay maintain that Drifters are "the meadow expressing contentment" -- a manifestation of ecological health. Where Drifters are abundant, the soil is fertile, the water is clean, and the ecosystem is balanced. Where they are absent, something is wrong.

## Behavior

| Behavior | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Drifting** | Default state. Drifters float on air currents 0.5-2 meters above ground, following pollen sources. |
| **Clustering** | When multiple Drifters converge on a rich pollen source, they form slowly rotating clusters of 10-50 individuals. These clusters are visually spectacular. |
| **Signaling** | Drifters' glow intensity increases when the soil beneath them is particularly healthy. Farmers use Drifter glow as a soil quality indicator. |
| **Rain response** | Before rain, Drifters descend to ground level and tuck their filaments close to their bodies. This is a reliable weather predictor (15-30 minute advance notice). |
| **Player following** | Drifters occasionally follow players for short distances, attracted by movement or (the Fay say) curiosity. This is charming and mechanically meaningless. |

## Drops and Resources

Drifters are not harvested. They are too small, too delicate, and too beloved. However, they leave behind:

| Resource | Method | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|--------|-----|
| **Drifter down** | Shed naturally; collect from meadow grasses after dawn | Common | Soft crafting material for pillows, insulation, decorative items |
| **Drifter pollen** | Accumulates on flowers that Drifters visit frequently | Common | Cooking ingredient (adds golden color and mild sweetness), mild potion base |
| **Drifter glow** | Cannot be collected -- observed only | N/A | Soil quality indicator for farming optimization |

---

## Economic Role

Drifters play an indirect but important role in Meridian's economy:

- **Agricultural indicator** -- Farmers who learn to read Drifter behavior can optimize planting, irrigation, and harvest timing, improving crop yields
- **Tourism** -- Hearthfield's golden-hour Drifter displays are a draw for visitors, supporting the local hospitality economy
- **Crafting material** -- Drifter down is a starting-tier crafting material, introducing new players to the gathering-crafting loop
- **Cultural symbol** -- Drifter imagery is popular on goods, signage, and clothing, making them a minor but consistent motif in Meridian's visual economy

## Ecological Relationships

| Partner | Relationship |
|---------|-------------|
| **Wildflowers** | Mutualistic pollination -- Drifters spread pollen; flowers provide food |
| **Hearthstone Beetles** | Drifters and Beetles occupy the same habitat without competition. Beetles in soil, Drifters in air. |
| **Fay** | The Fay consider Drifters a sign of the land's health and grow protective of meadows where Drifters congregate |
| **Coppershell Tortoises** | Drifters sometimes rest on Tortoise shells. The Tortoises do not seem to mind. |

## Narrative Hooks

- Old Hawthorn's hidden garden has an unusually large and bright Drifter population. Hawthorn says they have been tending the garden's Drifter colony for decades. The Drifters respond to Hawthorn's presence by glowing brighter.
- A quest involving Drifter absence: a previously healthy meadow suddenly has no Drifters. What changed? (Soil contamination from a nearby construction project, leading to a conflict between development and ecology.)
- Drifters in the Frontier behave differently -- their glow is blue-white instead of golden, and they cluster around the signal sources. What does this mean?
