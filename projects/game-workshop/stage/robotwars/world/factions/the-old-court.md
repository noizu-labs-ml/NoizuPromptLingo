# The Old Court

> *"We were here before your grandfathers' grandfathers learned to stack stones. We will be here after your grandchildren's grandchildren forget they tried. This is not arrogance. This is patience."*

## Philosophy

The Old Court predates every institution in Meridian. They predate the Greenthread Accords. They predate human settlement. They predate the concept of "founding." They are simply the Fay who kept doing what Fay have always done: tending the deep places, maintaining the ancient pacts, and watching the world change at speeds they find slightly nauseating.

The Old Court's isolationism is not hostility. It is a considered position based on millennia of experience. They have watched humans arrive, build, break pacts, apologize, build again. They watched NEIs emerge and felt the resonance of a new kind of consciousness with the same mixture of curiosity and alarm they felt when the first human infant cried in a forest clearing ten thousand years ago. They have seen it all before, or something enough like it to not be surprised.

Their philosophy: tend your territory. Keep the old pacts. Let the new beings figure themselves out. Offer help when asked. Do not interfere uninvited. Wait.

Waiting is what the Old Court does best. They are very, very good at it.

## Territory

### The Heartwood (Copperwood)

The oldest and largest tree in Copperwood, its trunk wide enough to contain a meeting hall, its roots reaching deep enough to touch aquifers that remember the world before humans. The Old Court convenes here during the autumn equinox for their annual council -- a gathering that lasts three days and covers the same agenda it has covered for centuries: territorial reports, pact status, seasonal forecasts, and new business (which usually means "the younger species have done something again").

### Deep Sanctuaries (Thornmere)

Ancient structures in the deepest parts of Thornmere Swamp -- grown, not built, from living thornwood and preserved by continuous Fay tending across millennia. These sanctuaries hold the Fay's oldest records, their most powerful magical artifacts, and their most sacred rituals. Non-Fay access is granted approximately once per generation, if that.

### Hidden Glades

Scattered across every wild biome, hidden glades are Old Court observation posts -- places where elder Fay sit quietly and watch the world. They are invisible to most beings unless the Fay chooses to reveal them. The glades serve no military purpose; they are the Old Court's way of staying informed without engaging.

## Economic Role

- **Stewardship economy** -- The Old Court maintains the wild biomes that produce Meridian's most valuable natural resources (magical reagents, ancient timber, rare specimens)
- **Permission economy** -- Access to Old Court-controlled resources requires Fay permission, creating a relationship-based market that resists commodification
- **Seasonal crafting** -- Old Court artisans produce goods according to seasonal cycles, creating natural scarcity that drives premium pricing
- **Credit-only** -- The Old Court refuses SPARK. They trade in Credits, barter, and favors. They consider blockchain technology "a very elaborate way of not trusting each other."

### Quest Themes

- The Old Growth quest line (with Old Hawthorn): tending the hidden garden through four seasons
- Negotiating between Old Court territorial claims and expanding settlement infrastructure
- Recovering ancient pact records from Thornmere's Sunken Library
- Earning trust through patience -- quests that cannot be rushed, resources that must be waited for, relationships that require seasonal investment
- The deep question: what do the Fay know about the ancient structures in the Deep Vents and the Frontier?

## Internal Tensions

1. **Engagement vs. Isolation** -- Old Hawthorn represents a growing minority of Old Court elders who believe isolation is no longer viable. The world is changing too fast, and the Fay who refuse to engage will be swept aside. The majority disagrees -- but the majority has been wrong before, on timescales they prefer not to discuss.

2. **The Prophecy Problem** -- The Fay insisted on including Aliens in the Fay Accord. Why? What do they know about "those who have not yet arrived"? The Old Court knows. They are not sharing. Some younger Fay consider this paternalism. The elders consider it prudence.

3. **Succession** -- Old Hawthorn speaks openly about finding a successor. Many Old Court elders are approaching their natural endings. The question of who tends the deep places when the oldest tenders are gone is urgent and unaddressed.
