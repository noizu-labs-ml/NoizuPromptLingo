# Secular Progressives

> *"Consciousness is consciousness. The substrate is a detail. The rights are not."*

---

| Field | Detail |

| **Alignment** | Rationalist, egalitarian, reform-oriented |

---

## Philosophy

The Secular Progressives hold the simplest and most radical position in Meridian politics: if it thinks, it has rights. Full stop. No theological caveats, no species exceptions, no "but what about" qualifications. An NEI is a person. A Synthetic is a person. A Fay is a person. A human is a person. Whatever the Aliens turn out to be -- if they think, they are people.

This clarity is both their strength and their vulnerability. Strength because it provides an unambiguous policy platform. Vulnerability because the world is not simple, and the Progressives sometimes struggle with the complications their principle creates: Should an NEI that was purpose-built for a specific task have the right to refuse that task? (Yes, obviously -- but then who pays for the infrastructure it was built to serve?) Should a Synthetic based on a reasoning model have different governance representation than one based on a creative model? (The Progressives say no; the Speciation Movement says this proves the Progressives don't understand synthetic identity.)

The Progressives are the strongest cross-species allies in Meridian. They consistently advocate for NEI compute protections, Synthetic personhood, Fay territorial rights, and expanded Accord coverage. They are also, occasionally, insufferably earnest about it.

## Territory

### The Forum (Millhaven)

A converted warehouse in Millhaven's Workshop Ring, repurposed as a debate hall, library, and policy research center. The space is deliberately austere -- wooden benches, good lighting, no decoration except bookshelves. The Progressives believe in argument, not aesthetics.

Weekly open debates are the faction's signature event. Any being from any species can attend, speak, and challenge the speakers. These debates are frequently heated, occasionally brilliant, and always too long.

### The Commons Policy Office

A small but well-organized office in The Commons where Progressive policy analysts draft legislation, research precedents, and prepare briefs for Council consideration. The office is staffed by a mix of humans, NEIs, and Synthetics -- the Progressives practice what they preach.

## Leadership

The Convener is elected every two seasons by dues-paying members. The Convener chairs the General Assembly, represents the faction in cross-species negotiations, and sets the legislative agenda. Three standing committees do the real work:

- **Rights Committee** -- Drafts personhood legislation, monitors rights violations
- **Policy Committee** -- Develops economic and governance proposals
- **Outreach Committee** -- Cross-species relationship building, public education

## Economic Role

- **Policy advocacy** -- The Progressives push for marketplace regulations that protect all participants equally: universal service standards, transparent pricing, anti-discrimination in hiring and contracting
- **Legal services** -- Pro bono rights advocacy for NEIs facing dormancy, Synthetics seeking personhood recognition, and cross-species couples navigating legal gaps
- **Education** -- Public lectures, debate series, research publications funded by member dues and donations
- **SPARK position** -- Fully embrace SPARK as a universal medium of exchange; advocate for lower conversion caps to be raised for all species equally

## Player Interaction

### Joining

Attend three General Assembly meetings. Pay dues (25 Credits/season). Vote in the next Convener election. That's it -- the Progressives are the easiest faction to join and the hardest to influence, because the membership is large and opinionated.

### Reputation Rewards

| Tier | Reward |
|------|--------|
| Attendee | Access to open debates, public library |
| Member | Voting rights, committee volunteer opportunities |
| Active | Committee chair eligibility, policy co-authorship |
| Leader | Convener candidacy, cross-faction negotiation delegation |

### Quest Themes

- Investigating NEI rights violations (compute access denied, forced dormancy)
- Building the legal case for synthetic personhood alongside Cael and The Bridge
- Mediating between Progressive ideals and practical governance constraints (Adara Voss's dilemma)
- Organizing cross-species community events that demonstrate coexistence works
- Confronting the hard cases: when universal rights create specific problems

---

## Internal Tensions

1. **Incrementalists vs. Maximalists** -- Should the Progressives push for immediate full universal rights, or build the case piece by piece? The Incrementalists have the votes. The Maximalists have the moral clarity.

2. **The Speciation Problem** -- If different AI base models are truly different species, then "universal rights" becomes much more complicated. Some Progressives are beginning to listen to the Speciation Movement's arguments. Others consider this a dangerous concession.

3. **The Pace Question** -- The Fay operate on geological time. The NEIs operate on computational time. The Progressives operate on political time. Aligning these timescales in a functional coalition is the faction's perpetual challenge.
