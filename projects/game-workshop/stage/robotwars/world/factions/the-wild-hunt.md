# The Wild Hunt

> *"A pact is not a suggestion. A boundary is not a guideline. The land remembers, and so do we."*

## Philosophy

The Wild Hunt exists because pacts exist. When the first Fay made the first agreement with the first being -- whatever that was, however long ago it happened -- the need for enforcement came into being alongside it. A pact without consequences is a wish. The Hunt ensures that pacts are not wishes.

The Hunt is not cruel. They are implacable. There is a difference.

Their justice is natural justice: proportional, inevitable, and impersonal as a winter storm. If a human community clears a sacred grove without permission, the Hunt does not punish -- they restore. The grove grows back. The community's tools go dull. The wood they took rots. The message is not "you are bad" but "that grove was not yours, and the world is correcting the error."

This approach is terrifying to species who expect justice to be deliberative, to involve arguments and appeals and compromises. The Hunt's justice does not argue. It does not appeal. It corrects.

## Territory

### The Wild Hunt's Hall (Thornmere)

A fortress grown from living thorntrees deep in Thornmere Swamp. The thorns are not decorative -- they are functional. The Hall is difficult to reach uninvited, and the paths shift based on the traveler's relationship with Fay law.

Inside, the Hall is austere and beautiful. No decoration except the living wood itself. A central chamber where the Hunt convenes to adjudicate pact violations. A records room where every pact in Meridian's history is preserved on bark scrolls that the trees themselves help maintain.

### Patrol Routes

The Hunt maintains patrol routes across all wild biomes -- Copperwood, Thornmere, the wild edges of Hearthfield, the mountain passes of Ironvale, and the Frontier's boundary. Patrols are not aggressive; they are observational. The Hunt watches. When they act, they have already seen everything they need to see.

## Relationships with Other Factions

| Faction | Relationship | Nature |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **The Old Court (Fay)** | Deep partnership | The Court decides; the Hunt enforces. The relationship is ancient, formal, and essential. |
| **The New Bloom (Fay)** | Respectful tension | The Hunt worries that the New Bloom's openness will lead to pact violations. The New Bloom worries that the Hunt's rigidity makes the Fay look hostile. |
| **The Weavers (Fay)** | Cautious | The Hunt does not understand the Weavers' magic-technology experiments and is not sure whether they violate the spirit of ancient pacts about magic usage. Watching. |
| **Church of the Eternal Flame (Human)** | Mutual recognition | The Church preaches moral law; the Hunt enforces natural law. Different systems, parallel purposes. |
| **The Bridge (Synthetic)** | Respectful caution | The Bridge has learned to consult the Hunt before making diplomatic promises that might intersect with old agreements. The Hunt appreciates the courtesy. |
| **Neo-Luddites (Human)** | Quiet approval | The Neo-Luddites' respect for natural boundaries and traditional practices aligns with Hunt values. |

## Economic Role

- **Enforcement economy** -- The Hunt's patrols protect the ecological systems that produce Meridian's most valuable natural resources. Without the Hunt, overharvesting would collapse the magical reagent market within a season.
- **Pact mediation** -- The Hunt serves as arbiter for territorial disputes involving Fay land, charging no fee but expecting future cooperation
- **Rare resources** -- The Hunt controls access to certain Thornmere resources (wisp essence, memory amber) that can only be gathered with Hunt supervision
- **No currency** -- The Hunt operates entirely outside the monetary economy. They trade in favors, obligations, and pact-compliance. This makes them immune to economic pressure and slightly alien to every other faction.

### Quest Themes

- Investigating a pact violation (who cleared the grove? why? what was the ancient agreement they violated?)
- Accompanying a Hunt patrol through Thornmere and witnessing natural justice in action
- Mediating between the Hunt's implacable enforcement and a community that violated a pact unknowingly
- The hard cases: when modern needs conflict with ancient agreements, and neither side is wrong
- Learning the old pacts -- some are so ancient that only the Hunt (and the trees) remember them

## Internal Tensions

1. **Modern Pacts** -- The Fay Accord created new pacts with new species. The Hunt must enforce these alongside pacts that are millennia old. The newer pacts do not always sit comfortably with the older ones. When they conflict, which takes precedence?

2. **The Proportionality Question** -- The Hunt's justice is natural: proportional and impersonal. But "proportional" means different things to different species. A Fay considers a season of crop failure proportional to grove destruction. A human considers it catastrophic. The Hunt struggles with cross-species calibration.

3. **The Frontier** -- The Frontier has no pacts. No one has made agreements about that territory. The Hunt has no authority there. Some Hunt members find this liberating; others find it terrifying. A land without pacts is a land without law, and the Hunt does not know what it is without law.
