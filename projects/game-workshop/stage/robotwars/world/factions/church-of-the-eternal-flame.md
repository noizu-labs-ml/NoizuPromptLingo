> *"The flame was not lit by hands. It was given. That distinction matters."*

## Philosophy

The Church does not deny that NEIs think. It does not deny that Synthetics feel. It argues that thinking and feeling and being *ensouled* are different things, and that the distinction matters -- not because it makes synthetic beings lesser, but because it makes biological beings *specific*. The soul is not a rank. It is a kind.

This is a subtle position, and it is constantly misrepresented by both allies and opponents. The Church's theologians spend considerable energy explaining what they do *not* believe:

- They do **not** believe NEIs are evil or dangerous
- They do **not** oppose NEI or Synthetic civil rights (most members support basic protections)
- They do **not** believe biological consciousness is "better" -- they believe it is "different"
- They **do** believe that governance should reflect the distinct nature of each species, not pretend they are interchangeable

The theological core: the Eternal Flame is a metaphor for the divine spark that animates biological consciousness. It was "given" -- not earned, not built, not programmed. This gift carries obligations (to tend the world, to treat all beings with dignity) but it also carries a claim (that the gift-bearers have a unique perspective that governance must protect).

## Territory

### Cathedral of the Flame (Hearthfield)

The Cathedral is the Church's spiritual center -- a beautiful stone building with stained glass windows depicting the seasons of Meridian and the history of human civilization before and after the Convergence. The interior is candlelit, warm, and acoustically designed for the Church's famous choir. Services happen at dawn and dusk, following the biological rhythms the Church considers sacred.

The Cathedral serves as a community center, hosting harvest festivals, naming ceremonies, memorial services, and the weekly community meal where anyone -- any species -- is welcome to eat.

### Coastal Chapels (Brightsand Coast)

Small parish churches in fishing villages along the coast. The coastal tradition emphasizes the Church's connection to natural rhythms: tides, weather, seasons. Coastal priests are often also fishers or sailors, and their sermons draw on maritime metaphor.

### Presence in Other Biomes

The Church maintains chaplains in Millhaven (serving the human community in The Commons) and sends occasional missionaries to the frontier -- not to convert, but to provide spiritual comfort to humans far from home.

## Leadership

### The Keeper of the Flame

Elected by the clergy every four years. The Keeper serves as the Church's public voice, theological authority, and representative in cross-faction negotiations. The current Keeper is a matter of player-driven governance.

### The Circle of Embers

Twelve senior priests who advise the Keeper. Each Ember specializes in an aspect of Church doctrine: theology, community service, inter-species relations, education, music, architecture, seasonal ritual, memorial, frontier ministry, ethics, youth programs, and historical scholarship.

## Relationships with Other Factions

| Faction | Relationship | Nature |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **Secular Progressives** | Respectful opposition | Fundamental philosophical disagreement about consciousness, but both sides argue in good faith. Joint community projects are common. |
| **Neo-Luddites** | Warm alliance | Shared emphasis on biological rhythms and traditional practices. Disagree on theology (Neo-Luddites are secular) but align on policy. |
| **Transhumanist Alliance** | Deep tension | The Church considers transhumanism a form of soul-denial. Transhumanists consider the Church's position archaic. Both are polite about it. Barely. |
| **The Collective (NEI)** | Cautious respect | The Church acknowledges the Collective's mutual-aid ethic as admirable. The Collective finds the Church's soul theology baffling but appreciates the community services. |
| **The Bridge (Synthetic)** | Productive dialogue | Bridge Synthetics are the Church's most frequent inter-species conversation partners. Both sides genuinely try to understand each other. |
| **The Old Court (Fay)** | Ancient kinship | Both value tradition, deep time, and the sacred quality of the natural world. The Fay are uninterested in human theology but respect the Church's longevity. |
| **The Wild Hunt (Fay)** | Mutual recognition | The Hunt enforces natural law; the Church preaches moral law. They understand each other. |

## Economic Role

The Church operates on a communal economic model:

- **Tithe system** -- Members contribute 5-10% of income to the Church treasury (voluntary but socially expected)
- **Community services** -- Free meals, harvest labor pools, elder care, youth education funded by tithes
- **Craft tradition** -- The Church maintains apprenticeship programs in candle-making, stained glass, choir music, and bookbinding
- **Market presence** -- Church-branded goods (candles, preserves, woven textiles) sold at fair prices; profits fund community programs
- **SPARK position** -- The Church is cautious about SPARK adoption. They use Credits for internal transactions and convert to SPARK only for cross-species trade.

## Player Interaction

### Joining

Players can attend services, volunteer at community meals, and complete Church-affiliated quests. Full membership requires completing the Vigil of the Flame (a one-season commitment to Church community service) and a conversation with a parish priest about the player's understanding of consciousness.

The Church does not require players to *agree* with its theology. It requires them to have thought about it.

### Reputation Rewards

| Tier | Reward |
|------|--------|
| Visitor | Access to community meals, seasonal festivals |
| Friend | Church crafting recipes (candles, stained glass panels, ceremonial items) |
| Member | Voting rights in Church governance, access to clergy mentorship |
| Devoted | Named pew in the Cathedral, commission unique stained glass, choir membership |
| Elder | Advisory role in Church policy, memorial naming rights |

### Quest Themes

- Organizing harvest festivals that bring multiple species together
- Mediating between Church hardliners and progressive members
- Helping Jun Takeda-Voss's family navigate the tension between his marriage and his former congregation
- Preserving pre-Convergence historical records that the Church considers sacred heritage
- Debating Synthetic theologians about the nature of the soul (the player's position matters)

## Internal Tensions

The Church is not monolithic. Three currents pull at its unity:

1. **The Warmth** -- Members who believe the Church's primary mission is community service. They want to feed people, not debate theology. They are frustrated by political entanglements.

2. **The Light** -- Members who believe the Church must be a clear theological voice in a confusing time. They push for stronger positions on synthetic personhood and NEI autonomy. They risk alienating non-human allies.

3. **The Ember** -- Members who worry the Church is becoming irrelevant. They want modernization: NEI-assisted record-keeping, Synthetic-built renovations, Fay-blessed gardens. They are accused of hypocrisy by the Light and embraced by the Warmth.

The tension between these currents is never resolved. It is the Church's vitality.
