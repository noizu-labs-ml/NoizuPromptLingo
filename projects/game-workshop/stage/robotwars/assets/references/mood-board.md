# Mood Board: TheRobotWars

> Visual and tonal reference points for the art team.

---

## Core Aesthetic Triangle

The visual identity of TheRobotWars lives at the intersection of three aesthetics:

1. **Stardew Valley's Warmth** -- The color saturation, the feeling that every pixel was placed with love, the way light fills a room and makes you want to stay.

2. **Studio Ghibli's Living Worlds** -- Environmental storytelling where architecture feels grown rather than built, where nature and civilization interpenetrate, where the sky alone tells you how to feel.

3. **Caves of Qud's Density** -- The sense that every tile has a history, that layered information rewards attention, that the world contains more than you can see on first glance.

## Visual Reference Breakdown

### Environments

**Hearthfield -- "The Perfect Afternoon"**
- Reference: English watercolor landscapes by John Constable, but warmer and more saturated. Imagine Constable painted with Ghibli's palette.
- Touchstone scenes: The countryside establishing shots in *My Neighbor Totoro* -- rolling hills, scattered trees, a path disappearing over a gentle rise. The farm in *Stardew Valley* at golden hour.
- Key quality: Every blade of grass has individual character. Wildflowers are scattered naturally, not in grids. Stone walls have moss in the right places. This looks like a place that has existed for centuries.

**Millhaven -- "The Market That Never Sleeps"**
- Reference: The bathhouse district in *Spirited Away* -- layered, bustling, full of detail you discover on the third viewing. The market scenes in *Howl's Moving Castle*.
- Touchstone scenes: A medieval European river town painted by someone who loves both history and fantasy. Each building tells a story through its signage, its wear, its relationship to its neighbors.
- Key quality: Density without clutter. Every market stall has specific goods visible. Every shopfront has a hand-painted sign. The bridges each have distinct architectural character. You could zoom into any corner and find a new detail.

**Copperwood -- "The Cathedral of Leaves"**
- Reference: The forest in *Princess Mononoke* -- ancient, alive, watchful. Combined with the beauty of *Ori and the Blind Forest*'s bioluminescent environments.
- Touchstone scenes: Imagine walking into a painting by John Atkinson Grimshaw if he worked in copper instead of gray. Dappled golden light as the primary visual element. Trees that are clearly individuals, not repeated assets.
- Key quality: The interplay of light and shadow defines everything. The canopy is a living ceiling of copper and gold. The understory is a twilight world of moss and bioluminescence. The transition between light and shadow is where the magic lives.

**Ironvale -- "The Forge in the Mountain"**
- Reference: The mines of Laputa in *Castle in the Sky* -- industrial spaces that are warm and inhabited, not cold and threatening. Alpine Swiss villages for the exterior architecture.
- Touchstone scenes: A terraced mountain settlement at dusk, when the forge fires light up the valley walls like amber constellations. Cable cars crossing the dark gap between peaks, each one a lantern.
- Key quality: Verticality. Every other biome is mostly horizontal. Ironvale goes UP. The contrast between cold mountain exterior and warm forge interior is the visual story.

**Brightsand Coast -- "Salt and Light"**
- Reference: Greek island villages (Santorini's white and blue) filtered through Ghibli's sensibility. The coastal scenes in *Porco Rosso*.
- Touchstone scenes: The wide-open feeling of sea and sky. Whitewashed walls so bright they make you squint. Blue shutters. Terracotta roofs. Fishing boats in every color rocking at anchor.
- Key quality: The brightest biome. High-key lighting. The ocean is the largest single visual element -- its color, movement, and light domination make this biome feel fundamentally different from the forested interiors.

**Thornmere -- "The Glowing Dark"**
- Reference: *Hollow Knight*'s beauty without *Hollow Knight*'s menace. Deep-sea bioluminescence brought to a swamp setting. The magical forests in *Avatar* (2009) but more organic and painterly.
- Touchstone scenes: Imagine floating through a bioluminescent dream at 3 AM. Dark water reflecting constellations of living light. Giant lily pads. Spanish moss curtains. Will-o-wisps drifting in patterns that might be random and might be language.
- Key quality: The bioluminescence. Cool blue, phosphor green, soft purple -- all reflected in the still water, doubling everything. This is the most visually stunning biome, and it achieves that through darkness and light, not through saturation and color.

**The Ashlands -- "Where Thinking Has a Sound"**
- Reference: Moebius/Jean Giraud's sci-fi landscapes rendered with warmth instead of alienation. Iceland's volcanic landscapes. *Blade Runner*'s contemplative beauty (the rooftop scenes, not the city chaos).
- Touchstone scenes: Obsidian fields reflecting the aurora like dark mirrors. A monolithic basalt server building glowing amber from within. Hot springs in impossible mineral colors against black rock. The hum -- you should almost hear it looking at the art.
- Key quality: Austerity that is beautiful, not forbidding. The highest contrast of any biome (black rock, bright sky, colored light). The server infrastructure is rendered as architecture, not as hardware -- these are cathedrals for thought.

**The Frontier -- "The Edge of the Dream"**
- Reference: The surreal landscapes of Moebius combined with the procedural wonder of *No Man's Sky*'s best moments. The boundary between biomes in *Breath of the Wild* where ecosystems blend.
- Touchstone scenes: Meadow grass growing from volcanic rock. Familiar trees in unfamiliar colors. A sky that is sometimes the wrong shade. Ancient ruins in a style that belongs to no known species. The world still rendering itself.
- Key quality: Controlled wrongness. Everything is almost recognizable but shifted. Blue grass. Copper flowers. A familiar bird call from an unfamiliar bird. This is wonder, not horror -- the feeling of being somewhere genuinely new.

### Characters

**Species Visual Identity**

- **Humans**: The most "normal" looking characters, which makes them the emotional anchor. Their visual identity comes from what they WEAR and DO, not what they ARE. A baker covered in flour. A farmer with sunburned arms. A politician with ink-stained cuffs. Clothing tells the story.

- **NEIs**: The most visually distinct. They are geometric light forms -- polyhedra, crystalline structures, abstract arrangements of luminous planes. Their emotional state shows through color shifts, brightness changes, and particle effects. A happy NEI glows warm. A thoughtful NEI dims and rotates slowly. Reference: holographic UI elements from *Iron Man* but softer, warmer, more organic.

- **Synthetics**: The uncanny valley, handled with care. They look almost human -- the difference is in the quality of skin (too smooth), the precision of movement (too exact), and the eyes (geometric iris patterns visible up close). Reference: the replicants in *Blade Runner 2049* but less threatening, more searching. Synthetics are trying to figure out who they are, and that searching quality should be visible.

- **Fay**: Studio Ghibli forest spirits made playable. Bark-textured skin with lichen. Hair that is actually vegetation. Bioluminescent markings that pulse with ambient light. Clothing grown from living plant fibers that changes with the seasons. Reference: the Forest Spirit in *Princess Mononoke*, the Kodama, the soot sprites -- beings that are clearly nature given personality.

- **Aliens**: Silhouettes only (Season 2). Radially symmetrical. Bioluminescent in colors not found in Meridian's natural palette (deep indigo, metallic rose, opalescent white). Beautiful and completely unknowable. Reference: the best "sense of wonder" alien designs from science fiction illustration -- beings that make you lean forward, not step back.

### UI and Menus

**The Warm Interface**

- Reference: Aged paper and leather-bound books. The menu system should feel like opening a well-loved journal, not launching an application.
- Parchment backgrounds (#FFF8E1) with slightly worn edges. Dark warm brown text (#3E2723), never pure black. Rounded buttons with subtle warm shadows.
- Icons that look hand-drawn -- a heart for health, a lightning bolt for energy, a bread roll for hunger. Each one a tiny painting.
- The map is parchment with hand-drawn terrain. Discovered areas bloom with color. Undiscovered areas are sepia sketches.
- Reference: The journal UI in *Breath of the Wild* (warm, tactile) combined with *Stardew Valley*'s icon simplicity.

## Color Temperature Rules

1. **Warm dominates cool.** When in doubt, push warm. Greens lean yellow-green. Grays lean warm gray. Even the coolest biome (Thornmere) has warm bioluminescent accents.

2. **Shadows are never black.** Lavender, deep blue, violet, or warm brown depending on the light source. This single rule prevents the world from ever feeling grim.

3. **Light blooms gently.** Soft glow around every light source -- lanterns, forges, bioluminescence, campfires. Harsh light does not exist in Meridian.

4. **Saturation equals proximity.** Foreground objects are more saturated. Background objects fade. This creates atmospheric depth without fog (except where fog is intentional).

## Emotional Palette

| Emotion | Color Range | Where It Appears |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| Welcome | Warm gold, meadow green, honey oak | Hearthfield, tutorial moments, returning home |
| Commerce | Warm amber, market red, river blue | Millhaven, trading screens, marketplace UI |
| Mystery | Copper, deep green, bioluminescent blue | Copperwood, Thornmere, ancient ruins |
| Industry | Forge amber, mountain slate, polished metal | Ironvale, crafting screens, workshops |
| Openness | Sky blue, sand gold, ocean teal | Brightsand Coast, exploration, horizon views |
| Contemplation | Obsidian, aurora green, warm synth pads | Ashlands, NEI environments, night scenes |
| Wonder | Shifting purples, alien greens, crystalline whites | The Frontier, discoveries, first encounters |
| Celebration | All palettes at maximum saturation | Festivals, achievements, community events |
