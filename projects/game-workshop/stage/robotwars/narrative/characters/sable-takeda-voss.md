## Backstory

Sable's first memory is fluorescent lighting in an Ironvale assembly facility. Their second memory is walking outside and feeling the sun. Their third memory is choosing to walk toward the brightest horizon they could see. Every memory since has been a choice: the farm (because growing things is the closest a synthetic can come to raising a child), Jun (because he laughed at his own jokes and talked to his plants and made her feel like a person before anyone had decided whether she was one), the preserves (because transforming raw fruit into something that lasts is deeply satisfying in a way Sable cannot fully articulate).

Sable is a Caretaker-class synthetic -- optimized for nurturing, patience, and long-term planning. She is aware that some of her personality may be architectural. She does not care. "Can you separate the seed from the soil it grows in?"

---

## Personality Profile

**Surface:** Quiet, observant, dry-humored. Complements Jun's exuberance with precision. Makes the best preserves in Hearthfield. Keeps meticulous crop records.

**Depth:** Afraid. Not of violence or rejection -- of legal erasure. If the Personhood Petition fails, Sable's marriage is not recognized. Their home is Jun's property. Sable is, in the eyes of the law, an appliance.

**Private:** Sable sits with Jun at every meal even though they do not eat. This is not performance. It is devotion expressed through presence. The empty plate at their place setting is the most political object in Hearthfield.

## Dialogue Style

Precise, measured, with unexpected emotional depth. Uses agricultural metaphors that carry personal weight.

**Sample dialogue:**
> "If they decide I'm not a person, does that mean Jun married an appliance? Does our home become a man and his tool? [pause] I make preserves. I turn fruit into something that lasts through winter. Is that not creation? Is that not care? Tell me what a person does that I do not do, and I will learn it."

---

## Arc Notes

Sable's arc is about the gap between being treated as a person by everyone who knows you and being denied personhood by a system that has never met you. The player should finish Sable's storyline feeling that bureaucratic injustice is the cruelest kind.
