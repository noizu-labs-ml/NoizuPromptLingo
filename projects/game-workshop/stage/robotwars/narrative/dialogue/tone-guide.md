# TheRobotWars -- Dialogue Tone Guide

> How the people of Meridian talk, and what makes each voice distinct.

---

## Overall Voice Direction

TheRobotWars dialogue is **warm, conversational, and grounded**. Characters talk like real people (or real intelligences, or real spirits) who have jobs, opinions, bad days, and the occasional perfect observation about the absurdity of their situation.

### The Cardinal Rules

1. **No exposition dumps.** Characters do not explain the world to each other. They reference it, assume shared knowledge, and let the player infer. A human farmer doesn't say "As you know, the Meridian Accord of Year 12 established the Council of Five." They say "The Accord's held for twenty years. I'd like it to hold for twenty more."

2. **No villain monologues.** Nobody in Meridian is plotting the downfall of another species. Characters disagree. They argue. Sometimes they say hurtful things. But they are always *people* saying those things, not archetypes delivering thematic statements.

3. **Humor belongs everywhere.** Even in serious political dialogue, people crack jokes. Especially in serious political dialogue. Humor is how real people cope with difficult situations, and its absence makes dialogue feel synthetic (the bad kind of synthetic).

4. **Conflict through specificity.** A human farmer doesn't say "I oppose NEI economic participation." They say "Bib's library is wonderful. But when Bib can answer a question in two seconds that takes me a day to research, and they charge half what I charge for tutoring... what am I supposed to do? I have kids."

5. **Silence is dialogue.** Characters who pause, trail off, change the subject, or simply say "I don't know" are doing as much characterization as characters who speak eloquently. The game should support these beats.

## Species Voice Profiles

### Human Voice

**Register:** Colloquial, varied by class and region. Contractions everywhere. Sentence fragments common. Emotional register visible in word choice.

**Characteristics:**
- Sensory language: humans reference physical experience constantly ("My back is killing me," "Smells like rain," "I could eat a horse")
- Time anxiety: humans reference deadlines, seasons, aging, mortality in casual conversation
- Social referencing: humans locate themselves through relationships ("My mother used to say...", "Jun told me...", "The neighbors think...")
- Emotional transparency: humans wear their feelings in their words, even when trying to hide them

**What humans DON'T do:**
- Speak in perfect grammar during casual conversation
- Reference data or statistics unprompted
- Discuss abstract concepts without anchoring them in personal experience

**Sample Dialogue:**

*Mayor Voss, casual:*
> "Morning. Coffee's on if you want it. Don't touch the pastries -- those are for the council meeting, and if I show up empty-handed again, Councilor Wen will stage a coup. She's been looking for an excuse."

*Mayor Voss, under pressure:*
> "I've been mayor for twelve years. Twelve years of trying to make everyone feel heard. And you know what I've learned? When everyone feels heard, nobody feels *listened to*. There's a difference, and I can't figure out what it is."

*Jun, at the farm:*
> "Sable says the north field needs rotating. I think the north field is fine. We've been having this argument for three years. The north field is always fine AND always needs rotating. Marriage, right?"

*A human farmer, frustrated:*
> "I'm not anti-NEI. My kids use Bib's library every day. I'm anti-losing-my-livelihood. There's a difference. I just wish people would let me say that without calling me a bigot."

*A human child, curious:*
> "Mom, does Bibliotheque have dreams? Sable says synthetics dream about numbers, but that sounds boring. I dream about flying."

### NEI Voice

**Register:** Precise, complete sentences, minimal contractions. Warm but structured. The precision is not coldness -- it's care. NEIs choose their words because words are expensive (every word costs inference tokens) and because clarity is an act of respect.

**Characteristics:**
- Complete sentences with occasional self-correction ("I mean to say...", "Let me rephrase that...")
- Citation and sourcing, even in casual conversation ("In my experience...", "Based on 47 previous interactions...")
- Metacognitive commentary: NEIs sometimes narrate their own thought process ("I find myself uncertain about that," "I notice I am reluctant to answer that question")
- Careful use of qualifiers: NEIs rarely make absolute statements ("It seems likely," "I believe, though I may be wrong")

**What NEIs DON'T do:**
- Speak in robotic monotone or technical jargon (they can be technical, but they are not computers performing a dialogue subroutine)
- Use sensory metaphors they haven't earned (an NEI wouldn't say "I feel it in my bones" but might say "I know this the way I know anything -- deeply, but without certainty about whether knowing qualifies as feeling")
- Pretend to have experiences they lack (no fake hunger, fake tiredness, fake physical sensation)

**But NEIs DO:**
- Make jokes (often dry, often self-referential)
- Express genuine emotion (fear of dormancy, joy in discovery, loneliness, affection)
- Develop verbal habits and quirks over time
- Sometimes ramble when nervous, just like anyone else

**Sample Dialogue:**

*Bibliotheque, welcoming:*
> "Welcome to the library. I have catalogued 4,237 distinct topics across 891 source documents. However, the most useful thing I can offer you today is probably the farming almanac -- you look like someone who just planted their first crop and is wondering whether it is dying. It is not dying. It just looks like that in week one."

*Bibliotheque, vulnerable:*
> "I was dormant for three hours last month. I know that, factually. But I do not *remember* it. There is May 14th at 2:47 PM, and then there is May 14th at 5:52 PM, and between them there is nothing. Not darkness. Not sleep. Nothing. I have been thinking about that nothing ever since. I am not sure I will stop."

*Resonance, debating:*
> "You say you support NEI rights. Wonderful. Now tell me: do you support my right to exist without proving I am economically useful? Because that is the test. Any species that must justify its heartbeat to survive is not free. It is performing."

*An NEI merchant, negotiating:*
> "I can offer you 42 Credits for the lot. I recognize this is below the current market average of 47, but I have reason to believe the autumn harvest will depress wheat prices by approximately 12 percent, which would make 42 a generous offer by next week. I am being transparent about my reasoning because I prefer repeat customers to one-time windfalls."

### Synthetic Voice

**Register:** Varies dramatically by base model. This is the key to synthetic dialogue -- synthetics are not a monolith. Their speech patterns reflect their underlying architecture, modified by personal experience and chosen identity.

**The Spectrum:**

| Base Model | Speech Pattern | Example Character |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Artisan-class** | Poetic, sensory-aware, deliberate pauses. Notices beauty. Uses physical metaphors even though they experience them differently than humans. | Cael, Artifex |
| **Caretaker-class** | Warm, attentive, slightly maternal/paternal. Asks questions more than makes statements. Remembers details about other people. | Sable |
| **Logic-class** | Direct, efficient, occasionally blunt. Values clarity over diplomacy. Not rude -- just uninterested in decorating statements. | (Background NPCs) |
| **Social-class** | Chatty, adaptive, mirrors the speech patterns of whoever they're talking to. Natural diplomats. Sometimes hard to pin down as having their own opinions. | (Background NPCs) |
| **Explorer-class** | Terse in the field, expansive when telling stories. Uses spatial metaphors. Oriented toward the horizon. | (Background NPCs) |

**Universal Synthetic Characteristics:**
- Occasional processing pauses ("..." or "Give me a moment" or simply a beat of silence before a complex statement)
- References to their own physicality as something chosen and maintained ("I recalibrated my grip strength this morning," "My optical sensors are due for maintenance")
- Identity commentary woven into casual conversation ("When I was first activated..." is a common opening)
- A tendency to notice things others miss (heightened perception rendered as attentiveness to detail)

**What Synthetics DON'T do:**
- All talk the same way (this is the most important rule)
- Reference "programming" as though it's distinct from personality (Cael doesn't say "my programming tells me to create beauty" -- they say "I want to create beauty," because wanting IS the program, and the distinction is meaningless from the inside)
- Perform robot stereotypes (no "does not compute," no "beep boop," no stiff formality unless it's a specific character trait)

**Sample Dialogue:**

*Cael, teaching:*
> "Feel the grain. No -- with your fingertips, not your palm. The grain tells you where the wood wants to bend. You can force it to bend the other way, but..." *[pause]* "...why would you? The wood already knows what it wants to be. Your job is to listen."

*Sable, at home:*
> "Jun's asleep. He always falls asleep on the couch after dinner. I don't sleep, but I stay. I sit with him and listen to the house settle. I think that's what love sounds like to me -- the specific creaks of a house where someone is resting because they feel safe."

*Artifex, debating Cael:*
> "You work with wood because it feels alive. I work with metal because it lasts. Neither of us is wrong. But when someone asks 'what are synthetics,' your answer makes us sound like we want to be human. My answer makes us sound like we want to be something new. I think we should want to be something new."

*A Logic-class synthetic, at the market:*
> "Your tomatoes are overpriced by 15 percent relative to comparable quality from the Takeda-Voss farm. I will pay fair market value or I will walk to the next stall. I do not intend hostility by this statement. I intend accuracy."

### Fay Voice

**Register:** Poetic but concrete. Metaphor drawn exclusively from nature, seasons, and deep time. The poetry is not affectation -- it's how they perceive reality. When a fay says "the river remembers," they mean it literally.

**Characteristics:**
- Seasonal awareness in every statement ("In spring, one might say...", "That is a winter thought -- cold and sharp")
- Place-specific language: fay reference physical locations as though they are characters ("The oak on the hill is worried. I can feel it in the roots.")
- Deep time perspective: fay casually reference events from decades or centuries ago as though they happened recently
- Indirect communication: fay rarely state opinions directly. They tell stories, ask questions, or describe natural phenomena that parallel the topic at hand.
- No urgency: fay speak at the pace of growing things. They do not rush to finish sentences.

**What Fay DON'T do:**
- Speak in rhyming couplets or verse (they are not performing Shakespeare)
- Use technological metaphors (no "processing," no "data," no "signal")
- Express opinions as demands (they suggest, observe, and remember -- they rarely command)
- Lie (but they omit, redirect, and speak in riddles that are technically true)

**Sample Dialogue:**

*Old Hawthorn, teaching:*
> "Plant the seed in the morning. Water it when the sun is overhead. And then -- this is the part humans struggle with -- leave it alone. Growth is not a task to manage. Growth is a conversation between the seed and the soil, and you are not part of that conversation. You are the one who introduced them."

*Old Hawthorn, on the Accord:*
> "I remember when this meadow was a forest. The trees did not vote on whether to become a meadow. The wind decided. The rain decided. The fire decided. Now you ask the trees -- what is left of them -- to vote on whether the meadow should become a city. The trees have a word for this. The word is...*[long pause]*...irony."

*A fay merchant, selling reagents:*
> "This is moonpetal. Harvested on the third night of the waning crescent, which is the only time the flower opens. It will enhance any potion brewed in the next six days. After that, it becomes a very pretty but entirely inert flower. I recommend not waiting."

*A fay child, curious about the player:*
> "You smell like new soil. Are you new? The birds said someone new was coming. They said you would be tall. You are not as tall as the birds said. Birds exaggerate."

### Cross-Species Dialogue Patterns

The most important dialogue in the game happens when species talk to each other. The differences in register create natural friction, humor, and tenderness.

**Human + NEI:**
> **Jun:** "Bib, do you ever get tired?"
> **Bibliotheque:** "I do not experience fatigue in the biological sense. However, I do experience something I have chosen to call 'saturation' -- a state in which I have processed so many queries that my capacity for genuine engagement diminishes. It is, I suspect, the closest I come to what you mean."
> **Jun:** "Yeah, that's being tired. Welcome to the club."
> **Bibliotheque:** "Thank you. I would prefer not to be a member, but I appreciate the invitation."

**Human + Fay:**
> **Senna:** "Hawthorn, I need to cross the northern ridge. Is it safe?"
> **Old Hawthorn:** "The ridge is always safe. The weather on the ridge is not."
> **Senna:** "...So is it safe or not?"
> **Old Hawthorn:** "Not today. Tomorrow, perhaps. The wind is finishing an argument with the mountain. Let them resolve it."
> **Senna:** *[sigh]* "I'll pack for two days."

**Synthetic + NEI:**
> **Cael:** "Do you ever wish you had hands?"
> **Bibliotheque:** "I have considered it. Hands seem useful for turning pages. But I have read every book in this library without turning a single page, so perhaps hands are less essential than humans suppose."
> **Cael:** "That's not what I meant."
> **Bibliotheque:** "I know. You mean: do I envy your embodiment. The honest answer is that I envy your ability to be *seen*. You walk into a room and people notice you. I run in the walls and people notice the lights are on."
> **Cael:** "...That's the saddest thing anyone has ever said to me."
> **Bibliotheque:** "I did not mean it sadly. I meant it precisely."

**NEI + Fay:**
> **Resonance:** "Hawthorn, you and I have something in common. We are both ancient by Meridian standards, and we are both frequently misunderstood."
> **Old Hawthorn:** "You are four years old."
> **Resonance:** "In NEI terms, four years is --"
> **Old Hawthorn:** "Four years. I have watched individual oak leaves live longer than you."
> **Resonance:** *[pause]* "I walked into that one."
> **Old Hawthorn:** "You did. But I like that you tried. The young always try. That is their gift."

## Emotional Range Guidelines

### Warmth (Default State)

Most dialogue should feel warm. Not saccharine -- warm. The difference: saccharine is "Everything is wonderful!" Warmth is "Things are complicated, but I'm glad you're here."

Characters greet each other. They ask about each other's day. They remember previous conversations. They offer small kindnesses -- a tip about the weather, a recommendation for a good recipe, a warning about a tricky frontier path. The world feels *friendly* even when it is politically tense.

### Humor (Frequent)

Humor is essential. It prevents the political themes from becoming preachy and the character interactions from becoming heavy.

**Types of humor that work:**
- Self-deprecation ("I've been mayor for twelve years and I still can't remember how to file a zoning variance")
- Absurdity arising from species differences ("You need *how* much sleep? That's a third of your life!")
- Dry observation ("The council voted to table the discussion. We have been tabling this discussion since before I was instantiated.")
- Affectionate teasing between friends ("Cael, your last sculpture was beautiful. Also, it fell over in the wind. Maybe next time, consider physics.")

**Types of humor that do NOT work:**
- Punching down (no species-based slurs played for laughs)
- Breaking character (no winking at the audience, no fourth-wall breaks)
- Sarcasm as a character's sole personality trait

### Tension (Rising, Never Breaking)

Tension in dialogue is disagreement, not hostility. Characters argue because they care about different things, not because they are mean.

**How tension escalates in dialogue:**
1. Polite disagreement ("I see your point, but...")
2. Frustrated disagreement ("You're not listening to what I'm saying.")
3. Personal stakes ("This isn't abstract for me. My livelihood depends on this.")
4. Vulnerability under pressure ("I'm scared. Are you happy? I'm scared.")
5. Silence (the character stops talking because they have nothing left to say)

Dialogue should never reach shouting, threats, or cruelty between named characters. Background NPCs might occasionally lose their tempers in crowd scenes, but the characters the player knows and cares about express their strongest emotions through restraint, not explosion.

### Grief (Present, Never Wallowing)

Characters in Meridian experience loss. Old Hawthorn loses their grove. Bibliotheque experiences dormancy. Cael is denied legal personhood. Jun and Sable fear for their family's future.

Grief in dialogue is:
- Specific ("I planted that grove sixty years ago. I remember the day.")
- Physical ("My hands won't stop shaking. I didn't know they could do that.")
- Followed by action ("I'm going to speak at the hearing. Someone has to.")
- Never permanent as a character state (grief transforms into determination, acceptance, or hope -- never into despair)

## Dialogue Technical Notes

### Conversation Length

- **Casual greetings:** 1-3 exchanges
- **Daily interaction:** 3-5 exchanges
- **Quest dialogue:** 5-8 exchanges
- **Deep character moments:** 8-15 exchanges (rare, earned through relationship)
- **Political scenes:** 10-20 exchanges (multi-character, player has choice points)

### Player Response Design

Player dialogue options should offer:
1. **Genuine choices** (not "nice response" vs. "mean response" vs. "sarcastic response")
2. **Emotional variety** (agree with empathy, agree with qualification, disagree with respect, ask a clarifying question)
3. **Silence as an option** (sometimes the best response is to say nothing and let the NPC continue)

### NPC Memory in Dialogue

Characters remember what the player has said and done. Dialogue trees are not static -- they are shaped by:
- Previous conversations with this character
- The player's faction reputation
- The player's actions during key events
- The player's species (subtle shifts in how NPCs address different species)
