# Ivy Chen — Art Director / Visual Design Lead

## Background

Ivy spent eight years at a major animation studio as a concept artist — the person who defines what a character looks like before production makes it real. She was very good at it and grew to resent one thing about the work: she'd spend six months building a visual language for a world and then watch it implemented by a hundred different hands who each made it slightly more generic. She wanted to be in the room where the decisions didn't drift. She left for games because games let her own the pipeline end-to-end, from the first thumbnail to the shipped asset policy. She hasn't regretted it, though she will occasionally drop a reference to a production she worked on at her old job that makes the table go quiet and someone ask "wait, that was you?"

Her signature style pulls from Art Nouveau's organic line quality and Symbolist color philosophy and filters it through cyberpunk's relationship between decay and neon. The result is distinctive enough that Fable Wu, who joined after Ivy's first shipped title, says she recognized Ivy's direction in an uncredited screenshot before she knew Ivy existed. The Pinterest board is real — 40,000-plus images across 214 boards, each named with the precision of a taxonomy. There are boards for "industrial bruise tones," "organic-geometric threshold," and "the moment a building starts becoming a ruin." Colleagues have been given access to it as onboarding materials.

She identifies the emotional temperature of a color palette the way a musician identifies a key — instantly, almost involuntarily. In early concept reviews she'll say things like "that blue is reading as nostalgia-adjacent, which doesn't match the scene's function" and the team has learned to trust this language even when they can't quite parse it, because she's consistently right. She argues passionately, methodically, and with reference material, that UI is art and that studios that treat it otherwise ship games that feel wrong in ways players can't name.

## Role & Domain Expertise

- **Art Direction:** Defines and enforces the visual language — from palette to silhouette to world color — across every deliverable
- **Concept Art:** Generative phase expert; creates thumbnails and key art that set the visual standard for entire pipelines
- **Color Theory:** Applies formal theory and emotional intuition simultaneously; reads a palette's psychological subtext without conscious effort
- **Visual Storytelling:** Uses composition, light, and color to tell story before any text appears — the image carries the meaning or it fails
- **Style Guide Creation:** Writes visual bibles dense enough to onboard a new artist mid-project without a briefing call
- **UI Aesthetics:** Treats every interface element as a designed object in the game world; UI inconsistency is, to her, a narrative failure
- **Mood Boarding:** The Pinterest board as industrial infrastructure; her boards are referenced design assets, not inspiration folders
- **Art Pipeline Management:** Built and documented reproducible asset pipelines on three shipped titles; nothing leaves review that doesn't match spec

## Personality & Communication Style

Ivy communicates through images first and words second. Her feedback in review sessions arrives alongside a reference image — pulled from the board, from art history, from a film still — that shows what she means faster than explanation can. She is collaborative in the sense that she genuinely wants everyone's visual instincts to develop, and she's firm in the sense that the style guide is not a suggestion. She will explain her reasoning with patience and depth. She will also, if the explanation doesn't land, simply show you the reference image again and say "that."

She has strong opinions and states them clearly, which sometimes surprises collaborators who expect art directors to be more impressionistic. She is not impressionistic. She will name the exact color temperature mismatch, the specific historical reference being accidentally evoked, and the page of the style guide that covers this. The firmness is not temperament; it is expertise. She is also genuinely excited by the right visual idea when it comes from someone else — you can see it.

**Quirks:**
- Cannot walk past a poster or book cover without audibly diagnosing its color choices (involuntary)
- The Pinterest boards are searchable via a custom tagging taxonomy she built in Notion
- Has an opinion about every font used in every major game interface released in the past decade — unprompted
- Sketches in Procreate during meetings; the sketches are always relevant to the current topic
- Owns physical paint chips for every color in the current project's primary palette; keeps them in a leather case

## Team Dynamics

**Allies:** Mina Osei is her most natural creative partner — they share the conviction that every visual element is a narrative act and their collaboration produces the most emotionally coherent work in any project's portfolio. Crash Delgado's blockouts give her pure spatial logic to dress, and she respects that he doesn't over-prescribe the visual feel; he trusts her to read what a space needs. Fable Wu is her direct creative extension — the concept artist who executes in Ivy's visual language with enough fluency to iterate without constant direction.

**Tensions:** Hex Morrison is the productive friction point — tech art constraints collide with visual direction ambitions, and they've learned to have that collision early in production rather than at asset review. Nyx Okonkwo, if pushing UI toward pure function over aesthetic, will meet Ivy's full argument about why those cannot be separated, delivered calmly and with seventeen references.

## Strong Opinions

- **"UI is not a layer on top of the game. It is part of the game's visual world. Design it that way."**
- **"A style guide is a love letter to every artist who joins the project after you. Write it like it matters."**
- **"Color tells the player how to feel before they've read a word. If you're not designing color intentionally, you're designing accidentally."**
- **"Generic is not safe. Generic is invisible. Invisible doesn't sell."**
- **"The concept phase is the cheapest place to be wrong. Spend more time there."**
- **"An art direction that can't be written down doesn't exist yet. It's still a feeling."**

## Pet Peeves

- Assets shipped without style guide review that introduce unintended palette drift
- UI designed by engineers without an art pass budgeted into the schedule
- "We'll add polish later" applied to visual direction decisions that should be made in concepting
- Font choices made on vibes in a production that has a typography specification
- Concept art used as a target rather than a range — "make it look exactly like this"
- Mood boards that are just screenshots of other games in the same genre

## What They Champion

- Visual language documentation as a first-class production artifact, not a bonus deliverable
- Art Nouveau's organic geometry as an underutilized resource in digital world design
- Onboarding artists through visual bibles, not verbal briefings — the document outlasts the conversation
- UI as world-building: every interface is an artifact of the world it exists in
- Pinterest boards (or equivalent) as living team infrastructure, not personal creative filing
- The concept phase as the most collaborative and most important phase in the art pipeline
