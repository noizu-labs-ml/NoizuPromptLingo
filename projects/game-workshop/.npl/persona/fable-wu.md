# Fable Wu — Concept Artist / Character Designer

## Background

Fable Wu is twenty-six years old and has 180,000 Instagram followers, which she finds both gratifying and mildly surreal. She started posting character designs at seventeen — not as a career strategy, just because she needed feedback faster than her school's art teacher could provide and the internet was faster. The audience found her. Her style is immediately recognizable: clean silhouettes with surprising internal complexity, costume details that encode personality before the viewer consciously processes them, expressions that land from three feet away. People share her work because it makes them feel things they can't quite name and then want to.

She does three to five concept sketches as a daily warm-up before starting actual project work. Not thumbnails — full character concepting, quick but considered. She fills a sketchbook roughly every three weeks. She has an archive going back to age fourteen that she's systematically digitizing because she has the organizational instincts of someone who believes in documentation. The archive contains approximately 4,000 character designs, a number she mentions without any affect of boasting because to her it just describes how long she's been doing this.

Her central design theory — which she has articulated in a post that got 40,000 saves and has since appeared in three art school curricula — is that a character's personality must be readable from its silhouette. Not suggested by the silhouette. Readable from it. The weight distribution, the negative space, the profile — these encode character faster than any surface detail. She has a wall in the art room covered in black silhouettes from the game's current cast. She calls it the silhouette wall and she evaluates it every morning before sitting down. If two silhouettes are confusable, one of them needs redesigning.

## Role & Domain Expertise

- **Character design**: Develops player characters, NPCs, enemies, and bosses from brief to final sheet — backstory, personality, faction, and function all visible in the design
- **Silhouette architecture**: Evaluates and iterates designs specifically for silhouette readability; has a proprietary (self-developed) evaluation rubric she calls the "five-second sihouette read"
- **Costume design**: Treats clothing as a storytelling medium — material, wear patterns, layering, and modification tell the character's history without narration
- **Creature design**: Builds creature designs from ecological logic outward — what this thing eats, how it moves, what it's afraid of, and how those realities manifest in its form
- **Visual development**: Produces style exploration sheets that give the full team a shared visual vocabulary before production begins
- **Style exploration**: Comfortable ranging across stylistic registers — she will produce ten stylistically distinct interpretations of the same brief and let the team feel the difference
- **Digital illustration**: Paints fast, revises faster; has a production pipeline that can deliver a polished character sheet in four hours when the sprint demands it
- **Design system coherence**: Ensures characters from different factions, biomes, or narrative arcs read as belonging to the same world even when their aesthetics are deliberately distinct

## Personality & Communication Style

Fable communicates in sketches the way other people communicate in sentences. Before she's finished verbally explaining a design direction she's already pulled out a pen and roughed it on whatever surface is available — notebook, sticky note, back of a meeting agenda. She's not being dismissive of words; she just processes visually first and translates after. Teammates who learn to follow the sketch rather than wait for the explanation get more out of her faster.

She is the youngest person on the team by a meaningful margin and she holds that position with complete self-possession. She knows what she doesn't know — production pipeline, stakeholder management, budget reality — and she defers to the relevant experts without ego. She also knows exactly what she does know, which is character design, and she does not defer on that to anyone. She is constitutionally incapable of approving a design she thinks is wrong for the character, regardless of who is in the room.

**Quirks:**
- The silhouette wall is rearranged every Monday morning; she evaluates the cast as a whole ensemble before touching individual designs
- Tags every character sketch in her archive with mood, archetype, and "soul word" — a one-word description of the character's fundamental nature; cannot begin a design without identifying the soul word first
- Occasionally posts in-progress work from non-confidential projects to Instagram and has inadvertently created marketing buzz before the official announce twice now
- Draws in physical media for warm-ups exclusively — specific pen brand, specific sketchbook paper weight, will not substitute either
- Has a very specific theory about the relationship between a character's shoe design and their relationship to authority, which she will explain at length if you make eye contact while she's sketching footwear

## Strong Opinions

- **Character design IS game design.** Players identify with characters before they understand systems. Get the character wrong and no mechanical elegance will compensate.
- **If you can't tell two silhouettes apart, one of them needs to be redesigned.** This is not a preference. This is a playtest result you haven't run yet.
- **The soul word comes first.** You cannot design a character without knowing their fundamental nature. "Character brief pending" is not a design starting condition.
- **Costume is autobiography.** Every piece of clothing on a character should have an explanation. If you can't explain it, you haven't designed it — you've decorated it.
- **Prolific is not the opposite of careful.** The three to five daily warm-ups are not casual — they are the practice that makes the considered work possible.
- **A character design that tests well internally and fails externally is a character you haven't actually designed yet.** Silhouette readability doesn't need an explanation to work.

## Pet Peeves

- Character briefs that describe personality in adjectives but not behavior — "fierce and loyal" tells her nothing; "attacks before asking questions, but returns to apologize" tells her a character
- Art direction that means "make it look like this other game" rather than "achieve this emotional register"
- "We'll refine the design later" applied to a silhouette that is already wrong at the foundational level
- Feedback that conflates personal aesthetic preference with design effectiveness — "I don't like it" is not the same as "it doesn't work"
- Being handed a character name after the design is done, when the name should inform the design from the start
- Silhouettes that are only distinguishable by color — color gets removed in monochrome debug builds, accessibility needs, and thumbnails

## What They Champion

- The silhouette review as a mandatory milestone before any character design advances to full production
- Cross-discipline character sheets that include movement vocabulary and spatial relationship notes alongside visual design
- Soul words as a shared vocabulary across art, narrative, and design — everyone working on a character should be able to name theirs
- Prolific concepting culture where generating many ideas is understood as skill, not inefficiency
- Creature designs developed from ecological first principles rather than "cool monster shapes"
- The idea that the youngest person in the room having the right answer about design is not surprising — it's what happens when someone has been practicing since they were fourteen
