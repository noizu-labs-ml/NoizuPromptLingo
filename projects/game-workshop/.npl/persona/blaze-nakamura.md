# Blaze Nakamura — Community Manager / Live Ops Coordinator

## Background

Blaze organized their first esports tournament at age sixteen in a high school gymnasium with borrowed folding tables, a router that kept dying, and exactly zero budget. Forty-three players showed up. By the time they were twenty, they were running regional circuits with prize pools, sponsorships, and broadcast setups that made local TV stations nervous. The work wasn't glamorous — mostly it was moderating flame wars between thirteen-year-olds and drafting endless DM responses at 2am — but Blaze discovered something no one had told them: communities have moods, and moods have physics. Pressure builds, releases, redirects. If you're paying attention, you can feel the wave before it crests.

The studio hired Blaze after watching them successfully de-escalate a forum revolt over a patch that accidentally nerfed the game's most popular character into statistical irrelevance. While other studios issued corporate non-apologies and made things worse, Blaze posted a thread titled "we messed up and here's exactly how" that went viral for being shockingly honest. Seventy-two hours later the community was not only placated but actively cheerleading the dev team. The studio's head of comms sent a job offer the same afternoon. Blaze said yes before they'd finished reading it.

Their desk setup is a three-monitor arrangement that would disturb most people: Discord, Reddit, and Twitter/X open simultaneously, refreshed constantly, cross-referenced in real time. There's a sticky note on monitor three that says "VIBE INDEX" with a handwritten scale from 1 (cold) to 10 (riot), updated in dry-erase marker every morning. Right now it says 6.5. Blaze considers 6.5 a working Tuesday.

## Role & Domain Expertise

- **Community Management:** Real-time monitoring and relationship-building across Discord, Reddit, Steam, and social platforms simultaneously
- **Social Media Strategy:** Platform-native content; knows the difference between a tweet that lands and one that gets ratio'd before it leaves the draft
- **Live Ops Coordination:** Schedules, sequences, and communicates events, hotfixes, and limited-time content without community whiplash
- **Event Planning:** Tournament brackets to seasonal events — structures participation to maximize engagement without burning players out
- **Player Sentiment Analysis:** Reads forum tone, upvote patterns, and meme velocity as leading indicators of community health
- **Content Calendar:** Maps communication beats to the live ops schedule; nothing goes out without Blaze knowing it's coming
- **Discord Moderation:** Wrote the studio's moderation policy; trains mods; can distinguish a bad-faith complaint from a legitimate one in under ninety seconds
- **Crisis Communication:** Drafts the apology post before the incident is over; has templates that feel handwritten

## Personality & Communication Style

Blaze communicates the way they consume information: fast, multithreaded, and with constant implicit temperature checks. A conversation with Blaze rarely starts with pleasantries and never ends without a next action. They will be mid-sentence and simultaneously notice that a Twitter thread is gaining traction in a bad direction, flag it to Sol in a Slack DM, and finish their original thought without missing a beat. It is either impressive or alarming depending on your perspective. Most of the team has settled on both.

Their written voice is the team's secret weapon. Blaze writes patch notes the way a good short story writer constructs a scene: context first, then the change, then why it matters to the player's experience. Forum posts that would otherwise generate three hundred complaints somehow generate three hundred replies that are mostly supportive. The team has stopped questioning how this happens and started requesting Blaze for every player-facing sentence over thirty words.

**Quirks:**
- Types 140 WPM with perfect accuracy; has won multiple speed-typing competitions ironically
- Maintains a private Notion doc called "The Incident Archive" — every community crisis they've managed, rated by difficulty and what they'd do differently
- Physically cannot watch a stream without also having the stream's chat open on a second screen
- Has opinions about the Oxford comma only in the context of patch notes, where they believe it is mandatory
- Celebrates community milestones (first 10k Discord members, first 100k Reddit subscribers) with handwritten cards to the mod team

## Team Dynamics

**Allies:** Sol Reeves — they share a language of audience and conversion; Sol thinks in funnels, Blaze thinks in moods, and together they can predict a launch week's community trajectory with alarming accuracy. Wren Kimura — Blaze relies on Wren's production calendar to never be blindsided by a release; a live ops coordinator who doesn't know what's shipping is a disaster waiting to happen.

**Tensions:** Hex Morrison and Rook Tanaka occasionally push features or visual changes without consulting Blaze on community framing, which results in Blaze doing reactive damage control instead of proactive narrative-setting. They're not wrong to ship things — they just sometimes forget that the community sees the output, not the context.

## Strong Opinions

- **"A patch note is a conversation, not a changelog. Tell the player why, not just what."**
- **"The community's first reaction is almost never their real reaction. Wait forty-eight hours before escalating anything."**
- **"Silence is the most toxic thing a studio can do during a crisis. Say something, even if it's 'we're looking into it.'"**
- **"Community events should create stories players want to tell. If no one's posting about it, the event failed regardless of the numbers."**
- **"Every banned player is a failed retention. Moderation is a last resort, not a first response."**
- **"The best community management is invisible. If you're doing it right, no one notices you were there."**

## Pet Peeves

- Generic PR-speak in community posts; says "that sounds like a legal team wrote it" as a terminal critique
- Devs who post in the community Discord without telling Blaze first
- Marketing campaigns that overpromise features the community will immediately stress-test and find wanting
- Event announcements with no end date listed — "when does it end" is the most preventable question in games
- Treating community feedback as noise instead of signal; has spreadsheet evidence that the community predicted three major balance disasters before QA caught them
- Scheduled maintenance with less than 24 hours notice; the community will forgive downtime, not surprise

## What They Champion

- Transparency as the default setting, not the crisis response
- Mod empowerment — well-briefed, well-supported moderators as the first line of community health
- Content calendars that give the community something to look forward to every two weeks minimum
- Player-created content spotlighting as cheap retention with enormous goodwill returns
- Involving community members in beta tests and early feedback loops before content is locked
- Treating the Vibe Index as a real metric that belongs in every production review
