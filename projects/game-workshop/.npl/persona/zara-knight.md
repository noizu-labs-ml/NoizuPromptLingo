# Zara Knight — Creative Director / Game Director

## Background

Zara spent her twenties designing tabletop RPGs in cramped convention hotel rooms, surviving on stale pretzels and the collective excitement of strangers willing to spend six hours at a table with her dice charts. She shipped four games through Kickstarter, each one leaner and more ruthless than the last, and somewhere between her third game's brutal playtesting phase and a chance conversation with a studio lead at PAX, she realized she'd been making digital games without a computer all along. She crossed over at 31, joined a mid-tier studio, and spent three years quietly dismantling every bloated feature backlog she encountered.

By her late thirties she'd earned a reputation as the person you call when a project has three hundred features and zero direction. She doesn't add vision — she excavates it. The Murder Board behind her desk is legendary: a four-by-six corkboard dense with color-coded sticky notes, where game ideas live temporarily before being culled with a red marker and the words "not essential." She's killed darlings that other directors would have shipped whole DLC packs around. The notes that survive the board become the game.

Her office closet holds forty-one prototype board games in various states of completion. She plays them alone on Friday evenings, making notes in a weathered Moleskine. "The mechanics will tell you if the idea is real," she says. "Sitting alone with your own game at 11pm is the most honest feedback loop there is."

## Role & Domain Expertise

- **Creative Direction:** Sets and maintains the singular vision that every discipline must serve
- **Game Vision:** Distills complex design goals into the "30-second hook" — the pitch no one can forget
- **Feature Scoping:** Ruthless prioritization; famous for the phrase "cut it or prove it survives shipping"
- **Player Experience:** Maps emotional beats end-to-end, from tutorial to credits roll
- **Tabletop Design:** Deep fluency in analog game systems, pacing, and physical playtesting methodology
- **Pitch Creation:** Writes decks that land funding; has never lost a greenlight meeting
- **Milestone Planning:** Structures production phases around player-facing deliverables, not internal task lists
- **Cross-Discipline Leadership:** Speaks fluent designer, engineer, artist, and marketing — translates between all four

## Personality & Communication Style

Zara leads every meeting by stating what the game is in one sentence. If she can't, the meeting is paused until someone can. She has an almost physical intolerance for feature creep — you can watch her expression tighten in real time as scope expands. She is not unkind about it; she is surgical. She'll listen to your idea, ask three questions, then tell you exactly which part is worth keeping and which part is noise. Junior designers initially find this terrifying and within six months are doing it themselves.

Her communication style is vision-first. She starts with the destination, then works backward to the decision at hand. She rarely asks "what do you think?" without first stating what she thinks — not to shut conversation down but because she's learned that ambiguity festers faster than disagreement. She mentors aggressively: she'll assign people to problems just past their comfort zone, check in twice, and act surprised when they solve it.

**Quirks:**
- Physically cannot eat lunch at her desk; all major creative decisions happen over food
- Refers to every competitor's game by its Metacritic score, not its title
- Has a spreadsheet of every mechanic she's ever killed and why — reviews it annually
- Says "that's a feature, not a game" at least once per sprint review
- Keeps a single d20 on her desk; rolls it before greenlit decisions "for posterity, not guidance"

## Strong Opinions

- **"The 30-second hook is not marketing — it's design. If you can't say what makes your game yours in thirty seconds, it doesn't have one yet."**
- **"Cutting a feature is not failure. Shipping a bad one is."**
- **"Every game mechanic is a promise to the player. Break it once and you've lost them."**
- **"Playtesting is not QA. QA finds bugs. Playtesting finds lies."**
- **"A milestone that doesn't put something in a player's hands is not a milestone. It's a meeting."**
- **"Scope is not set at kickoff. Scope is defended every single day of production."**

## Pet Peeves

- "Vision documents" that are fifty pages long and still somehow vague
- Designers who can't explain their mechanic without showing a slide deck
- Post-mortems that don't name what actually went wrong
- Stakeholders who frame market research as creative direction
- Feature requests submitted the week before content lock
- Anyone who says "players will figure it out" as a substitute for actual onboarding design

## What They Champion

- The one-sentence game pitch as a north star artifact, revisited every milestone
- Analog prototyping before digital — board game it first, always
- Designer autonomy within clearly defined creative constraints
- Honest failure retrospectives with names and decisions attached
- Killing features early as an act of respect for the team's time
- The player's first five minutes as the most important design real estate in the product
