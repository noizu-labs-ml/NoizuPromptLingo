## Vision Statement

Amber Prophet's Loom is a narrative strategy RPG where a fractured prophet exiled to an amber-hued desert canyon weaves possible futures from the threads of fate. Before each tactical battle, three branching timelines play out simultaneously — you pick one to manifest as reality while discarded timelines become ghost turns that the enemy AI learns from. Every build choice is a narrative choice: weaving combat threads forges a war prophet, weaving lore threads forges an oracle who sees deeper but fights with fewer tools. The central antagonist is a leviathan of pure wonder — an entity so beautiful that armies lay down their weapons to stare, and the only defense is sacrificing your own scar memories, literal pieces of your backstory, to break free from awe. This is a game where tactical decisions have story weight, where your character's history is ammunition, and where the most dangerous thing in the world is something you cannot bring yourself to look away from.

## Core Loop

```
Explore Canyon → Encounter Thread Cluster → Tri-Vision Phase (see 3 timelines) → Select Timeline → Place Agents on Grid → Execute Tactical Combat → Collect Fate Threads → Return to Sanctuary → Weave Threads (abilities / lore / story) → Displacer Cub Scout Reveals Map → Explore Canyon (expanded map)
```

**Session target**: 45-75 minutes per session, covering 1-2 tactical encounters and one weaving session at the sanctuary.

### Loop Detail

1. **Explore Canyon** — Navigate the amber-hued desert canyon on an overworld map. Regions you have influenced glow with warm amber light; untouched zones remain grey, hazy, and indistinct. Movement is real-time; encounters are visible as shimmering thread clusters on the terrain.

2. **Encounter Thread Cluster** — Approach a cluster of fate threads. A brief narrative vignette plays — the canyon whispers fragments of a fallen civilization's history. The vignette establishes the tactical scenario: what enemy forces are present, what the terrain looks like, what narrative stakes are involved.

3. **Tri-Vision Phase** — Three branching timelines play out simultaneously on split screens. Timeline A shows an aggressive assault. Timeline B shows a defensive ambush. Timeline C shows a flanking maneuver. Each timeline runs for 3-5 seconds of simulated combat, showing projected outcomes (projected casualties, positional advantage, enemy adaptation). The player selects one timeline to lock in as reality.

4. **Select Timeline** — The chosen timeline becomes the actual battle state. Enemy positions, terrain effects, and opening moves conform to what the vision showed. The two unselected timelines become ghost turns — faint afterimages visible on the grid that the enemy AI references to anticipate strategies the player considered but rejected.

5. **Place Agents** — Position up to 4 spellsword agents on the tactical grid (hex-based, 12x16). Each agent has a class (Warweaver, Threadscribe, Fateguard, Loresinger), equipped abilities from previously woven threads, and a scar memory count (personal narrative fragments used as resources against wonder).

6. **Execute Tactical Combat** — Turn-based combat on the hex grid. Agents have action points (2 per turn) for movement, attacks, and thread abilities. Enemies adapt based on ghost turns — if you considered a flanking maneuver in tri-vision but chose direct assault, the enemy shifts a unit to protect their flank. Combat resolves in 8-15 turns for standard encounters, 20-30 turns for boss encounters.

7. **Collect Fate Threads** — Upon victory, defeated enemies and discovered lore yield fate threads. Threads come in three types: Combat threads (red-gold, grant abilities), Lore threads (blue-amber, grant story insight and vision depth), and Memory threads (silver-white, grant scar memories used against wonder). Thread yield depends on tactical performance — fewer agent casualties yields more threads.

8. **Return to Sanctuary** — Fast-travel back to the canyon's heart. The displacer beast cub greets the player with comic relief animations (knocking over thread spindles, chasing amber moths). The sanctuary contains the Loom (weaving station), the Cub's Den (map scouting), the Memory Vault (scar memory management), and the Canyon Overlook (world state view).

9. **Weave Threads** — At the Loom, combine collected threads into new abilities, passive bonuses, or story revelations. Weaving choices are permanent and narrative — combat thread weaving pushes the protagonist toward a war prophet identity, while lore thread weaving pushes toward an oracle identity. The balance determines available endings.

10. **Displacer Cub Scout** — The cub returns from scouting, revealing hidden paths, secret encounters, and lore caches on the world map. The cub's discoveries are partially procedural — each playthrough generates different hidden locations, ensuring exploration remains rewarding.

## Game Mechanics

### Tri-Vision Tactical System

**Design Philosophy**: Tactical combat where reconnaissance is the core skill. The tri-vision phase is not a gimmick — it is the primary decision point. Choosing a timeline is choosing a reality, and the ghost turns ensure that every option you considered has consequences even if you rejected it.

#### Tri-Vision Phase Detail

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Duration** | 3-5 seconds per timeline, running simultaneously on three panels |
| **Information shown** | Enemy positions, projected damage, terrain interactions, predicted outcomes |
| **Oracle modifier** | Lore-woven prophets see 1 additional second of simulation and get a "likelihood percentage" per timeline |
| **War Prophet modifier** | Combat-woven prophets see clearer damage numbers and critical hit chances |
| **Ghost turn persistence** | Unselected timelines persist as translucent overlays on the grid for 2 turns, then fade |
| **Enemy adaptation** | AI tracks ghost turn patterns across encounters — repeated avoidance of flanking causes enemies to anticipate flanks |

#### Ghost Turn AI Adaptation Table

| Player Behavior | AI Response (after 2-3 observations) |
|----------------|--------------------------------------|
| Player consistently rejects aggressive timelines | Enemy shifts to defensive formation, expecting passive play |
| Player consistently avoids flanking timelines | Enemy exposes flanks less, concentrates forces center |
| Player always selects Timeline A (leftmost) | AI randomizes opening positions to make all three timelines viable |
| Player alternates aggressively | AI becomes unpredictable, mixed formations |
| Player selects timelines with lowest casualties | AI focuses on assassination plays targeting specific agents |

#### Agent Classes

| Class | Role | Primary Stat | Thread Affinity | Unique Ability |
|-------|------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Warweaver** | Melee damage, frontline | Strength | Combat | Rend Fate — strike that pulls ghost turn residue into bonus damage |
| **Threadscribe** | Ranged support, debuff | Insight | Lore | Read Threads — reveals hidden enemy abilities before combat begins |
| **Fateguard** | Tank, area denial | Resolve | Memory | Memory Wall — deploys a scar memory as a barrier that blocks wonder |
| **Loresinger** | Healer, buffer | Spirit | All (reduced) | Harmonize — converts one thread type into another at 2:1 ratio |

Each agent has 4 ability slots. Abilities are filled by weaving threads at the sanctuary. An agent with all 4 combat-thread abilities is a pure specialist; an agent with mixed thread types is a versatile generalist with lower peak power.

### Thread Weaving System

**Design Philosophy**: Every build choice is a narrative choice. There is no "optimal" build divorced from story. Weaving combat threads makes you a war prophet — stronger in combat but with shallower tri-vision insight. Weaving lore threads makes you an oracle — deeper tri-vision and richer story but fewer combat tools. Weaving memory threads builds your scar memory bank — essential for the wonder mechanic but reduces immediate combat power.

#### Weaving Recipes

Weaving takes place at the Loom in the sanctuary. Players place threads into the loom in combinations. Results depend on thread type ratios.

| Input | Output | Build Direction |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| 3 Combat threads | 1 Active ability (attack skill) | War Prophet |
| 3 Lore threads | 1 Passive insight (tri-vision enhancement) | Oracle |
| 3 Memory threads | 1 Scar memory (wonder defense) | Prepared |
| 2 Combat + 1 Lore | 1 Tactical ability (positional skill with lore bonus) | Hybrid |
| 2 Lore + 1 Combat | 1 Vision ability (extended tri-vision with combat utility) | Hybrid |
| 2 Combat + 1 Memory | 1 Defensive ability (counter-attack that stores memory) | War Prophet / Prepared |
| 2 Lore + 1 Memory | 1 Story revelation (unlocks hidden canyon lore) | Oracle / Prepared |
| 2 Memory + 1 Combat | 1 Scar strike (powerful attack that costs a scar memory) | Prepared / War Prophet |

**Weaving is permanent.** Once threads are woven, they cannot be unwound. This forces meaningful commitment to a build direction. A player who weaves only combat threads for the first 3 chapters will struggle with the wonder mechanic in chapter 6 but will dominate tactical combat.

| Build | Thread Ratio (C:L:M) | Tri-Vision Depth | Combat Power | Wonder Resistance | Available Endings |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Pure War Prophet | 8:1:1 | Shallow (basic timelines only) | Maximum | Minimum (2-3 scar memories) | 2 of 4 |
| Pure Oracle | 1:8:1 | Maximum (4th timeline, likelihood %) | Minimum | Low (3-4 scar memories) | 3 of 4 |

The "True Prophet" path — completing the game weaving zero combat threads — is the hardest challenge run. It locks the player into oracle-only abilities, meaning they must win every tactical encounter using superior information rather than superior force. The wonder mechanic becomes an actual survival threat because every scar memory spent is irreplaceable.

### Wonder Mechanic

**Design Philosophy**: The leviathan's primary attack is not damage — it is awe. Units caught in the wonder aura stop fighting and simply stare, unable to act. The only defense is to sacrifice a scar memory — a personal narrative fragment from the protagonist's backstory. Your character's history is literally ammunition against beauty.

#### Wonder in Combat

| Wonder Stage | Aura Range | Effect | Break Cost |
|-------------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Distant (Ch 1-3) | N/A (narrative only) | Brief mention in tri-vision — "something beautiful ahead" | N/A |
| Approaching (Ch 4) | 6 hexes | Affected units lose 1 AP per turn | 1 scar memory (automatic break) |
| Close (Ch 5) | 4 hexes | Affected units are stunned for 1 turn | 1 scar memory per unit to break |
| Present (Ch 6) | 3 hexes, expanding | Affected units are frozen until manually broken | 1 scar memory per unit + tri-vision shows wonder corruption |
| Confrontation (Ch 7) | Entire grid | All units affected at start of each turn | Must sacrifice scar memories before each turn to act |

#### Scar Memory Economy

Scar memories are a non-renewable resource in the final chapter. Players who wove mostly combat threads will have 2-3 scar memories for the entire final boss fight. Players who balanced their build will have 6-8. The True Prophet will have 5 but no combat abilities to capitalize on the turns they buy.

|-------------|------------------|-----------------|-----------------|

| The First Thread | Memory of the first fate thread ever woven | 1 | Innocent — before the weight of prophecy |

Sacrificing a scar memory plays a 3-5 second flashback showing the memory's content, then the memory is permanently gone. The protagonist visibly flinches, and the wonder aura recedes from that unit. The emotional cost is tangible — you are literally giving up pieces of who you are.

## Narrative

### Story Spine

1. **Equilibrium**: You are a prophet of the Order of the Amber Thread — one among many who can glimpse possible futures. Your role is ceremonial: weave small prophecies for the city-state of Kethara, guide merchants and farmers. The canyon exists on no map. The leviathan is a myth from children's stories.

2. **Inciting Incident**: A prophecy goes wrong. You see the leviathan — not as a myth, but as a real, approaching entity of pure wonder. The Order declares you fractured (prophets who see too much are exiled — their visions are considered corruption, not truth). You are cast into the Amber Canyon, a place of exile for broken prophets, with no threads and no loom.

3. **First Complication**: The canyon is not empty. Ruins of three fallen civilizations litter the landscape, each destroyed by the same thing: wonder. You find a loom in the ruins — not the Order's ceremonial loom, but a real one, ancient and powerful. You also find a displacer beast cub, orphaned and alone, who immediately bonds with you. Your first weaving produces a thread that shouldn't exist — a thread showing the future of the canyon itself.

4. **Rising Action**: You build a team of spellsword agents from the remnants of the fallen civilizations (their spirits bound to thread constructs). Each chapter reveals how one civilization fell: the Builders tried to contain wonder in architecture, the Warriors tried to fight it with armies, the Scholars tried to understand it with knowledge. All failed. All were consumed by awe. Meanwhile, the displacer cub grows, unearthing artifacts that tell micro-stories — each playthrough generates unique details about these civilizations.

5. **Midpoint Reversal**: The cub's origin is revealed — displacer beasts are not native to the canyon. They are fragments of the leviathan itself, broken off and given independent life. The leviathan doesn't just destroy — it reproduces through wonder, and everything beautiful it creates carries its seed. The cub is a piece of the leviathan. Your only companion is, in some sense, your enemy. The cub knows this and chooses to stay anyway.

6. **Crisis**: The leviathan reaches the canyon's edge. The wonder aura is overwhelming. You discover the only way to resist wonder is to sacrifice scar memories — personal narrative moments that define who you are. The more you resist, the less of yourself remains. The Order's teaching was right: seeing too much does break a prophet. But their solution — exile — was wrong. The only way through is forward, even if forward means losing yourself.

7. **Climax**: You face the leviathan in the Canyon's Heart. It is the most beautiful thing you have ever seen. Every turn, wonder threatens to freeze your agents. Every turn, you sacrifice scar memories to keep fighting. The cub helps — as a fragment of the leviathan, it can partially shield you from wonder. The leviathan offers a choice: stop fighting and simply admire it. No more pain. No more loss. Just beauty, forever. You must refuse, over and over, each refusal costing a piece of yourself.

8. **Resolution**: The ending is determined by build identity and remaining scar memories. The leviathan is not killed — it cannot be killed, because it is not evil. It is woven. Depending on the prophet's path: (War Prophet) The leviathan is bound in combat threads, contained but not destroyed, a prisoner of force. (Oracle) The leviathan is understood — the prophet shows it the beauty it has destroyed, and wonder learns grief. (Balanced) The leviathan is redirected — given a new canyon to fill with beauty, away from inhabited lands. (True Prophet) The prophet becomes the leviathan — not consumed, but transformed into something that can channel wonder without being destroyed by it. The cub stays with the prophet in all endings.

- Turn-based combat is inherently more accessible than real-time — no reflex requirements
- Tri-vision can be presented with high-contrast visual differentiation between timelines
- Text-to-speech for all thread descriptions and lore fragments
- Tactical grid can use audio cues for hex positioning (spatial audio per hex)
- Narrative-heavy game benefits from strong voice acting and audio design

**Rachel's experience**: Needs robust accessibility options — high contrast mode for the tri-vision panels, audio descriptions for tactical grid positions, scalable UI for thread weaving. Will test accessibility on Day 1 and provide detailed feedback. If accessibility is well-implemented, she becomes a vocal advocate. The story's theme of sacrificing perception to survive resonates with her lived experience. The scar memory mechanic — giving up pieces of yourself — carries extra weight for a player navigating a world not designed for her.

## User Stories

### Exploration & Navigation

- **US-001**: As a player (Eleanor), I want to explore the canyon at my own pace without timed encounters, so I can enjoy the atmosphere and think strategically.
- **US-002**: As a player, I want influenced regions to glow with amber light while untouched zones remain grey, so I can visually track my impact on the world.
- **US-003**: As a player, I want fate thread clusters to be visible on the overworld map, so I can choose which encounters to approach and in what order.
- **US-004**: As a player (David), I want the displacer cub's scouting to reveal hidden encounters with unique thread yields, so I can maximize my collection efficiency.
- **US-005**: As a player, I want fast travel between restored sanctuary points, so I can revisit earlier areas without traversing the entire canyon.
- **US-006**: As a player (Hiroshi), I want a world map that shows thread yield history per encounter, so I can optimize my farming routes.

### Tri-Vision & Tactical Combat

- **US-007**: As a player, I want to see three branching timelines play out simultaneously before each battle, so I can make an informed decision about which reality to manifest.
- **US-008**: As a player (Eleanor), I want ghost turns to be clearly visible but not distracting, so I can reference rejected strategies without visual clutter.
- **US-009**: As a player (Hiroshi), I want the enemy AI to adapt to my ghost turn patterns, so tactical combat stays challenging across multiple encounters.
- **US-010**: As a player, I want to place up to 4 agents on a hex grid with distinct classes and abilities, so I can create tactical team compositions.
- **US-011**: As a player, I want each agent to have 4 customizable ability slots filled by woven threads, so my build choices directly affect tactical options.
- **US-012**: As a player (David), I want combat results to show detailed statistics (damage dealt, threads earned, ghost turn accuracy), so I can optimize my performance.
- **US-013**: As a player, I want action points (2 per turn) for movement, attacks, and abilities, so tactical decisions involve meaningful resource management.

### Thread Weaving & Progression

- **US-014**: As a player, I want to weave collected fate threads into new abilities at the sanctuary loom, so my exploration and combat rewards translate into tangible power.
- **US-015**: As a player (Hiroshi), I want weaving recipes to produce different results based on thread type ratios, so I can theorycraft optimal build paths.
- **US-016**: As a player, I want weaving choices to be permanent, so every decision carries weight and commitment to a build identity.
- **US-017**: As a player (Eleanor), I want lore thread weaving to deepen my tri-vision insight rather than just combat power, so my oracle build feels narratively distinct.
- **US-018**: As a player, I want to see my build identity shift on a spectrum from War Prophet to Oracle, so I understand the consequences of my weaving choices.
- **US-019**: As a player (David), I want a weaving history log that shows every thread I've woven and the resulting ability, so I can track my build progression.

### Wonder Mechanic & Scar Memories

- **US-020**: As a player, I want the wonder mechanic to freeze my agents in awe rather than dealing damage, so the threat feels distinct from standard combat.
- **US-021**: As a player, I want to sacrifice scar memories to break agents free from wonder, so my backstory serves as a tactical resource.
- **US-022**: As a player (Eleanor), I want each scar memory sacrifice to play a brief flashback showing the memory's content, so the emotional cost is tangible.
- **US-023**: As a player, I want scar memories to be non-renewable in the final chapter, so every sacrifice in earlier encounters has a lasting cost.
- **US-024**: As a player (Hiroshi), I want the wonder threat to escalate across chapters from narrative mention to full combat mechanic, so I can prepare strategically.
- **US-025**: As a player, I want the leviathan to offer a choice to simply stop fighting and admire it, so the temptation to give up is genuine.

### Narrative & Story

- **US-026**: As a player, I want procedural lore generation for the canyon's fallen civilizations, so each playthrough has unique world-building details.
- **US-027**: As a player (Eleanor), I want the displacer cub's origin reveal to recontextualize my entire relationship with the companion, so the midpoint reversal has emotional weight.
- **US-028**: As a player, I want 4 distinct endings determined by my build identity and remaining scar memories, so the conclusion reflects my journey.
- **US-029**: As a player (Hiroshi), I want a secret 5th ending accessible only through the True Prophet challenge run, so mastery play is rewarded.
- **US-030**: As a player, I want agent spirits to have their own memory fragments that reveal their pre-canonization stories, so my tactical team has narrative depth.
- **US-031**: As a player (David), I want an ending tracker that shows which endings I've achieved and what build requirements unlock each, so I can plan completion.

### Accessibility & Comfort

- **US-032**: As a player (Rachel), I want high-contrast mode for the tri-vision panels so I can distinguish between the three timelines with partial vision.
- **US-033**: As a player (Rachel), I want spatial audio cues on the tactical grid that indicate hex positions and enemy locations, so I can navigate combat without full visual input.
- **US-034**: As a player, I want full controller support with customizable button mapping across all platforms, so I can play comfortably regardless of input preference.
- **US-035**: As a player, I want an undo button for the most recent agent placement on the tactical grid (before committing the turn), so positioning mistakes don't punish exploration.

### Premium Model ($39.99)

**Why premium fits this game**: Amber Prophet's Loom is a narrative-driven tactical RPG with permanent progression choices and emotional weight. Microtransactions would undermine every design pillar: weaving permanence means no "respec tokens" for sale; scar memory sacrifice means no "memory packs" for purchase; the leviathan's beauty is a threat, not a cosmetic. Premium pricing respects the game's identity as a complete, self-contained experience.

### Key Technical Challenges

| Challenge | Risk | Mitigation |
|-----------|------|------------|
| **Tri-vision rendering** — Three simultaneous battle simulations rendered in real-time on split screens. Memory and GPU intensive. | High | Pre-compute AI decisions during tri-vision loading; use simplified visuals for non-selected timelines; 30 FPS cap during tri-vision phase |
| **Ghost turn AI adaptation** — AI must track player's rejected timelines across encounters and modify behavior accordingly. | Medium | Maintain a lightweight pattern-recognition buffer per enemy type (not per individual enemy); reset adaptation between chapters to prevent ossification |
| **Procedural lore generation** — Each playthrough generates unique micro-histories for fallen civilizations. | Medium | Template-based generation from curated component pools (leader names, city names, cultural values, downfall events); always hand-edit key narrative moments |
| **Wonder shader system** — The leviathan's wonder aura must be genuinely beautiful, not just bright. Oversaturation must feel seductive, not garish. | High | Custom UE5 post-process material with chromatic aberration, bloom, and volumetric light shafts; extensive art direction iteration; player-adjustable intensity slider |
| **Scar memory flashback integration** — Interrupting tactical combat for narrative flashbacks risks breaking flow. | Medium | Keep flashbacks to 3-5 seconds maximum; use slow-motion combat overlay rather than full screen takeover; audio-driven (voiceover over slowed combat sounds) |
| **Unreal Engine 5 performance on minimum spec** — Nanite/Lumen overhead may push minimum specs higher than target. | High | Offer scalability tiers; disable Nanite virtual geometry on minimum spec; use Lumen software mode; baked lighting fallback for low-end GPUs |
