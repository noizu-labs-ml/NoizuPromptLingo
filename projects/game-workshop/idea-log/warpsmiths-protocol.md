# Warpsmith's Protocol

**Narrative Story / Detective Mystery**

## Core Loop
You are a warpriest investigator dispatched to a mysterious celestial palace where an ancient artifact -- the Protocol -- has been stolen from the sanctum. The palace exists in multiple overlapping time periods simultaneously, and you can shift between them at will. In the past, the palace is pristine and the suspects are alive. In the present, it is a crime scene. In a fractured future, it is a ruin haunted by the consequences of the theft. You interview witnesses across timelines, cross-reference testimonies that span centuries, and piece together who stole the Protocol and why -- knowing that the answer may force you to question whether the theft was a crime or a rescue.

## Features
- **Temporal Cross-Examination**: Question a suspect in the past, then immediately question the same person in the present -- where they may have aged, changed allegiances, or died (in which case you interview their ghost). Contradictions between timelines are your primary investigative tool. A suspect claims they were in the garden at the time of the theft, but in the future timeline their ghost haunts the sanctum. Where does the truth live?
- **Timefold Exploration**: The palace is a physical space you walk through, but each room exists in three temporal states. A locked door in the present might be open in the past. A bloodstain in the future leads you to a hidden passage in the past that explains how it got there. Exploration is a continuous act of temporal reasoning -- the map is three-dimensional in time, not space.
- **Moral Cul-de-sac**: The narrative has no clean resolution. Every suspect has a credible motive, and the stolen Protocol itself is ambiguous -- it could be a weapon, a contract, or a living entity. Your final accusation shapes the palace's future timeline, and the game makes it clear that "correct" and "just" are not always the same thing. Multiple endings are not about choosing a side -- they are about choosing what kind of truth you can live with.

## Why This Is Awesome
**Story-Driven Explorer** will find this is a narrative game that trusts its audience. The writing is dense, layered, and genuinely surprising. The temporal investigation mechanic creates a detective story where the "crime scene" is a living document you can interrogate across centuries. The characters are complex -- not twist-villains, but people caught in a situation where every choice has a cost.

**Challenge Seeker** will engage with the temporal logic puzzles at the narrative's core. Cross-referencing testimonies across timelines requires genuine deductive reasoning, not just clicking every dialogue option. The game tracks your conclusions and challenges you when you leap to assumptions. Getting the "full truth" requires paying attention, taking notes, and thinking critically about what each timeline actually shows you versus what characters claim happened.

**Flow State** players will appreciate the pacing. The palace is compact but dense -- every room has layers. There is no filler, no fetch quests, no padding. You are always investigating, always learning, always piecing something together. The time-shifting mechanic creates a rhythm of observation, hypothesis, and verification that becomes meditative in its own way.
