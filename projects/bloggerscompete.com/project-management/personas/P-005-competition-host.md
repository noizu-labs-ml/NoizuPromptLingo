# Priya Nair — The Community Builder

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 34-48 |
| **Role** | Newsletter creator + online community founder |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Food blogging / culinary content |
| **Location** | UK (London / Manchester area) |

## Bio

Priya runs "Spice Routes Weekly," a popular South Asian food newsletter with 22,000 subscribers, and moderates a 4,000-member Discord community for South Asian food bloggers. She's known for curating quality content, hosting virtual cookbook swaps, and giving smaller creators a platform. She's been looking for a structured way to run her annual "Best New Food Blog" competition, which she currently manages via a painful Google Form + committee voting spreadsheet.

## Goals

1. Host credible, automated competitions for her community without managing the judging logistics herself
2. Give her community members a prestigious, legitimizing recognition that feels earned, not arbitrary
3. Increase Discord engagement and newsletter content by integrating competition events into her editorial calendar

## Frustrations

1. Her current competition process takes 40+ hours of manual work across nominations, scoring, and announcing
2. Community members complain that voting competitions favor whoever has the biggest following, not the best content
3. She's turned down 3 sponsorship deals for her annual competition because she couldn't offer the sponsor credible reach/legitimacy metrics

## Behaviors

- Publishes her newsletter every Thursday; spends Fridays on community management
- Uses Beehiiv for her newsletter, Discord for community, and Notion for editorial planning
- Actively scouts new food bloggers to feature; maintains a personal list of ~200 blogs she follows
- Has run two previous competitions manually; the second one caused a community drama incident she's still recovering from

## Job to Be Done

> "When I want to celebrate the best bloggers in my niche, I want to run a structured, AI-judged competition that feels fair and credible, so my community trusts the results and sponsors take the event seriously."

## Relationship to Product

Priya would discover BloggersCompete via another newsletter creator or a podcast about online community building. She'd sign up for Pro specifically for the competition hosting feature, and the $12/mo price is trivial relative to the hours she saves. She'd configure a custom "Best South Asian Food Blog" competition, share it via her newsletter, and drive 60-80 entries from her community. The white-label or custom branding options would matter a lot for her — she wants it to feel like "her" competition, not a BloggersCompete competition. Churn risk: if the competition customization is too rigid or if she can't export winner data for her newsletter content.

## Scenarios

1. **Launching the Annual Competition** — Priya creates a "Best South Asian Food Blog 2025" competition on the platform, sets a 3-week submission window, and promotes it in her newsletter and Discord. She watches entries roll in without touching a spreadsheet.
2. **Sponsor Pitch Deck** — Priya approaches a specialty spice brand for competition sponsorship, using BloggersCompete's competition analytics (entry count, aggregate audience reach of participants, score distributions) to make the case for a £2,500 sponsorship.
