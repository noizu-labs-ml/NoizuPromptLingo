# Rachel Torres — The Team Lead

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 32-45 |
| **Role** | Content Marketing Manager |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | SaaS / B2B Marketing |
| **Location** | Major metro (Chicago, NYC, SF) or fully remote |

## Bio

Rachel manages the content team at a 60-person SaaS company, overseeing two content writers, an SEO specialist, and the company blog that drives ~35% of their inbound pipeline. She reports to the CMO and is constantly being asked to prove content ROI. She knows her team is producing quality work, but "quality" is hard to defend in a spreadsheet. She's always looking for tools that can translate creative output into business metrics.

## Goals

1. Demonstrate to leadership that the company blog is a top-tier asset compared to competitors
2. Give her writers objective feedback loops so she doesn't have to be the sole quality arbiter
3. Track improvement trends across all 6 quality dimensions over quarterly cycles

## Frustrations

1. Her current content audit process is manual, inconsistent, and depends entirely on her subjective judgment
2. Leadership keeps asking "how do we compare to [competitor blog]?" and she has no credible answer
3. Her writers take feedback personally — AI-scored dimensions give her a neutral third party to reference

## Behaviors

- Runs bi-weekly content reviews with her team using a mix of Google Analytics, Ahrefs, and a homegrown rubric doc
- Attends Content Marketing World and follows CMI, Animalz, and Siege Media
- Manages an editorial calendar in Notion; tracks content performance in a shared Looker dashboard
- Evaluates tools on a 30-day trial basis with a formal scorecard before pitching to finance

## Job to Be Done

> "When my team ships content, I want a consistent, objective quality score across multiple dimensions, so I can coach writers with data instead of opinions and prove content quality to my CMO."

## Relationship to Product

Rachel would discover BloggersCompete via a LinkedIn ad or a Content Marketing Institute article. She'd start with a Pro trial to evaluate multi-blog support and analytics depth. The Team plan at $29/mo is a no-brainer expense she can justify in a single Slack message to her CMO — it's less than one hour of her time. The white-label feature would matter if she ever wants to run partner content competitions. She'd churn if the API access is too limited to pull data into her existing reporting stack.

## Scenarios

1. **Monthly Quality Review** — Rachel generates a dimension scorecard for the last 8 posts published by her team, uses the Writing Quality and SEO scores to run a 30-minute team retrospective, and assigns specific improvement focuses to each writer.
2. **Competitor Gap Analysis** — Rachel submits three competitor SaaS company blogs to compare against theirs, discovers they're lagging on Visual Design, and uses this to justify a $2K budget for custom illustration assets.
