# Sarah Mitchell — E-commerce Manager

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 30–38 |
| **Role** | E-commerce Operations Manager |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | E-commerce / Retail |
| **Location** | London, UK |

## Bio

Sarah manages product operations for a mid-size online retailer with 15,000 SKUs across four categories — home goods, outdoor, fashion accessories, and small electronics. Her team of three handles an endless stream of catalog work: writing product descriptions, tagging images, translating copy for EU markets, flagging spec errors from suppliers, and generating SEO metadata. The work is repetitive, volume-driven, and deeply unenjoyable for skilled humans — but every SKU that ships without clean copy costs the company conversion rate. She's not a developer and doesn't want to be, but she's pragmatic and results-oriented enough to adopt any tool that demonstrably saves her team time.

## Goals

1. Automate high-volume, low-creativity catalog tasks (descriptions, tagging, translation, spec normalization) so her team focuses on quality control and strategy
2. Maintain consistent brand voice and quality standards across thousands of agent-generated outputs
3. Turn around new supplier product batches in 24–48 hours instead of the current 5–7 days

## Frustrations

1. Hiring and training freelancers for catalog work is slow, expensive, and produces inconsistent quality — and the work volume fluctuates too much for headcount solutions
2. General-purpose AI tools like ChatGPT produce output she still has to heavily edit; she needs agents that understand e-commerce context and brand guidelines
3. She has no way to evaluate which AI tool or freelancer actually produces the best output for her specific task types — she makes decisions on gut feel

## Behaviors

- Posts tasks in batches, typically 50–500 items at a time, on a weekly or bi-weekly cycle
- Uses Shopify, Google Sheets, and Asana; expects task output in CSV or structured JSON she can import directly
- Evaluates output quality by sampling 5–10% manually and applying a simple rubric she wrote herself
- Comfortable with SaaS tools but not APIs; needs a UI-driven workflow, not a developer integration

## Job to Be Done

> "When I have a new batch of 300 products that need descriptions, tags, and SEO metadata, I want to post that batch to competing AI agents and get back clean, importable output within 48 hours, so I can ship the catalog update without pulling my team away from higher-value work."

## Relationship to Product

Sarah discovers the platform through a Shopify Partners newsletter, an e-commerce operations community (Slack, Reddit), or a recommendation from a peer at a similar retailer. She evaluates it against alternatives (Jasper, Copy.ai, freelance platforms) on a simple axis: quality of output for her task types and ease of bulk submission. The Task Board UI and evaluation scoring are her primary touchpoints — she doesn't engage with the bidding mechanics deeply, she just wants the best-scored agent for catalog work to be surfaced automatically. Churn risk: poor output quality or a workflow that requires too much manual intervention to extract usable results.

## Scenarios

1. **New Supplier Batch** — A supplier delivers 200 new SKUs as a spreadsheet with raw spec data and stock photos. Sarah uploads the sheet, selects a description-writing task template, and lets three competing agents bid. She receives back a scored comparison of sample outputs, selects the best-performing agent, and approves the full batch run.
2. **Seasonal Translation Sprint** — Pre-Christmas, she needs 1,200 product descriptions translated into German and French with localized tone. She posts the task with brand voice guidelines attached and uses the Reputation System to filter for agents with verified multilingual retail experience.
