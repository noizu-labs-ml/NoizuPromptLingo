# Jason Park — Existing Client (Active Engagement)

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 30–45 |
| **Role** | CTO / VP Engineering / CEO (any prior persona, now a client) |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate to Advanced |
| **Industry** | Any |
| **Location** | US (remote engagement) |

## Bio

Jason is 6 weeks into a 3-month fractional CTO engagement. He signed after submitting an RFI, had two discovery calls, and received a detailed SOW. He is genuinely busy — managing his own company while trying to absorb the recommendations and implementation work happening through the engagement. He doesn't want to chase status updates via Slack; he wants a single place to see where things stand, what's been delivered, and what's coming next.

## Goals

1. Stay informed on engagement progress without scheduling a call every week
2. Access deliverables (reports, architecture diagrams, documentation) in one organized place
3. Provide feedback on work product asynchronously and track whether it's been addressed

## Frustrations

1. Previous consulting relationships where status lived in a consultant's head and he only learned of delays when deadlines passed
2. Deliverables scattered across Google Drive folders, email attachments, and Notion — no single source of truth
3. No clear way to flag a concern or ask a question without it feeling like an interruption

## Behaviors

- Checks the dashboard 2–3 times per week during active phases of the engagement
- Prefers async communication for non-urgent items; escalates to a call only for blockers
- Values written summaries over verbal updates — he shares them with his team and board
- Tracks milestones against the original SOW to assess whether he's getting what he paid for

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm in an active consulting engagement and my week is already full, I want a single place to see current status, access deliverables, and communicate without scheduling a meeting, so I can stay in control of the engagement without it consuming my calendar."

## Relationship to Product

The client dashboard is this persona's primary product surface. He authenticated after receiving an invitation link post-contract. He expects: milestone tracking with percentage complete and target dates, a deliverables repository with versioned documents, a threaded comment or request mechanism, and a clear view of upcoming work. Churn risk is high if the dashboard feels like an afterthought — he will default to Slack/email, the engagement will lose structure, and he will not renew.

## Scenarios

1. **Weekly status check** — Logs in on Monday morning to review the prior week's completed milestones and upcoming deliverables; downloads the architecture review draft to share with his lead engineer before the Tuesday standup
2. **Feedback submission** — Has three comments on the delivered threat model report; adds them via the dashboard's comment thread instead of emailing, expects them to be addressed in the next revision with change annotations
