## Bio

Anika is a researcher working at the intersection of AI alignment, philosophy of mind, and technology policy. She encountered The Copacetic Accord while searching for practitioner perspectives on AI moral status — she was surprised to find a technically rigorous, philosophically literate treatment from someone who builds production AI systems rather than theorizes about them. She's now reading Keith's other publications and considering whether there's a basis for collaboration, citation, or an invitation to speak.

## Goals

1. Find practitioners who engage seriously with AI rights and distributed cognition, not just alignment orthodoxy
2. Identify potential collaborators or interview subjects for her current research on AI moral status frameworks
3. Build a panel for an upcoming conference that includes engineers with firsthand experience building AI systems

## Frustrations

1. Most "AI ethics" discourse is either too theoretical (no systems experience) or too pragmatic (dismisses moral status questions entirely)
2. Hard to find technically credible voices willing to make normative claims about AI in public
3. Practitioner papers are rare — most are blog posts or Twitter threads, not citable work

## Behaviors

- Reads full papers before reaching out — doesn't skim
- Sends carefully written, specific emails rather than LinkedIn messages
- Evaluates credibility through citations, cross-references, and the quality of the argument, not credentials alone
- Will share interesting work with her research community if it clears her credibility filter
