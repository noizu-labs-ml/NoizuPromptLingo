# Market Intelligence

Identify, validate, and score underserved niches and audiences across any monetization stream.

## Overview

The Market Intelligence skill provides the research and validation framework to identify profitable niches before you invest time and money building products. It works across all three income streams (AI Templates, Content Publishing, POD) to help you answer the critical first question: *"Is there actually a market for this?"*

**Core Purpose:**
- Discover underserved niches with passionate audiences
- Validate demand signals before building
- Profile target audiences (demographics, psychographics, pain points)
- Analyze competition and identify gaps
- Score opportunities to prioritize your efforts
- Understand audience size and willingness to pay

## When to Use This Skill

- **Before building anything** — Validate your idea has a real market
- **To choose between ideas** — Score multiple opportunities and pick the highest-potential
- **To find your first niche** — Systematic discovery of underserved opportunities
- **To understand your audience** — Build detailed customer persona beyond assumptions

## Core Concepts

### What Makes a Good Niche?

A niche should have:

1. **Defined Audience** — Specific group with clear identity/community, not "everyone interested in X"
2. **Real Pain Points** — Specific problems with time or money at stake; current solutions inadequate
3. **Willingness to Pay** — Evidence they spend money on solutions (products on Gumroad, similar offerings exist)
4. **Accessibility** — You can reach them online, limited competition or clear gaps, you have an edge

### Niche Scoring Framework

| Criterion | Weight | Scoring |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| **Market Size** | 15% | 1=tiny, 10=large and growing |
| **Pain Severity** | 20% | 1=nice-to-have, 10=critical daily frustration |
| **Willingness to Pay** | 20% | 1=no evidence, 10=proven buyers spending $100+ |
| **Competition Level** | 15% | 1=red ocean, 10=blue ocean/no direct competitors |
| **AI/Product Fit** | 15% | 1=poor fit, 10=perfectly suited to stream |
| **Your Advantage** | 15% | 1=no knowledge, 10=deep expertise/unique insight |

**Threshold:** Score 7.0+ = pursue; 5-7 = monitor; <5 = pass

## Research Process

### Phase 1: Discovery

**Goal:** Generate 10-20 potential niches to explore.

**Sources:** Reddit niche subreddits, Hacker News comments, Twitter/X discussions, Facebook Groups, Indie Hackers, ProductHunt launches, YouTube search trends.

**Key questions:** What problems does this community complain about? What solutions exist? What gaps remain? Would they pay for better?

> *For a structured discovery prompt with 5 frameworks, see [references/niche-discovery.md](references/niche-discovery.md)*

### Phase 2: Initial Screening

**Goal:** Filter 10-20 ideas down to 5-7 serious contenders.

**Quick Filter:**
- [ ] Audience size: 10K+ people interested
- [ ] Willingness to pay: Evidence of similar products sold
- [ ] AI fit: Problem suitable for templates, content, or design
- [ ] Your advantage: You understand this space or can learn quickly
- [ ] Accessibility: You can reach this audience

**Pass if:** Audience too small, no proof of willingness to pay, or no competitive advantage.

### Phase 3: Deep Dive Research

**Goal:** Thoroughly understand audience, pain points, competition.

For each promising niche:

1. **Audience Profiling** — Demographics, psychographics, professional context, online behavior
2. **Pain Point Mapping** — List 10+ specific problems, rank by severity and frequency, identify underserved ones
3. **Competition Analysis** — Find 5-10 existing solutions, note price/features/quality/satisfaction, find gaps
4. **Willingness to Pay** — Find products already selling to this niche, what they charge, monetization models

> *For deep research templates (audience, competition, demand, purchase intent), see [references/niche-research-templates.md](references/niche-research-templates.md)*

### Phase 4: Validation

**Goal:** Confirm demand signals before building.

- [ ] Search volume exists (Google Trends shows interest)
- [ ] Community activity (subreddits/forums have recent posts)
- [ ] Existing solutions (competing products available)
- [ ] Price point validated (similar products sell at expected price)
- [ ] Customer pain confirmed (people explicitly asking for solutions)

> *For keyword-based validation methodology, see [references/keyword-research.md](references/keyword-research.md)*

### Phase 5: Scoring & Prioritization

**Goal:** Rank opportunities by weighted score.

Apply the scoring framework from Phase 1. Each dimension 1-10, multiply by weight, sum to total (max 10.0).

```
Example:
Market Size: 8 x 0.15 = 1.2
Pain Severity: 9 x 0.20 = 1.8
Willingness to Pay: 8 x 0.20 = 1.6
Competition: 7 x 0.15 = 1.05
AI Fit: 8 x 0.15 = 1.2
My Advantage: 7 x 0.15 = 1.05
TOTAL: 7.9 -> Pursue
```

## Validation Signal Hierarchy

**Strong Signals (High Confidence):**
- Existing profitable products in space
- Large, active community discussing pain points
- High search volume for problem-related keywords
- People explicitly asking "how do I..."

**Medium Signals (Moderate Confidence):**
- Growing interest (Google Trends upward)
- Some existing solutions
- Moderate community size

**Weak Signals (Low Confidence):**
- Assumption-based ("I think people want...")
- Tiny communities
- No existing solutions (might mean no demand)
- Declining search interest

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| No audience size check | Too small to profit | Verify communities exist + size |
| Assuming vs. validating | Your assumptions are wrong | Prove demand with research |
| Ignoring competition | Already solved well | Analyze what exists + gaps |
| Too broad niche | Too much competition | Get specific (not "productivity") |
| Underestimating effort | Harder than expected | Validate willingness to pay |
| No advantage | Anyone can build it | What makes YOUR version better? |

## Audience Profiling Template

For your chosen niche, develop a complete profile covering:

- **Demographics** — Age, income, education, geography, profession
- **Psychographics** — Values, identity, aspirations, fears, frustrations
- **Online Behavior** — Platforms, communities, influencers, content preferences
- **Pain Points** — 10+ specific problems ranked by severity and frequency
- **Purchasing Patterns** — Current solutions, annual spend, buying process, purchase influences

## Competitive Gap Analysis

1. **Direct Competitors** — Top 5 products at same format/price. What do customers love? Complain about? What's missing?
2. **Indirect Competitors** — Free resources, courses, services, SaaS tools serving the same need differently
3. **Gaps** — Format gaps, quality gaps, audience gaps, price gaps
4. **Your Differentiation** — What can you do better, what unique insight do you have, why would customers choose you?
