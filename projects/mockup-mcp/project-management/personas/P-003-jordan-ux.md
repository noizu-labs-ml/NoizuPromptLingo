# Jordan Park — UX Designer

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 26–34 |
| **Role** | UX / Product Designer |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Tech (Mid-size Product Company) |
| **Location** | US / Canada / Remote |

## Bio
Jordan is the sole designer at a 60-person product company, supporting three product teams simultaneously. They live in Figma but are fluent enough with code to inspect components and write basic HTML/CSS. The hardest part of Jordan's job isn't the polished high-fidelity work — it's the relentless volume of early-stage requests: "Can you just sketch out how this might work?" Before they can open Figma, they need to think, and AI-assisted rough drafts let them think faster.

## Goals
1. Rapidly generate rough wireframe options during discovery so the team can react to something concrete before investing in high-fidelity work
2. Explore multiple layout directions quickly without committing hours to each one
3. Collect annotated feedback from non-design stakeholders on low-fidelity mockups before moving to Figma

## Frustrations
1. Every PM and engineer has a different mental model of what "wireframe" means — generating one quickly from a shared description reduces that ambiguity
2. Low-fidelity work is often dismissed as "too rough" by stakeholders who can't mentally fill the gaps — a slightly more rendered AI output bridges that gap
3. Feedback from non-designers is hard to interpret when it comes back as vague Slack messages rather than annotated comments on the actual design

## Behaviors
- Uses AI to generate starting points, then refines in Figma — never treats AI output as final
- Generates 3–4 layout variants from the same prompt and presents options to the team
- Embeds mockup images or links in Notion design briefs and review decks
- Comfortable invoking MCP tools from a terminal or Claude Code, but primarily wants a clean web interface or Figma plugin

## Job to Be Done
> "When a PM drops a vague feature request on me, I want to generate multiple rough layout options from the brief in under 10 minutes, so I can present real options to the team and make the feedback session productive instead of speculative."

## Relationship to Product
Jordan discovers Mockup MCP through designer communities (Slack workspaces, Designer Hangout, Twitter/X design threads) or through a developer colleague who already has it configured. They adopt it as a speed layer before Figma, not a replacement for it. Key features: quality of SVG/wireframe output, ability to specify component-level constraints (e.g., "a card list with a filter sidebar"), and the feedback-collection link for sharing with non-Figma stakeholders. Churn happens if the output quality is too far from "designer-grade rough" or if there's no way to export to Figma-compatible formats.

## Scenarios
1. **Discovery sprint** — The team has 48 hours to present three UX directions for a redesigned dashboard. Jordan prompts Mockup MCP with the feature brief three times with different layout constraints, gets three wireframe variants, imports them into a Figma frame as reference layers, and uses them as the starting point for the sprint presentation.
2. **Async stakeholder review** — Jordan generates a mobile checkout flow wireframe, sends a feedback link to the head of sales and two customer success reps who have never opened Figma. They leave timestamped annotations. Jordan synthesizes the notes in 30 minutes and starts the refined version.
