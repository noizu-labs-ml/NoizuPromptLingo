# Maya Okonkwo — Product Manager

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 30–40 |
| **Role** | Senior Product Manager |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | B2B SaaS |
| **Location** | US East Coast / Hybrid |

## Bio
Maya owns the roadmap for a suite of B2B analytics tools and spends most of her week translating business requirements into engineering tickets, running discovery sessions, and collecting feedback from customers and internal stakeholders. She can read a database schema and write a basic SQL query but does not code. Her currency is clarity — she needs to get engineering, design, sales, and executives aligned on the same vision before a sprint begins.

## Goals
1. Quickly generate visual representations of feature ideas to communicate intent to engineering without ambiguity
2. Collect structured feedback from stakeholders (executives, customers, sales) on mockups before committing to development
3. Reduce the lag between "idea" and "reviewed mockup" from days (design queue) to hours

## Frustrations
1. Design team bandwidth is a constant bottleneck — even a quick wireframe takes 2–3 days to get back from a queued designer
2. Text-based specs (PRDs, Confluence pages) leave too much room for misinterpretation; engineers build the wrong thing
3. Stakeholder feedback is scattered across Slack threads, email, and Loom comments — hard to synthesize into actionable decisions

## Behaviors
- Uses Notion or Confluence for documentation; pastes mockup images or embeds links inline
- Shares mockup feedback links via email or Slack and expects non-technical stakeholders to comment without signing up for another tool
- Iterates on a mockup 2–3 times based on feedback before handing off to engineering
- Uses AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude.ai web) for drafting PRDs and specs but not for visual generation yet

## Job to Be Done
> "When I have a feature idea that needs stakeholder sign-off, I want to generate a mockup from my spec in minutes, so I can collect feedback and get alignment before the design team is even in the loop."

## Relationship to Product
Maya likely discovers Mockup MCP through securamcp.com directly (not through a developer tool) — possibly via a Product Hunt launch, a PM newsletter, or a colleague recommendation. She uses the companion website heavily: generating mockups via a chat interface and sharing feedback-collection links with stakeholders who never install anything. The key features are the web interface (she won't configure an MCP server herself) and the feedback-collection layer. Churn happens if stakeholders find the sharing/feedback UX confusing or if the mockup fidelity is too low to replace a designer sketch.

## Scenarios
1. **Pre-sprint alignment** — Maya is preparing the next sprint and has a new onboarding flow idea. She describes it in plain English to the Mockup MCP web interface, generates a three-screen wireframe, embeds the feedback link in a Notion page, and sends it to three stakeholders. By EOD she has annotated comments and can finalize the PRD.
2. **Executive review** — The CPO wants to see what the new reporting dashboard will look like before greenlighting the quarter's roadmap. Maya generates a high-fidelity wireframe in 15 minutes, shares a view-only link for the executive to comment on, and avoids a 45-minute Figma walkthrough meeting.
