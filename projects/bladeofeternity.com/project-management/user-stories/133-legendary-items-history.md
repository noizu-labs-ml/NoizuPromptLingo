# US-133: Legendary Items with Living History

**Persona:** Jamie — IF enthusiast (26, sighted, narrative quality)
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** Item Framework & Equipment

## Story
As Jamie, I want legendary items to carry a living history that grows with each owner so that finding a famous weapon is a moment of narrative weight — and wielding it means becoming part of its story.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Legendary items (unique named items, one per server) have a persistent `history` record: ordered list of {owner_name, ownership_start, ownership_end, notable_events[]} that accumulates over the life of the game
- [ ] When a player acquires a legendary item, the LLM generates a new history entry acknowledging the transfer: "Thornbane passes from the cooling hands of Sera Ashveil to yours — the blade hums softly, as if weighing your worth."
- [ ] Notable events are automatically recorded: when the item is used to land a killing blow on a named boss, when it achieves a first-on-server kill, when it is used in a PvP duel, when it is repaired by a master smith
- [ ] The `examine` command on a legendary item renders the full history as prose, LLM-generated from the structured history data: "Three warriors have carried Thornbane..."
- [ ] History prose is paginated for screen reader users: `examine thornbane more` advances through additional history sections without re-reading the entire entry
- [ ] At server launch, legendary items have a seed history (1–3 previous fictitious owners generated by LLM at world-generation time) giving them a sense of pre-existing legend
- [ ] If a legendary item is destroyed (durability reaches 0 and shatters), a final epitaph is appended to its history and the item enters a "broken relic" state — it cannot be fully repaired, but its history remains examinable
- [ ] Legendary item locations are tracked server-wide; a public `lore` command shows which legendary items exist and their last known region (not precise location)

## Notes
The history data structure must be stable and backwards-compatible: as the game runs for months or years, history entries accumulate and must serialize cleanly. Consider a versioned JSON schema for history entries so that new event types can be added without breaking existing records.

The "broken relic" state for destroyed legendary items is a significant design choice: it means legendary items can permanently leave the economy if their owner is reckless. This creates scarcity pressure that increases over the server's life. Admins may want the ability to restore a broken relic via a special repair quest — but this should be documented and rare.

The seed history at world generation (AC-6) is crucial for Jamie's experience: the item should feel old and storied even when the first player finds it. The seed history should be thematically consistent with the item's origin zone.

For the `lore` public tracking command (AC-8): last known region should update when the item's owner moves zones, but with a deliberate lag (15 minutes) to prevent real-time tracking for PvP purposes. This is both a gameplay balance decision and a player privacy concern.

This system has a significant LLM token budget: generating history prose for a legendary item with 20 previous owners is expensive. Cache the generated prose and invalidate only when a new history entry is appended. TTL-based cache expiry is not appropriate here — invalidate on write.
