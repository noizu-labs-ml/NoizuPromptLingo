# US-164: Sound Propagation & Eavesdropping

**Persona:** Jamie — IF enthusiast (26, sighted, narrative quality)
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** Mutable World & Environment

## Story
As Jamie, I want sounds to travel realistically through the room graph — hearing combat two rooms away muffled through stone walls, catching a fragment of conversation from an adjacent room, detecting a creature by the sound of its movement before I see it — so that spatial awareness is earned through attention and the world feels acoustically alive.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] A sound propagation model computes attenuation per passage: stone wall (high attenuation), open doorway (low attenuation), corridor (directional amplification), closed wooden door (moderate attenuation); each passage carries an `acoustic_modifier` value
- [ ] Significant sounds (combat, explosions, conversations, creature movement, environmental events) emit from their source room and propagate to adjacent rooms at reduced volume/fidelity, then to rooms beyond at further reduced fidelity (configurable falloff)
- [ ] Players in adjacent rooms receive ARIA polite announcements of distant sounds with directional cue and appropriate muffling: "From the south — beyond the heavy door — you hear the crash of metal on stone and someone shouting."
- [ ] Eavesdropping mechanic: `listen [direction]` command focuses attention on sounds from that direction; if sound is present, the player receives a higher-fidelity fragment: "Pressing your ear to the door, you catch fragments: '...the shipment...' and '...north gate by midnight...'"
- [ ] Creatures generate characteristic sounds during movement, combat, and idle states; experienced players can identify creature type by sound alone: "Something large is moving in the room to your east — the scrape of claws on stone and a low, wet breathing"
- [ ] Sound propagation considers room topology: sounds travel further along corridors than through rooms with soft furnishings; water-filled rooms muffle sound; caves amplify via echo
- [ ] Stealth system integration: moving quietly reduces sound emission; running amplifies it; sound generated by a character affects NPC awareness in adjacent rooms
- [ ] Combat participants receive ambient sound from adjacent rooms as ARIA polite announcements — aware that reinforcements may be coming without breaking combat narration flow

## Notes
Jamie's interest is narrative quality — sound propagation is one of the most powerful tools for creating a sense of a living world that extends beyond the player's immediate location. The sound of a distant battle implies consequence; a fragment of overheard conversation plants intrigue; the approach of something unseen builds tension. These are classic literary effects that the sound system should reliably produce.

The eavesdropping mechanic is particularly rich for interactive fiction: the player leans into a door and catches fragments. This implies that conversations (LLM-generated, between NPCs) are happening in real time in adjacent rooms and that the player can intercept them through environmental attention. The LLM must generate NPC conversations that reward eavesdropping — containing genuine information, plot hooks, or dramatic content.

Acoustic modeling should be simple but physically consistent. The key parameters are: source volume (loud = farther propagation), passage attenuation (each passage type has a standard attenuation value), and room acoustic modifier (cave vs. carpeted study). A recursive propagation algorithm with a volume floor cutoff (sounds below audibility threshold don't propagate further) is sufficient and efficient.

The integration with stealth creates interesting tactical options: listen from adjacent room before entering, identify occupants by sound, choose moment to enter or avoid. This rewards patient, attentive play over rushing — a gameplay style that Jamie and Lena both appreciate. Marcus's PvP application: listening for enemy approach, timing ambushes by sound.
