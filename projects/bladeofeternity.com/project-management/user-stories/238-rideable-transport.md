# US-238: Rideable Transport

**Persona:** Tyler — MMO refugee, sighted, growth/clans focused
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** World Depth & Exploration

## Story
As Tyler, I want to book passage on ships and join merchant caravans for long-distance travel so that journeys between distant regions feel like an adventure with their own events and atmosphere rather than a fast-travel menu selection.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Transport types: sailing ship (coastal and ocean routes), river barge (river corridor routes), merchant caravan (overland routes), and ferry (short river/bay crossings)
- [ ] Each transport type has its own ambient narration vocabulary: ship (creak of hull, sail wind, wave spray, gulls, distance to horizon described through sound), caravan (wagon creak, horse clop, road dust, fellow travelers' conversation snippets)
- [ ] Transport journey narrated as episodic vignettes over the travel period: 3–5 narrative beats per journey including ambient description and optional event trigger
- [ ] Events during transport: random encounter appropriate to transport type (pirates attacking a ship, bandits ambushing a caravan), NPC traveler dialogue interaction, discovery (spotted landmark from sea, heard news from a passing traveler), weather event
- [ ] Booking transport accessible via NPC dialogue menu at ports and caravan depots; cost in gold, travel time in game hours, and destination stated before confirmation
- [ ] Player can rest or interact with fellow travelers during transport; rest in transit provides partial rest benefits; NPC travelers have dialogue and may offer quests or information
- [ ] Ship combat: if pirates attack, transport combat sequence begins with player participating on the ship deck; naval boarding mechanics differ from standard combat
- [ ] Transport arrival narrated with destination-appropriate atmosphere: arriving by sea differs from caravan arrival, each with distinct sensory framing

## Notes
Tyler wants the full MMO experience including the social experience of shared transport. Ships and caravans are naturally social spaces — other player characters and named NPCs sharing the journey creates encounter opportunities. The episodic vignette format (3–5 beats per journey) prevents transport from being either a boring wait or an overwhelming stream of events. The events during transport (pirate attack, caravan ambush) are the signature moments: unexpected excitement transforms transport from a logistics system into a content category. Ship combat with boarding mechanics is a genuinely distinct combat type — fighting on a moving ship deck with the physics engine modeling the ship's motion would be a standout feature. Booking via NPC dialogue (not a transport menu UI) maintains the fiction-first approach: Tyler talks to the harbormaster, not a travel kiosk. Tyler's clan-building instinct means he'll want to charter entire ships for his guild — the transport system should support group bookings. The rest-in-transit benefit (partial rest) makes long sea voyages valuable beyond just moving between locations: Tyler can recover stamina during a voyage he had to take anyway. Arrival narration should be one of the LLM's showcase moments — arriving by sea at a major city deserves a proper description of the harbor coming into view through sound and smell.
