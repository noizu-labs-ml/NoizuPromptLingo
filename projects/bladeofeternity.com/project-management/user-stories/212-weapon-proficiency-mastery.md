# US-212: Weapon Proficiency and Mastery

**Persona:** Tyler — MMO refugee, sighted, growth/clans focused
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** Advanced Combat & Tactics

## Story
As Tyler, I want my weapon skill to improve through actual use so that the weapons I've fought with for hundreds of hours feel meaningfully different from one I just picked up, and mastery unlocks unique techniques that define my combat identity.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Each weapon type (sword, axe, spear, bow, staff, etc.) has an independent proficiency track from 0–100 with XP earned per combat use
- [ ] Proficiency milestones at 25/50/75/100 unlock passive bonuses: accuracy improvement, damage increase, miss chance reduction, and at 75+ a unique special attack
- [ ] Proficiency level for equipped weapon shown in status channel: "Longsword: 67/100 (Expert)"
- [ ] Level-up milestone announced in post-combat summary: "Longsword proficiency reached 50 — you fight with the blade like an extension of your arm"
- [ ] Special attacks unlocked at 75+ listed in ability menu with proficiency requirement noted; unavailable specials visible but locked with requirement shown
- [ ] Weapon switching in combat reflects proficiency delta: switching from mastered sword to unfamiliar axe announces the difference: "The unfamiliar weight of the axe throws your rhythm"
- [ ] Proficiency persists across all characters using the same account for that weapon type; visible in character sheet under "Combat History"
- [ ] Proficiency milestone narrations generated by LLM tailored to weapon type: sword mastery narration differs from axe mastery narration in tone and imagery

## Notes
Tyler's MMO background means he expects progression systems to have depth — a weapon mastery track that rewards investment over time is directly in his wheelhouse. The 0–100 scale with named tiers (Novice, Apprentice, Journeyman, Expert, Master) gives him vocabulary to discuss progression with his clan. The special attacks at 75+ are the identity-defining layer: the "Sword Master's Riposte" or "Berserker's Cleave" aren't just bonuses, they're personality. Weapon switching feedback in combat is important for tactile legitimacy — if mastery is real, unfamiliar weapons should feel different. The LLM milestone narration is the emotional payoff for grinding; it should reference the weapon's unique properties. Proficiency visibility in the status channel gives Tyler the at-a-glance information he needs without opening menus. The persistence model (account-wide per weapon type, not per character) is a design decision worth flagging — it may need iteration based on whether players want to reset and experience the learning arc again on alts.
