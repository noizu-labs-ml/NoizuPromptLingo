# Persona 06: Tyler — The MMO Refugee

## Demographics
- **Age:** 22
- **Location:** Columbus, OH
- **Occupation:** Part-time warehouse worker, part-time community college student
- **Vision:** Fully sighted

## Technology Profile
- **Setup:** Gaming laptop (mid-range), mechanical keyboard, Discord always open
- **Browser:** Chrome
- **History:** 6,000 hours across WoW, FFXIV, ESO, RuneScape, Lost Ark
- **Current state:** Burned out on visual MMOs, scrolling Reddit for "something different"

## Goals
- Find an MMO that respects his time — no daily login chores, no FOMO mechanics
- Experience player agency that matters beyond gear score
- Join a community that's smaller and tighter than a 10,000-person Discord server
- Be part of something early — a founding player, not a latecomer

## Frustrations
- Modern MMOs are skinner boxes — do dailies, chase gear, repeat
- Every new MMO looks different but plays identical
- Loot is the only progression — he wants reputation, territory, political influence
- Massive servers feel lonely — 2,000 players and nobody talks

## Behaviors
- Lurks game subreddits looking for "hidden gem" recommendations
- Tries every indie MMO that gets posted to r/MMORPG
- Values clan politics and player-driven stories over scripted raids
- Will tolerate jank if the systems are deep
- Streams to 15 viewers and wants a game weird enough to grow his audience

## Relationship to Blade of Eternity
Tyler is the growth vector beyond the accessibility community. He's not blind, doesn't care about accessibility specifically — he wants a genuinely different MMO experience. The text-only medium is his "something different." Clan wars, player economy, crimes system, and emergent world events map directly to his desire for player agency. The small community size is a feature, not a bug.

## Key Scenario
Tyler joins a clan. Within two weeks, his clan is contesting Mordoon territory against a rival. He's smuggling crafted weapons into the contested district, running crime missions to undermine the rival's economy, and writing battle reports in clan chat. The game's systems are creating stories he actually wants to tell people about.

## Success Metric
Tyler streams Blade of Eternity regularly, his viewer count doubles, and he recruits his Discord group.
