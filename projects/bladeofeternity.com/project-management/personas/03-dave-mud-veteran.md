# Persona 03: Dave — The MUD Veteran

## Demographics
- **Age:** 45
- **Location:** Minneapolis, MN
- **Occupation:** Systems administrator
- **Vision:** Fully sighted

## Technology Profile
- **Setup:** Dual monitor, mechanical keyboard, terminal-centric workflow
- **Browser:** Chrome
- **Current games:** Still plays Achaea (Iron Realms), dabbles in Discworld MUD, tried Torn City
- **History:** Started on TinyMUD in 1991, played every major MUD family (DikuMUD, LPMud, MOO)

## Goals
- Find a modern text game that captures the depth and persistence of classic MUDs
- Experience AI-generated content that breaks out of the "1000 handwritten rooms" ceiling
- Participate in a living economy where his crafting and trading matter
- Reconnect with the social dynamics of guilds/clans in a text environment

## Frustrations
- MUD communities are aging out — his favorite servers have 12 peak-hour players
- Modern "text games" are usually single-player interactive fiction, not persistent worlds
- Browser-based RPGs added graphics and lost the imagination
- He misses the era when game text was *good writing*, not filler

## Behaviors
- Types commands faster than most people type sentences
- Expects a rich command vocabulary (not just "attack" — he wants "attack brute with slingshot aiming for the lantern")
- Will map the entire world in a text file within the first week
- Judges a game by its economy and crafting depth before its combat
- Evangelizes text games to anyone who'll listen

## Relationship to Blade of Eternity
Dave validates the game systems depth — economy, crafting, clans, PvP, housing. He doesn't need accessibility features himself but will appreciate the clean keyboard contract and the fact that the UI doesn't waste screen space on graphics. The AI narrative generation is what excites him most: rooms that change, NPCs that remember, a world that doesn't feel memorized after month two.

## Key Scenario
Dave has played for three months. He returns to Rune's Financial District and notices the NPC merchant has different stock because other players over-farmed iron. He checks the price history, corners the leather market, and messages his clan about the arbitrage opportunity. The AI-generated economy narrative makes this feel organic, not scripted.

## Success Metric
Dave tells his MUD community "this is what we've been waiting for" and recruits 5+ players.
