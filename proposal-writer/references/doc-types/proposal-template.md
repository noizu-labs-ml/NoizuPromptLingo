# Proposal Template

> **How to use this template:** Work through each section in order. Sections marked [REQUIRED] must appear in every proposal. Sections marked [CONDITIONAL] appear only when relevant. Delete all guidance blockquotes before sending.

---

## COVER PAGE [REQUIRED]

```
[Your Company Logo]

PROPOSAL FOR
[Project or Engagement Name]

Prepared for:
[Client Company Name]
[Client Contact Name, Title]

Prepared by:
[Your Company Name]
[Your Name, Title]
[Your Email | Your Phone]

Date: [Month DD, YYYY]
Proposal Reference: [PROP-YYYY-NNN]
Valid Through: [Date — typically 30–60 days]
```

> **Guidance:** The cover page sets tone before a single word is read. Use the client's logo alongside yours only if you have permission. The "Valid Through" date creates soft urgency and protects you from being locked into pricing months later. Proposal Reference numbers let you track versions and refer to specific documents in later conversations.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Misspelling the client contact's name or title. Getting the client company's legal name wrong. Forgetting to update the date when resending a revised version.

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** Write this last, even though it appears first. It is the most-read section — many decision-makers read only this. It must stand alone as a complete answer to: "Why should we hire you, for what, and at what cost?" Keep it to one page. Do not use bullet points. Write in prose. State the problem, your solution, your differentiator, and the investment — in that order.
>
> **Word count:** 200–350 words.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Turning this into a company bio. Starting with "We are pleased to present..." (delete that sentence every time). Burying the price. Using jargon the client did not use themselves.

[Client Company Name] is facing [concise restatement of the core problem in the client's own language]. Without [addressing this], [specific negative consequence] becomes increasingly likely.

[Your Company Name] proposes [one-sentence description of the engagement]. Our approach [key differentiator — what makes your approach distinct from a generic solution]. Based on [N] years working with [relevant domain or client type], we believe this is the right scope at the right time.

This engagement is scoped at [Investment Range or Fixed Price] and is designed to deliver [primary outcome] within [timeframe].

We are confident in this proposal and look forward to discussing it with you.

---

## UNDERSTANDING OF THE PROBLEM [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** This section proves you listened. Restate the client's situation, pain, and goals — in their language, not yours. Reference specifics from your discovery conversations or their RFP. Do not introduce your solution here. The goal is for the client to read this and think "yes, they get it."
>
> **Word count:** 300–600 words.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Copying the RFP verbatim. Making this about your capabilities. Diagnosing problems the client did not mention (you will look presumptuous, not smart). Being so generic it could apply to any client.

### Current Situation

[Describe the client's current state. What systems, processes, or conditions exist today? What is working? What is not?]

### Core Challenge

[Name the central problem or tension. Why is the current situation unsustainable or suboptimal? What is the cost of inaction?]

### Goals and Success Criteria

[Restate what the client wants to achieve. If they gave you specific metrics or outcomes, quote them. If success criteria were not defined, flag this as something to establish together.]

---

## PROPOSED APPROACH / SCOPE OF WORK [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** This is the technical heart of the proposal. Describe what you will do, how you will do it, and why your approach is sound. Organize by phases if the work is sequential. Be specific enough to be credible, but do not give away so much methodology that a client could execute it without you.
>
> **Word count:** 500–1,200 words depending on engagement complexity.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Describing inputs (activities) instead of outputs (results). Using vague language like "work closely with" or "leverage best practices." Failing to explain why you are doing things in a particular order.

### Phase 1: [Phase Name] — [Duration]

[Description of what happens in this phase, who does what, what decisions get made, and what is produced.]

### Phase 2: [Phase Name] — [Duration]

[Description of Phase 2. Continue for as many phases as needed.]

### Our Methodology

[Optional: If your firm has a named methodology or framework, describe it here. Explain how it applies to this engagement specifically — not generically.]

---

## OUT OF SCOPE [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** This section is your legal and commercial protection. List everything a reasonable client might expect to be included but is not. Be explicit. Vague scope is where projects go over budget and relationships break down.
>
> **Word count:** 100–250 words.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Skipping this section. Using "and other items as agreed" as a catch-all — it provides no protection. Being so exhaustive you alarm the client.

The following items are explicitly excluded from this engagement:

- [Specific exclusion 1 — e.g., "Integration with third-party systems not listed in Section 3"]
- [Specific exclusion 2 — e.g., "Training of client staff beyond the two sessions defined in the Deliverables"]
- [Specific exclusion 3 — e.g., "Ongoing support or maintenance after the final delivery date"]
- [Specific exclusion 4]

Any work outside this scope will be addressed via a written Change Order prior to execution.

---

## DELIVERABLES [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** List every concrete thing the client receives. Be specific about format, quantity, and recipient. "Report" is not specific. "12-page executive summary in PDF and editable DOCX format, delivered to [Name]" is specific. This list becomes the acceptance criteria.
>
> **Word count:** Varies — use a table or numbered list.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Mixing deliverables with activities. Listing things you will do instead of things the client will receive. Using "final" without defining what "final" means.

| # | Deliverable | Format | Recipient |
|---|-------------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | [Deliverable name] | [PDF / DOCX / Live system / Workshop] | [Name or role] |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |

## TIMELINE & MILESTONES [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** Show a realistic sequence. Include client dependencies (when you need approvals, access, or feedback) and flag that delays from the client side will shift downstream dates. A simple table is more readable than a full Gantt at the proposal stage.
>
> **Word count:** Table-driven. Add 1–2 sentences of narrative around assumptions.

| Milestone | Target Date | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Project Kickoff | Week 1 | Pending signed agreement |
| [Phase 1 Complete] | Week [N] | Client review period: [N] business days |
| [Phase 2 Complete] | Week [N] | |
| Final Delivery | [Date] | |

Timeline assumes receipt of a signed agreement by [date]. Client-side delays exceeding [N] business days may require a timeline adjustment.

---

## INVESTMENT / PRICING [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** Name this section "Investment," not "Cost." Frame pricing in terms of value delivered. Show the total clearly — do not make the client do math. If you offer tiers or options, present no more than three. Include payment terms.
>
> **Word count:** 100–300 words plus table.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Hiding the price. Providing a range so wide it is meaningless. Forgetting to state payment terms. Failing to specify what currency.

### Option A: [Name] — [Price]

[One sentence describing what this option includes and who it is best for.]

### Option B: [Name] — [Price] [CONDITIONAL]

[Description of second tier.]

## ASSUMPTIONS [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** List every assumption baked into your pricing and timeline. If any assumption turns out to be false, you have grounds to issue a Change Order. This section protects both parties.
>
> **Word count:** 100–200 words.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Making this section too vague. Omitting dependencies on specific client personnel. Not calling out access requirements (systems, data, facilities).

This proposal is based on the following assumptions:

1. [Client company] will assign a dedicated project contact with authority to provide timely approvals.
2. Required access to [systems, data, facilities] will be granted within [N] business days of project kickoff.
3. [Client] feedback on deliverables will be provided within [N] business days.
4. [Specific technical or organizational assumption].
5. No more than [N] rounds of revisions are included per deliverable.
6. Travel is [included / not included]. If on-site work is required, travel expenses will be invoiced at cost.

## ABOUT US / WHY CHOOSE US [CONDITIONAL]

> **Guidance:** Include this section when you are competing or when the client does not know you well. Keep it short. Focus on what differentiates you — not on your history. Every firm claims "experienced team" and "proven process." What do you offer that others do not?
>
> **Word count:** 150–300 words.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Writing a company history instead of a differentiation argument. Listing credentials no one asked about. Being so modest you fail to make a case.

[Your Company Name] was founded on the premise that [founding principle or philosophy relevant to this client's problem].

What distinguishes our approach:

- **[Differentiator 1]:** [Brief explanation tying it to client value]
- **[Differentiator 2]:** [Brief explanation]
- **[Differentiator 3]:** [Brief explanation]

Our team includes [brief description of relevant expertise]. We have [specific number or type of engagements] in [relevant domain].

---

## RELEVANT EXPERIENCE / CASE STUDIES [CONDITIONAL]

> **Guidance:** Include 1–3 case studies closest to the client's situation. Use the Problem / Approach / Outcome format. If you cannot share client names, describe the client by industry and size. Quantify outcomes wherever possible.
>
> **Word count:** 150–300 words per case study.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Using case studies unrelated to the client's industry or problem. Listing capabilities instead of outcomes. Forgetting to get client permission before including them by name.

### Case Study 1: [Industry / Client Type]

**Challenge:** [What problem did the client face?]

**Approach:** [What did you do? In what timeframe?]

**Outcome:** [Quantified result — revenue, cost savings, time saved, risk reduced, etc.]

---

## TERMS & CONDITIONS [CONDITIONAL]

> **Guidance:** Include when this proposal will serve as the agreement (i.e., no separate MSA or SOW). If you have a separate standard agreement, reference it here rather than duplicating. At minimum cover: IP ownership, confidentiality, liability cap, and governing law.
>
> **Word count:** Varies. Link to full terms if lengthy.

By executing this proposal, both parties agree to the following terms:

- **Intellectual Property:** Upon receipt of final payment, [Client] receives [full ownership / a perpetual license] to all deliverables produced under this engagement. [Your Company] retains ownership of pre-existing tools, frameworks, and methodologies.
- **Confidentiality:** Both parties agree to keep confidential any non-public information shared during this engagement.
- **Liability:** [Your Company Name]'s total liability shall not exceed the total fees paid under this proposal.
- **Governing Law:** This agreement shall be governed by the laws of [State/Province/Country].
- **Cancellation:** Either party may terminate this agreement with [N] days written notice. Work completed to date will be invoiced.

---

## NEXT STEPS [REQUIRED]

> **Guidance:** Remove all friction from saying yes. Tell the client exactly what to do to move forward. Include a deadline if appropriate. Offer a call to discuss questions.
>
> **Word count:** 75–150 words.
>
> **Common mistakes:** Ending with "let us know if you have questions" — too passive. Not specifying the mechanism for approval (signature, email confirmation, etc.).

To move forward, please:

1. Review this proposal and note any questions.
2. [Schedule a 30-minute review call / Reply with approval via email / Sign and return the attached agreement].
3. Return a signed copy to [your email] by [date] to secure your project start date of [date].

We are available to discuss any aspect of this proposal. Please contact [Name] at [email] or [phone].

We look forward to working with you.
