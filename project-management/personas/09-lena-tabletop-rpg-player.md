# Persona 09: Lena — The Theater-of-the-Mind Tabletop Player

## Demographics
- **Age:** 38
- **Location:** Denver, CO
- **Occupation:** High school English teacher
- **Vision:** Fully sighted

## Technology Profile
- **Browser:** Chrome on a Chromebook
- **Gaming:** D&D (weekly in-person group), Storium, AI Dungeon, some Dwarf Fortress
- **Comfort level:** Moderate — uses tech daily but doesn't tinker

## Goals
- Find solo RPG experiences with the narrative richness of a good DM
- Play in short sessions (30-45 minutes) that fit between grading papers
- Experience emergent stories she can bring back to her tabletop group as inspiration
- Explore a world where prose quality matters — she teaches literature, she notices bad writing

## Frustrations
- AI Dungeon generates incoherent slop after 10 minutes
- Video game RPGs are time sinks — she can't commit to 200 hours
- She loves theater-of-the-mind play but can't always find a group
- Most text games have "programmer writing" — functional but artless

## Behaviors
- Plays in 30-45 minute sessions, usually late evening
- Reads room descriptions aloud to herself (English teacher habit)
- Prefers exploration and social interaction over combat optimization
- Copies interesting AI-generated prose passages to share with students as writing examples
- Joins communities slowly — lurks for weeks before posting

## Relationship to Blade of Eternity
Lena validates the "editorial first" design direction and the AI narrative quality bar. She's the player who will notice if Literata renders beautifully at 18px, if the line height feels right for extended reading, and whether the AI narrator maintains consistent voice across sessions. She won't grind, but she'll explore every corner of every room and read every word.

## Key Scenario
Lena enters Mordoon's vaults during a 40-minute evening session. The AI narrator describes the environment with enough literary quality that she saves the passage. She talks to an NPC guard who's writing a thesis — the dialogue is genuinely interesting, not filler. She logs off satisfied and tells her D&D group about the encounter over pizza.

## Success Metric
Lena maintains a steady 3-4 sessions/week habit for months, driven by narrative quality rather than progression mechanics.
