# Elena Vasquez — The Epic Novelist

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 34-42 |
| **Role** | Full-time fantasy/sci-fi novelist |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Traditional publishing |
| **Location** | Pacific Northwest, USA |

## Bio

Elena is three books into a five-book epic fantasy series published through a mid-tier press. She maintains a 200-page "world bible" in Google Docs that she dreads opening because it's riddled with contradictions she doesn't have time to fix. She spends roughly 40% of her writing time on reference and consistency work instead of actual prose.

## Goals

1. Eliminate continuity errors before her editor catches them
2. Generate supporting lore (minor character backstories, historical events between known dates) that sounds like her voice, not generic AI slop
3. Have a single searchable source of truth for her universe instead of scattered docs

## Frustrations

1. Her world bible is a 200-page Google Doc with no cross-referencing — finding "what did I say about the Dwarven economy?" takes 20 minutes
2. She's tried World Anvil but couldn't justify the manual data entry when she should be writing prose
3. ChatGPT generates decent lore but forgets everything between sessions and contradicts her established canon

## Behaviors

- Writes 2,000+ words per day on a strict schedule
- Uses Scrivener for prose, Google Docs for world notes, a physical whiteboard for timelines
- References her world bible 10-15 times per writing session
- Has a dedicated "research day" once a week for world-building

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm writing chapter 14 and need to know the political dynamics of a kingdom I mentioned in chapter 3, I want to search my entire universe and get a consistent, cross-referenced answer, so I can keep writing without breaking continuity."

## Relationship to Product

Elena would discover the Knowledge Base through writing community forums (r/fantasywriters, NaNoWriMo). She'd onboard by pasting her existing world bible and reviewing the extracted entities. The features that matter most: canon editor, consistency checker, generation with voice matching. She'd churn if generation quality feels generic or if the import process mangles her existing notes.

## Scenarios

1. **Mid-chapter reference check** — Elena is writing a scene set in a city she described 200 pages ago. She searches for the city, gets the full entry with all connections, and continues writing within 30 seconds.
2. **Lore expansion sprint** — Between drafts, Elena uses bulk generation to create founding myths for every major city in her world. She reviews each, edits a few, promotes them to canon, and catches two contradictions the consistency engine flags.
