# Persona 05: Jamie — The Interactive Fiction Enthusiast

## Demographics
- **Age:** 26
- **Location:** London, UK
- **Occupation:** Graduate student (comparative literature)
- **Vision:** Fully sighted

## Technology Profile
- **Browser:** Firefox
- **Current games:** Choice of Games catalog, Fallen London, IF Comp/Spring Thing entries, Twine community games
- **Tools:** Twine (as an author), Ink scripting language
- **Devices:** Primarily laptop, occasionally phone

## Goals
- Experience narrative that responds to choices in meaningful, persistent ways — not just branching paths that reconverge
- Find a game where writing quality matches literary fiction
- Explore the intersection of procedural generation and authored narrative
- Eventually contribute to the game's narrative (quest writing, lore)

## Frustrations
- Choice-based games where choices are cosmetic ("your choice didn't actually matter")
- AI-generated text that reads like a chatbot — generic, repetitive, tonally flat
- Games that prioritize combat systems over story depth
- Multiplayer games where other players break narrative immersion

## Behaviors
- Reads every room description fully — never skips text
- Saves interesting narrative passages to a personal collection
- Replays branching points to see all outcomes
- Analyzes narrative structure and pacing like a craft exercise
- Active on IF forums and r/interactivefiction

## Relationship to Blade of Eternity
Jamie validates the AI narrative quality. The "Night at Mordoon" heritage and editorial design direction signal literary ambition. Jamie will notice — and judge — if AI-generated quest narratives feel generic or if NPC dialogue lacks voice. The emergent world events (festivals that evolve, NPCs that migrate) are exactly the kind of procedural narrative she studies academically.

## Key Scenario
Jamie encounters "the mask or the knife" choice. She chooses the mask. Three game-weeks later, an NPC references her choice in unscripted dialogue. She replays the moment on an alt character, chooses the knife, and the world's response is meaningfully different — not just a variable swap but a tonal shift in how NPCs treat her.

## Success Metric
Jamie describes the game's narrative as "authored quality at procedural scale" in her blog post, and submits a guest-written quest for inclusion.
