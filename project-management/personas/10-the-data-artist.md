# Persona 10: The Data Artist

**Name:** Luna Zhang
**Age:** 29
**Location:** Brooklyn, NY
**Occupation:** Data visualization designer at a media company
**Device:** iPhone 15, MacBook Air
**Income:** $92K/year

---

## Profile

Luna lives at the intersection of data and aesthetics. She creates interactive data visualizations for news articles by day and generative art with Processing/p5.js by night. She doesn't play mobile games. She found AI Fighter through a design blog post about the "Neural Combat" style system and was drawn not to the competition but to the visual language: decision graphs as identity, neural pathways as motif, data as aesthetics.

For Luna, the fighter graph IS the art. She wants to design graphs that are beautiful to look at — elegant topologies, satisfying symmetries, unexpected emergent patterns in the training data. Whether her fighter wins or loses is secondary to whether the graph and its behavior produce something visually interesting. She's the persona that proves AI Fighter's design system works: the graph editor is both a game tool and an expressive medium.

---

## Goals

- Create fighters whose graphs are visually elegant — topology as art
- Screenshot and share graph designs, training curves, and decision heatmaps as visual compositions
- Explore the aesthetic space: what does a "minimalist" fighter look like vs a "maximalist" one? What visual patterns emerge from different strategies?
- Use AI Fighter's visual language in her own generative art projects (with attribution)

## Frustrations

- No way to customize graph layout, node positioning, or connection line rendering for aesthetic purposes
- Graph editor that auto-layouts nodes in ugly grids instead of letting her compose the visual arrangement
- No high-resolution export of graph visualizations
- Battle replays that prioritize information density over visual composition
- Color limitations — she wants more palette options for her graph's visual presentation (cosmetic)

## Behaviors

- Plays 1-2 sessions per day, mostly in Fighter Studio arranging graph layouts
- Spends more time positioning nodes for visual effect than optimizing for competitive performance
- Screenshots graph designs obsessively — her Instagram has a dedicated highlight for "neural art"
- Exports graph JSON to visualize with custom tools (D3.js, Processing)
- Will buy graph color theme cosmetics immediately — her primary spending driver
- Shares on design-focused communities (Dribbble, Behance, design Twitter) reaching audiences the game wouldn't otherwise touch

---

## Key Scenarios

1. **First session:** Opens Fighter Studio, ignores the tutorial's strategic guidance, and spends 30 minutes arranging a 10-node graph into a visually symmetric pattern. Screenshots it. Posts on Instagram: "Playing a mobile game but actually making art???"
2. **Week 2:** Has designed 5 fighters optimized for visual elegance, not competitive performance. One of them accidentally performs well because the symmetric topology happens to create balanced decision-making. She's delighted by this — "beauty = function"
3. **Month 1:** Has created a generative art series called "Neural Portraits" using exported graph data from AI Fighter rendered through her own D3.js pipeline. The series gets featured on a design blog. 500 people download AI Fighter because of it

---

## Design Implications

- The graph editor should allow manual node positioning, not just auto-layout — placement IS expression for this persona
- High-resolution graph export (SVG or PNG at 2x/3x) enables the "graph as art" use case that generates organic marketing
- Graph color themes (cosmetic shop item) are high-value: Luna will buy every one
- The JSON graph export format should include node positions, not just connections — spatial arrangement is part of the design
- Consider a "Gallery" feature in the Lab where players can share graph visualizations (not just fight replays)
- Training data visualizations (heatmaps, performance curves) should be screenshot-worthy — these are the "data art" artifacts
- This persona drives organic reach into design and creative communities that overlap minimally with the gaming audience — a distinct acquisition channel
