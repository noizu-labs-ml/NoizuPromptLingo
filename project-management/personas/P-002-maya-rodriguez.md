# Maya Rodriguez — AI Hobbyist

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 26–34 |
| **Role** | Freelance Graphic Designer + Digital Artist |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Creative / Design |
| **Location** | Austin, TX |

## Bio

Maya runs a small freelance design practice by day and experiments with generative AI tools by night. She uses Midjourney, DALL-E, and Stable Diffusion for visual work, and increasingly uses LLMs to accelerate her copywriting, client brief analysis, and creative ideation. She's not a developer — she doesn't write code — but she has enough technical curiosity to read about model capabilities and tweak prompts methodically. Her relationship with AI is deeply practical: she wants better outputs faster, and she's willing to invest time learning techniques that pay off across projects.

## Goals

1. Discover image generation and creative writing prompts that produce consistently high-quality outputs without hours of iteration.
2. Understand which AI models are best suited for specific creative tasks so she stops paying for tools that underperform.
3. Connect with a community of creative practitioners who use AI seriously, not just as a party trick.

## Frustrations

1. Most "prompt libraries" online are generic and untested — copy-pasting them produces mediocre results with no explanation of why they work or when they fail.
2. Technical AI forums assume coding knowledge she doesn't have; she feels out of place asking "non-technical" questions.
3. There's no reliable signal for which prompts are currently effective on which model version — what worked three months ago often doesn't work today after a model update.

## Behaviors

- Saves effective prompts in a Notes app organized by use case (portrait, landscape, copywriting, brainstorm).
- Follows r/StableDiffusion and r/ChatGPT but finds the post quality inconsistent.
- Watches YouTube tutorials on prompting but wishes they included the actual prompt text in a copyable format.
- Tests new prompts on personal projects before using them for paying clients.
- Spends 1–2 hours per week specifically exploring new AI techniques.

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm starting a new creative project and need AI-assisted outputs, I want to quickly find tested prompts for my specific use case, so I can deliver better client work without spending hours debugging prompts myself."

## Relationship to Product

Maya finds Meat Brains through a Twitter/X post sharing a surprisingly beautiful image prompt that links back to the platform. She browses the "Image Generation" category and immediately finds value — posts have clear descriptions, community voting surfaces the best ones, and comments explain model-specific behavior. She creates an account to save prompts and occasionally posts her own when she discovers something that works well. Features that matter most: category/tag filtering, saved post collections, model version annotations on prompts. She churns if the creative/art category becomes underpopulated relative to developer-focused content.

## Scenarios

1. **Finding a Character Illustration Prompt** — Maya has a client who needs consistent character illustrations across ten scenes. She searches Meat Brains for "character consistency Midjourney" and finds a top-voted technique using style reference seeds and weighted descriptors. She adapts it for her client's aesthetic and delivers the project two days early.

2. **Asking About Model Differences** — Maya is confused about whether Claude or GPT-4o produces better creative writing for brand voice work. She posts a question on Meat Brains with two sample outputs and asks the community to weigh in. Within 24 hours she has five detailed responses from practitioners who've run their own comparisons, saving her a week of personal experimentation.
