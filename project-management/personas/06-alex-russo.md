## Bio

Alex is a systems thinker who applies structured methodology to every domain of life. GTD practitioner for 6 years. Runs quarterly OKRs for personal development. Tracks habits (meditation, exercise, reading, journaling) with streaks. Uses Notion as a "life OS" with elaborate databases for everything from book notes to meal plans to career goals. The problem: Notion is powerful but dumb. Alex wants the structure of a personal operating system with the intelligence of an AI that understands the whole picture.

## Goals

1. **AI-powered personal operating system** — an agent that knows all commitments (personal, professional, social) and actively helps plan days, flag conflicts, and maintain momentum on goals
2. **Unified life + work OKRs** — professional KRs (ship design system, reduce design-to-dev handoff time) alongside personal KRs (run a half marathon, read 24 books, save $15K) in one hierarchy
3. **Habit tracking with smart nudges** — not just "did you exercise?" but "you've missed 2 days and your half-marathon KR is at risk — here's a 20-min bodyweight option"
4. **Weekly review automation** — the GTD weekly review is powerful but takes 90 minutes manually; wants an agent to draft it

## Frustrations

- **Notion is infinitely flexible but zero intelligent:** Can build any database, but it never proactively says "you're behind on your reading goal" or "you have a conflict Thursday"
- **Work tasks live in Linear, personal in Notion, habits in Streaks app:** Three tools, no unified view of the day
- **Weekly review is a slog:** Reviewing every project, every waiting-for, every someday-maybe manually across multiple systems
- **AI in Notion is a writing tool, not a planning tool:** Can summarize a page but can't analyze goal progress or suggest daily priorities
- **Productivity YouTube tells him tools exist that don't:** He's been promised "AI life assistant" by a dozen apps that deliver a chatbot wrapper on a todo list

## Job to Be Done

> "I want an AI that truly understands my entire life system — goals, habits, commitments, projects — and actively helps me stay on track, not just stores my lists and waits for me to check them."

## Key Scenarios

1. **Morning planning:** Planner agent generates: "Today: Design review at 10am (work). Gym at 6pm (habit, streak: 5). Reading goal: 2 chapters behind — 20 min tonight? Grocery list has 8 items (route past Trader Joe's after gym)." Alex adjusts and confirms.
2. **Weekly review:** Agent drafts: "This week: 14 items completed, 3 deferred. Exercise: 4/4 (on track). Reading: 1.5 books behind pace. Someday list: 3 items promoted to active. Work: design system milestone hit." Alex reviews in 20 minutes instead of 90.
3. **Goal conflict detection:** Planner agent flags: "You committed to a Saturday workshop (professional development KR) but you also have a long run scheduled (half marathon KR). Suggest: move long run to Sunday morning."
4. **Habit intelligence:** "Your meditation streak broke yesterday. Historically, you skip after late-night work sessions. You have a late meeting tomorrow — consider a 5-min guided session before bed."

## Product Implications

- **Free tier is the entry point** — personal todos, habits, basic OKRs
- **Pro tier ($14/mo)** when they want more agents and the full planner experience
- **This is the viral persona** — shares setups publicly, creates content about the tool
- **Mobile is essential** — personal capture happens away from desk
- **Planner agent sophistication** determines retention — must be genuinely intelligent, not template-based
- **Non-technical UX required** — this persona doesn't think in sprints or deploys; the personal layer must stand alone without dev-tool framing
- **Risk:** If the product feels like "developer tool with personal bolted on," this persona bounces immediately
