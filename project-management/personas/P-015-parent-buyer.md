# P-015: Jennifer Martinez

| Attribute | Value |

## Bio
Jennifer has two children (ages 8 and 11) who want to play mobile games. She treats games as potential educational enrichment rather than waste of time, spending significant time researching games before approving downloads. She spends $15-60 monthly on games with proven educational value (math puzzles, reading games, creative building) but blocks games with gambling-like mechanics or violent content.

## Goals
- Find games that are both engaging and educational for her children
- Protect her children from predatory monetization
- Set healthy limits on screen time and spending

## Frustrations
- Games disguising gambling mechanics as "fun rewards"
- Difficulty finding trustworthy educational games in app stores
- Children's peer pressure to play games she considers inappropriate

## Behaviors
- Researches games extensively before children can download
- Spends $15-60 monthly on educational games
- Sets strict time limits and screen controls
- Shares recommendations with other parent groups

## Job to Be Done
> "When my children discover a new game they want to play, I want to quickly assess its educational value and safety, so I can make informed decisions about what enters our home."

## Relationship to Product
Jennifer is a gatekeeper with purchasing power. She heavily researches and vetoes games before her children ever see them. She pays consistently for quality educational content and shares recommendations with parent groups, making her an influential node in family gaming decisions.

## Scenarios
- **Scenario 1:** Jennifer's 11-year-old asks for a game and she spends an hour researching reviews and content warnings before deciding
- **Scenario 2:** Jennifer subscribes to an educational app at $10/month after verifying its alignment with her children's school curriculum
