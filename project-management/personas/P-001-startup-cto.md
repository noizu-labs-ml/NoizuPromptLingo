# Marcus Chen — Startup CTO / Technical Co-founder

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 28–38 |
| **Role** | Founder / CEO or Technical Co-founder |
| **Technical Level** | Novice to Intermediate |
| **Industry** | SaaS, FinTech, HealthTech, or Consumer Tech |
| **Location** | US (Tier 2 city or remote) |

## Bio

Marcus is 18 months into building his B2B SaaS startup with a small team of 3–5 engineers. He has a product background and can read code but doesn't architect systems. After a painful series of technical decisions (wrong database, no staging environment, no CI/CD), he realizes he needs experienced technical leadership before the next funding round exposes his platform's fragility.

## Goals

1. Stabilize the technical foundation before Series A due diligence
2. Build a hiring pipeline and establish engineering culture without overpaying a full-time CTO
3. Translate investor and customer requirements into a credible technical roadmap

## Frustrations

1. Technical co-founder left; no one owns architecture decisions — every engineer has a different opinion
2. Can't evaluate whether his current engineering team is competent or just confident
3. Investors ask questions about scalability, security posture, and tech debt that he cannot answer credibly

## Behaviors

- Lurks on Hacker News, Indie Hackers, and startup Slack communities
- Has already tried one fractional CTO from a marketplace (bad fit — too junior, ghosted)
- Googles "fractional CTO cost" and "how to evaluate CTO candidate" obsessively
- Asks his investor network for warm intros before trusting anyone new

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm approaching a funding milestone and my platform feels like it could fall apart, I want someone who can walk in, assess the situation, and tell me exactly what needs to be fixed and in what order, so I can close the round without a technical mess derailing the deal."

## Relationship to Product

Discovers noizu.com via a referral from an investor or through Google search on fractional CTO services. The services page and credentials are what convert him — he needs to see proof of outcomes, not just titles. The RFI dashboard is his first interaction post-discovery: he submits a structured brief and wants a fast, substantive response. Once engaged, the client dashboard is critical for keeping him informed without requiring constant Slack pings.

## Scenarios

1. **Pre-funding technical assessment** — Needs a rapid 2-week architecture audit and remediation plan before a term sheet comes in; uses the RFI to describe the situation and request a scoped engagement
2. **Engineering team evaluation** — Wants Keith to interview and evaluate his 4 engineers and produce an honest assessment; worried one is underperforming but lacks the expertise to judge
