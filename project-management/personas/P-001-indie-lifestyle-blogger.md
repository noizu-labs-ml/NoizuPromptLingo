# Maya Chen — The Recognition Seeker

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 26-33 |
| **Role** | Part-time blogger, full-time barista |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Lifestyle / Food & Travel |
| **Location** | Mid-sized U.S. city (Portland, Austin, Nashville type) |

## Bio

Maya has been running her lifestyle blog "Small Cups, Big Trips" for three years — writing about specialty coffee, weekend travel, and slow living. She posts 2-3 times a week and has built a modest but loyal readership of around 4,000 monthly visitors. She pours real care into her writing and photography but often wonders if she's actually improving or just spinning her wheels in obscurity.

## Goals

1. Get external validation that her blog is genuinely good, not just "good for an indie blogger"
2. Discover where she stands relative to similar lifestyle blogs she admires
3. Find a community of serious bloggers who take their craft as seriously as she does

## Frustrations

1. Google Analytics tells her traffic numbers but nothing about quality — she can't tell if her writing is actually getting better
2. Blog "awards" feel like pay-to-play schemes with no credibility
3. Most blogging communities online devolve into follow-for-follow spam with no real feedback

## Behaviors

- Checks her blog stats every morning before her shift
- Engages actively in 2-3 blogging Facebook groups and one Discord server
- Reads other lifestyle blogs obsessively, bookmarking ones she wants to emulate
- Occasionally submits to blogging roundups but rarely hears back

## Job to Be Done

> "When I've spent hours crafting a post I'm proud of, I want to know how it measures up objectively, so I can see my growth over time and feel like the effort is worth it."

## Relationship to Product

Maya would discover BloggersCompete through a blogging community or Pinterest. She'd sign up for free to get her AI quality score, feel a hit of dopamine or productive frustration from the 6-dimension breakdown, and enter her first competition. The leaderboard would hook her — she'd check it the same way she checks Instagram likes. She'd likely upgrade to Pro within 2-3 months once she wants to see full analytics trends over time. Churn risk: if competitions feel rigged or the scores don't feel credible.

## Scenarios

1. **First Score Shock** — Maya submits her blog and discovers she scores well on Writing Quality and Engagement but poorly on Consistency. She didn't realize how irregular her posting schedule looks from the outside. She adjusts her calendar immediately.
2. **Entering the Monthly Lifestyle Challenge** — Maya competes in a "Best Slow Living Blog" themed monthly competition, makes the top 10, screenshots the badge, and posts it to her Instagram Stories.
