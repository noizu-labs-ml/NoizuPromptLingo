# Jordan Kim — The Eager Beginner

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 21-28 |
| **Role** | Graduate student / part-time retail worker |
| **Technical Level** | Novice |
| **Industry** | Personal finance / self-improvement |
| **Location** | U.S. suburban or college town |

## Bio

Jordan started their blog "Broke but Growing" six weeks ago, writing about budgeting on a student stipend and side-hustle experiments. They've published 9 posts, have about 80 monthly readers (mostly friends and family), and are consuming every "how to grow a blog" YouTube video they can find. Jordan has genuine ideas and a distinctive voice but no framework for knowing what's actually working or where to focus next.

## Goals

1. Get structured feedback on what they're doing right and what's holding their blog back
2. Understand what "good blogging" looks like so they can set realistic growth targets
3. Find a community of beginner bloggers at a similar stage who can relate to their experience

## Frustrations

1. Generic advice like "write consistently" and "find your niche" doesn't translate into actionable next steps
2. They can't tell if their writing is objectively weak or if their blog just needs more time and promotion
3. Most blogging tools and communities are designed for people who already have traction

## Behaviors

- Publishes 1-2 posts per week but with no consistent schedule
- Reads Pat Flynn, Adam Enfroy, and Blogging Wizard religiously
- Participates in Reddit's r/blogging but feels intimidated by posts from people making thousands per month
- Has signed up for 4 different blogging courses but finished none of them

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm just starting out and don't know if I'm on the right track, I want a clear breakdown of what I'm doing well and what to improve first, so I can make real progress instead of guessing."

## Relationship to Product

Jordan would discover BloggersCompete through a Reddit recommendation or a YouTube video about blog auditing tools. The free tier is their entry point — they'd never pay $12/mo at this stage. The 6-dimension score breakdown is exactly the structured feedback they've been craving. They might not enter competitions immediately (feeling too junior), but they'd use the score as a development roadmap. Long-term conversion to Pro is possible once they hit 1,000 monthly readers and start caring about tracking improvement over time. Churn risk: if scores feel discouraging without enough guidance on how to improve.

## Scenarios

1. **The First Audit** — Jordan submits their blog and gets a 54/100 overall, with strong Originality (73) but weak SEO (31) and Consistency (28). For the first time they have a clear priority: post schedule and basic SEO. They feel motivated rather than crushed because the scores come with specific notes.
2. **Lurking on the Leaderboard** — Jordan filters the leaderboard to "blogs under 6 months old" to find peers at a similar stage, discovers a personal finance blog they immediately subscribe to, and sends the owner a DM that starts a genuine mentorship relationship.
