## Demographics

- **Age:** 42
- **Role:** First-time founder, previously VP of Sales at a logistics company
- **Location:** Chicago, IL
- **Income:** $80K (bootstrapping from savings)
- **Tech comfort:** Low — can use Notion, Canva, and spreadsheets; can't read code

## Quote

> "I know exactly what this product needs to do. I just can't explain it in a way that developers understand."

## Goals

1. Translate domain expertise into a product spec a developer can build from
2. Get visual mockups to show investors and potential customers
3. Avoid burning $30K on a dev shop that builds the wrong thing
4. Maintain control of the vision even when he can't write the code

## Frustrations

- Hired a freelance developer who built something that didn't match his description
- Can't evaluate whether a technical proposal is reasonable or overengineered
- Wireframing tools assume design vocabulary he doesn't have
- Feels dependent on technical co-founders who keep flaking

## Behaviors

- Describes products in terms of user workflows and business outcomes, not features
- Screenshots competitor apps and annotates them in Apple Notes
- Reads startup blogs (First Round Review, Lenny's Newsletter)
- Budget-conscious — compares tool costs to freelancer hourly rates

## Scenario

David discovers noizu.ink through a startup community post. He types his idea for a fleet maintenance scheduling tool in plain English. The Sketch phase asks him clarifying questions he hadn't thought of ("Does a dispatcher assign jobs, or do drivers self-select?"). He ends up with a PRD that's clearer than anything he's written before. He moves into Draft, gets mockups, and emails them to three potential customers for feedback. One says "I'd pay for this today." David now has validation AND a spec he can hand to a developer — or push through Ink himself.
