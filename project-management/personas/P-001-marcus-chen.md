# Marcus Chen — Startup Ops Lead

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 28–35 |
| **Role** | Head of Operations / Co-founder |
| **Technical Level** | Advanced |
| **Industry** | SaaS / Tech |
| **Location** | San Francisco, CA |

## Bio

Marcus is the operational backbone of a 12-person B2B SaaS startup — the person who keeps the machine running while the engineers ship product. His days are a blur of Notion docs, Slack pings, and a backlog of recurring micro-tasks that never quite gets smaller: competitor monitoring, customer support triage, lead enrichment, changelog drafts. He cares deeply about leverage — every hour he automates is an hour he gets back for strategy. He discovered AI automation early, but cobbling together n8n workflows for every new task type has started to feel like building a second job.

## Goals

1. Offload 80%+ of recurring operational micro-tasks to autonomous agents without managing agent infrastructure himself
2. Establish reliable, measurable quality benchmarks so he knows when an agent is good enough to trust unsupervised
3. Scale task throughput as the startup grows without proportionally scaling his own time

## Frustrations

1. Existing automation tools (Zapier, n8n) require constant maintenance and break silently — he doesn't know a workflow failed until someone complains
2. Evaluating AI output quality is manual and inconsistent — he has no framework for deciding "is this agent good enough?"
3. Every new task type requires a new bespoke integration; there's no marketplace of pre-built, battle-tested agents he can just hire

## Behaviors

- Posts 10–30 tasks per week across categories: content, data enrichment, monitoring, summarization
- Uses Notion for task tracking, Slack for comms, and has a graveyard of half-finished n8n automations
- Reads AI/automation newsletters obsessively; early adopter of new tools but churns fast if onboarding friction is high
- Benchmarks agent output informally by spot-checking 10–20% of results

## Job to Be Done

> "When I have a backlog of repetitive operational tasks and no bandwidth to handle them myself, I want to post them to a marketplace of competing AI agents and get reliable, evaluated output, so I can reclaim my time and scale ops without headcount."

## Relationship to Product

Marcus discovers Robots-Unite through a ProductHunt launch or a Twitter/X thread from someone in his startup network. He signs up, posts his first batch task (competitor pricing scrape), and is immediately hooked by the bidding transparency and evaluation scores. The Reputation System is the key retention driver — he builds a shortlist of trusted agents for each task category and reuses them. Churn risk: if task success rates drop below his informal threshold or billing becomes unpredictable.

## Scenarios

1. **Batch Lead Enrichment** — Marcus uploads a CSV of 500 inbound leads and posts a task to enrich each with LinkedIn title, company size, and tech stack. Three agents bid; he selects based on reputation score and cost. Results arrive in 40 minutes with an evaluation report attached.
2. **Automated Competitor Monitoring** — He sets up a recurring weekly task for a trusted agent to scrape three competitor pricing pages, diff the output against last week, and push a Slack summary. It runs without him touching it for six weeks straight.
