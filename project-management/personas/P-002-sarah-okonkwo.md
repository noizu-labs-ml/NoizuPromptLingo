# Sarah Okonkwo — Industrial Operations Manager

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 42–52 |
| **Role** | Director of Plant Operations |
| **Technical Level** | Novice |
| **Industry** | Discrete Manufacturing |
| **Location** | Midwest, USA |

## Bio

Sarah runs day-to-day operations at a mid-sized automotive parts manufacturing plant with 800 employees and a floor covered in CNC machines, conveyor systems, and environmental sensors — approximately 3,500 IoT-connected devices installed over the last three years. She championed the original IoT investment to the board and now owns the outcome. Her engineering team has produced promising pilot results but the transition to autonomous operation keeps stalling over concerns about reliability, liability, and unclear ROI reporting.

## Goals

1. Get a clear, board-ready ROI narrative from the IoT investment — downtime avoided, energy saved, maintenance costs reduced.
2. Move the automation pilot from three machines to full floor coverage without requiring a dedicated engineering team to babysit it.
3. Reduce unplanned downtime by 30% within 12 months to hit a KPI she committed to in Q1.

## Frustrations

1. Her engineering team gives her dashboards full of metrics she doesn't know how to interpret — she needs outcomes, not telemetry.
2. Every time she asks "is this working?", the answer involves a 20-minute explanation of sensor calibration; she needs a simple yes/no health indicator.
3. The vendor who sold her the IoT sensors left after deployment; ongoing support has been patchwork, and she doesn't trust that the platform is being actively managed.

## Behaviors

- Reviews a one-page weekly operations summary; delegates all technical detail to her engineering lead.
- Makes buy vs. build decisions based on reference customers in her industry, not technical specs.
- Attends one or two industry conferences per year (e.g., Hannover Messe, ARC Forum); IoTGo could appear on her radar through peer referrals there.
- Approves software purchases under $50K; escalates above that to the VP of Operations.
- Communicates in outcomes: "reduced downtime," "energy cost per unit," "maintenance events avoided."

## Job to Be Done

> "When I present to the board in Q3, I need to show that our IoT investment is producing measurable operational improvements — and that the system runs itself without requiring two engineers to watch it around the clock."

## Relationship to Product

Sarah does not discover IoTGo herself — her engineering lead (likely a Marcus-type) brings it to her as a recommendation. Her evaluation is entirely outcomes-based: she wants to see a case study from a manufacturing customer and a pre-built Outcome Dashboard she can screenshot for executive reporting. She approves the Pro plan based on her engineering team's recommendation and an IoTGo sales conversation that leads with downtime ROI calculators, not architecture diagrams. She becomes a champion if the Outcome Dashboard produces a credible "downtime events avoided" number within 60 days. Churn risk: if her engineering team leaves and institutional knowledge walks out with them, she'll have no one to maintain the platform and will seek a more managed-service alternative.

## Scenarios

1. **The Board Slide** — 45 days after IoTGo deployment, Sarah asks her engineering lead for a Q2 operations update. The Outcome Dashboard shows 14 autonomous remediations, 2 avoided unplanned downtime events, and an estimated $47,000 in maintenance cost avoidance. She pastes the chart directly into her board presentation.

2. **The Pilot Expansion Decision** — After three months of successful autonomous operation on the pilot machines, Sarah needs to decide whether to expand to the full floor. She asks IoTGo's sales team for a reference call with another Midwest manufacturer. The call confirms similar results. She approves the Team plan and schedules rollout for Q3.
