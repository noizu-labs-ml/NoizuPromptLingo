## Bio

Travis runs four affiliate blogs simultaneously — a personal finance site, an outdoor gear review site, a pet care blog, and a kitchen appliances review site. Combined, they generate $8-12K/month through Amazon Associates, Wirecutter-style affiliate deals, and display ads. He treats blogging as a portfolio of assets, not a creative outlet. Every decision is validated through data: keyword difficulty, traffic potential, commission rates. He's not attached to any particular blog; he'd sell one tomorrow if the multiple was right.

## Goals

1. Use SEO dimension scores to identify which of his blogs has the strongest foundation for long-term search performance
2. Identify specific on-page SEO weaknesses across his portfolio faster than manual audits allow
3. Leverage any legitimate ranking or quality badge to improve click-through rates and advertiser perception

## Frustrations

1. Running 4 blogs means auditing each one thoroughly is a quarterly slog that takes a full week
2. His existing SEO tools (Ahrefs, Surfer SEO) don't give him cross-blog comparative views in a single dashboard
3. The "AI content" stigma is hurting his traffic; he needs credible quality signals to counter the narrative

## Behaviors

- Publishes 15-20 posts per month across his portfolio, using AI drafts that he edits
- Runs weekly Ahrefs rank trackers and monthly content audits
- Active in Income School's community and several private affiliate marketing Slack groups
- Monitors his site portfolio performance through a custom Google Data Studio dashboard

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm managing multiple affiliate blogs, I want a fast, cross-blog quality score that pinpoints SEO weaknesses, so I can prioritize improvements by ROI potential instead of auditing each site from scratch."

## Relationship to Product

Travis would discover BloggersCompete through an affiliate marketing forum or a "niche site" YouTube channel. He'd be drawn specifically to the SEO dimension score — he'll test it against his Ahrefs data to see if it correlates. He'd sign up Pro quickly (it's a business expense under $20) and submit all 4 blogs immediately. He's a power user on the analytics side but largely indifferent to community features or competitions. He might enter competitions if winning would generate a backlink — he'll calculate the DA before deciding. Churn risk: if SEO scores don't correlate with actual ranking performance, he'll cancel within 60 days.

## Scenarios

1. **Portfolio Triage** — Travis submits all four blogs and compares their SEO dimension scores side by side. His kitchen appliances site scores lowest (41/100 on SEO). He investigates the detailed breakdown, finds issues with internal linking and missing schema markup, and schedules fixes.
2. **The Backlink Calculation** — Travis enters a "Best Personal Finance Blog" competition not because he cares about the badge, but because the BloggersCompete domain authority is DR 52. A competition listing page = a legitimate DR 52 backlink. He submits his PF site the day the competition opens.
