# Derek Huang — The Context-Aware Advertiser

## Demographics
| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 39 |
| **Role** | Director of Developer Marketing |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Developer Tools / SaaS |
| **Location** | San Francisco, CA |

## Bio
Derek runs marketing for a mid-size developer tool company. He is exhausted by CPM-based display advertising with poor targeting and brand safety issues. He is actively looking for ways to reach technically sophisticated audiences in contexts where his brand won't look out of place. He has a modest experimentation budget ($2-3K/month) that he uses to test non-standard placements.

## Goals
1. Get his brand in front of developers and technical users who are already in a discovery mindset
2. Appear in a context that signals quality and curation — not banner ads on a link aggregator
3. Measure at minimum referral traffic and at best downstream signups from the placement

## Frustrations
1. Google Ads target technically-minded users but feel cheap and get blocked by ad blockers
2. Newsletter sponsorships are expensive, hard to scale, and hard to attribute
3. Most "premium placement" ad products on developer platforms are still just premium banner ads

## Behaviors
- Evaluates sponsorship by audience fit first, reach second — he would rather reach 5,000 right people than 50,000 wrong people
- Negotiates 30-90 day trial placements before committing to longer terms
- Uses UTM parameters on all sponsor links and checks weekly attribution reports
- Will ask for audience demographic data and category traffic stats before signing

## Job to Be Done
> "I want to sponsor a category that my customers are already browsing — so the placement feels like a natural recommendation, not an interruption."

## Relationship to Product
Derek represents the $500-2K/month Sponsor tier. He is one of the key revenue sources for the platform. His purchase decision requires a self-serve or low-friction sales flow — he won't want a 45-minute demo call to buy a $500 category sponsorship. He will churn if he can't see attribution data or if the category traffic is lower than represented. A good experience means he expands to multiple category sponsorships and refers other marketing peers.

## Scenarios
1. **Category Discovery** — Derek finds gotta.cc via a newsletter and notices the "Developer Tools" category has strong site quality and a browsing audience that matches his ICP. He clicks through to the Sponsor page, sees the self-serve pricing for category sponsorship, and places a 30-day trial order.
2. **Attribution Review** — Three weeks into his sponsorship, Derek pulls the gotta.cc attribution report. He sees 340 referral clicks to his site, 12 trial signups. The CPL is $42, comparable to his best-performing newsletter placements. He extends to 90 days.
