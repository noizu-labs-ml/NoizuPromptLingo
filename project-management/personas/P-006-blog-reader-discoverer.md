# Marcus Webb — The Curious Consumer

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 29-50 |
| **Role** | Product manager at a mid-size tech company |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Technology / Product |
| **Location** | Remote (Pacific Northwest) |

## Bio

Marcus is an avid reader who builds his knowledge through long-form blog content rather than social media. He has a carefully curated RSS reader with 80+ feeds, a Readwise account, and a habit of doing a 45-minute "reading block" every morning before standup. He's good at finding quality content in tech and product, but struggles to discover quality writing in areas where he's less networked — personal finance, health, and hobby topics like woodworking and home brewing.

## Goals

1. Discover genuinely high-quality blogs in niche topics without wading through SEO-spam and thin content
2. Use quality signals (not just popularity) to decide what's worth adding to his reading stack
3. Find new writers before they're famous — the satisfaction of being an early reader of someone great

## Frustrations

1. Google results in his niche hobbies surface the same 5 authority sites over and over; small great blogs are invisible
2. Most "best blog" lists are either outdated, pay-to-play, or based purely on traffic metrics
3. He's burned enough time on "highly recommended" blogs that turned out to be mediocre to be skeptical of social proof alone

## Behaviors

- Uses Feedly as his primary RSS aggregator; checks it every morning
- Follows 3-4 "link blog" style newsletters that curate quality writing (e.g., Dense Discovery, Sidebar)
- Regularly browses Reddit's r/slatestarcodex-adjacent communities for writing recommendations
- Occasionally uses Product Hunt to discover new content tools

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm looking for quality writing in an unfamiliar niche, I want a discovery layer with credible, multi-dimensional quality scores, so I can find excellent small blogs without spending hours sifting through mediocre content."

## Relationship to Product

Marcus would discover BloggersCompete via a curated newsletter or a Hacker News "Show HN" thread. He'd immediately browse the leaderboard filtered by topic, scan the 6-dimension scores to understand what's being measured, and click through to 3-4 blogs he's never heard of. His engagement is lightweight — he never creates an account. He might return monthly to check the leaderboard or browse recent competition winners. He represents a key value prop for bloggers (exposure to quality readers) but generates no direct revenue. Retention depends on leaderboard freshness and score credibility.

## Scenarios

1. **Niche Discovery Session** — Marcus filters the BloggersCompete leaderboard to "Home Brewing" blogs sorted by Writing Quality score. He finds two blogs in the top 10 he'd never encountered, subscribes to both via RSS, and adds one to his Readwise highlights feed.
2. **Competition Winner Browse** — Marcus clicks through from a newsletter mention of BloggersCompete's "Best Indie Science Blog" competition, reads the top 3 winners, and ends up spending 45 minutes going down a rabbit hole he attributes to the platform's curation.
