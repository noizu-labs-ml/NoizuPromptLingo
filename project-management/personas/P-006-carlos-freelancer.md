# Carlos Mendez — Freelance Consultant

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 32–45 |
| **Role** | Freelance Web / Product Consultant |
| **Technical Level** | Advanced |
| **Industry** | Consulting / Agency |
| **Location** | Latin America / EU / Southeast Asia (Remote) |

## Bio
Carlos runs a one-person consultancy serving 4–6 clients at a time, primarily small businesses and funded startups that need senior product and technical guidance without hiring a full-time team. He wins engagements through referrals and proposals, and his competitive advantage is speed — he can go from "discovery call" to "here's what I'd build" in 48 hours. He has built his practice around AI tooling to maximize output-per-hour, and his hourly rate justifies any tool that shaves 2+ hours per engagement.

## Goals
1. Produce professional-grade wireframes and flow diagrams during the scoping phase to differentiate proposals and demonstrate value before a contract is signed
2. Generate initial deliverable mockups quickly at engagement kickoff so clients feel momentum from day one
3. Reduce the time spent on visual artifacts that are "good enough" so he can invest more time in strategy and advice

## Frustrations
1. Clients expect visual deliverables but rarely budget for a designer — Carlos has to produce them himself at no extra charge to stay competitive
2. Building wireframes in Figma takes longer than the strategic thinking they represent, creating an awkward time-cost ratio
3. Client feedback on mockups is disorganized — email threads, WhatsApp messages, and screenshots with arrows drawn in markup apps

## Behaviors
- Builds a personal toolkit of AI-augmented workflows; evaluates new tools within 30 minutes and adopts if ROI is immediate
- Uses Claude Code or Cursor for code generation; comfortable adding MCP servers to extend capability
- Sends clients a link rather than an attachment whenever possible — reduces version confusion
- Charges a "discovery and scoping" flat fee that covers the first deliverable; mockup speed directly increases margin on this phase

## Job to Be Done
> "When I'm scoping a new client engagement, I want to generate a credible wireframe set from a discovery call transcript, so I can include visuals in the proposal without spending 4 hours in Figma before the contract is even signed."

## Relationship to Product
Carlos discovers Mockup MCP through developer Twitter/X, Indie Hackers, or a freelancer Slack community. He adopts quickly if there's a free tier for scoping-phase use and a paid tier for unlimited client deliverables. Key features: speed of generation, shareable feedback links (clients don't install software), and professional enough output to include in a proposal PDF. Churn happens if the per-generation cost is high relative to his proposal volume or if the feedback-collection UX is clunky enough that clients call him instead of leaving comments.

## Scenarios
1. **Proposal differentiation** — Carlos is competing for a $25k e-commerce redesign project. He records the discovery call, feeds the transcript to Claude, and uses Mockup MCP to generate a 6-screen wireframe set for the proposed new checkout flow. The proposal PDF includes the wireframes. He wins the engagement; the client says his proposal was "the only one that showed they'd actually listened."
2. **Kickoff deliverable** — On day one of a new engagement, Carlos generates an architecture diagram and user flow diagram from the client's tech spec, presents them in the kickoff meeting, and uses the feedback-collection link to gather corrections. The client perceives the engagement as already delivering value before a line of code is written.
