# Persona: Yuki Tanaka — Language Learner / Non-Native Reader

## Profile
- **Age:** 27
- **Role:** Software Engineer, relocating from Tokyo to US company
- **Environment:** MacBook Pro, home desk, reading English technical documentation
- **Tech Comfort:** High (engineering background), English proficiency B2

## Goals
- Hear correct pronunciation of technical English terms and acronyms
- Pause and get plain-language explanations of dense passages
- Read at a comfortable pace that doesn't outrun comprehension
- Build vocabulary in context, not from a dictionary

## Frustrations
- Technical PDFs assume native fluency — idioms and dense noun phrases trip her up
- No way to hear how "Kubernetes" or "mutex" is pronounced in natural speech
- Can't ask "what does this sentence actually mean?" without switching to a translator
- Reading speed is slower; she re-reads paragraphs multiple times

## Behaviors
- Reads with a notepad; writes down unfamiliar terms
- Often looks up terms in parallel (expensive context switching)
- Prefers slower TTS speed; values clarity over naturalness
- More likely to use text Q&A than voice (still building spoken English confidence)

## Key Scenarios
1. Hears "mutex" read aloud for the first time — asks "how is that spelled and what does it mean?" — gets both
2. Pauses on a passive-voice heavy sentence and asks "explain this more simply" — gets a plain restatement
3. Asks "what acronyms appear in this document?" — gets a list with expansions found in the doc itself

## Acceptance Criteria
- TTS handles technical terminology without garbling (especially acronyms)
- "Explain simply" command produces plain-language restatement, not just a synonym
- App surfaces acronym definitions from within the document, not external knowledge
