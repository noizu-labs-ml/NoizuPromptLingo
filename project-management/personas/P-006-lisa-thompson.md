# Lisa Thompson — Content Creator

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 28–40 |
| **Role** | Full-time YouTuber and AI Newsletter Author |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Media / Creator Economy |
| **Location** | Toronto, Canada |

## Bio

Lisa runs a YouTube channel with 180,000 subscribers focused on practical AI tools for knowledge workers — think "how to use AI to research faster, write better, and run a smarter business." She also writes a weekly newsletter to 25,000 subscribers. Her audience skews toward smart non-technical professionals: lawyers, consultants, marketing managers, HR leaders who want to leverage AI without coding. Lisa keeps up with AI advances professionally; she needs to test new tools and techniques weekly to stay current and credible. She uses Meat Brains primarily as a research tool — scouting what's trending, finding proven techniques to feature in tutorials, and gauging which topics the advanced community thinks matter.

## Goals

1. Discover emerging prompt techniques before they go mainstream so her tutorials arrive early enough to build authority.
2. Validate that a technique she plans to feature actually works — community upvotes and comments give her a signal she trusts more than a single YouTube comment.
3. Build a reputation in the advanced AI community that makes practitioners share her content, expanding beyond her current audience.

## Frustrations

1. By the time a technique hits mass-market AI blogs, it's old news and her audience has already seen it — she needs upstream sources.
2. She can't easily tell from a Meat Brains post whether a technique is accessible to her non-technical audience or requires API access she can't expect her followers to have.
3. She wants to credit the original sources of techniques she features, but community posts often don't have clear authorship lineage — they aggregate from multiple sources without attribution.

## Behaviors

- Spends 2–3 hours per week browsing Meat Brains, sorted by "Rising" and "Top This Month."
- Bookmarks posts using a Notion research database tagged by topic and complexity level.
- Comments occasionally with her YouTube context — "I'm considering featuring this in a tutorial for non-technical users, does it require API access?"
- Cross-posts her own prompts when she discovers something new; her posts often go viral because her audience also joins Meat Brains to comment.
- Occasionally posts "I'm making a video on X — what should I cover?" discussion threads.

## Job to Be Done

> "When I'm planning my next tutorial or newsletter issue, I want to identify what the advanced AI community is excited about right now, so I can create content that's timely, accurate, and credible enough to satisfy both my mainstream audience and the technical practitioners who'll call me out if I get it wrong."

## Relationship to Product

Lisa discovers Meat Brains through a tweet from a prompt engineer she follows. She immediately recognizes it as a better signal source than her current mix of Twitter/X following, subreddit browsing, and Discord lurking. She creates an account and spends the first session purely reading. She starts posting her own content after her first video featuring a Meat Brains technique — she credits the platform and links back, driving 3,000 new signups in a weekend. The platform benefits from her audience; she benefits from the community's credibility. Features that matter most: "Rising" sort for early trend detection, clean embeddable prompt blocks she can screenshot for thumbnails, user attribution with profile links. She churns if the content quality regresses and the platform becomes unreliable as a research source.

## Scenarios

1. **Researching an Upcoming Tutorial** — Lisa is planning a video on "using AI for competitive research." She searches Meat Brains for techniques in that category, finds three high-voted posts covering different approaches, reads the comment threads for nuances, and bookmarks the best one. She emails the original poster to ask if they'd be willing to do a brief interview for her video.

2. **Driving Traffic Back** — Lisa publishes a YouTube video titled "This Reddit-for-AI-Prompts site has the best techniques I've found." The video gets 120,000 views. In the description she links to Meat Brains and the specific post she featured. That week, Meat Brains sees its highest new user registration since launch.
