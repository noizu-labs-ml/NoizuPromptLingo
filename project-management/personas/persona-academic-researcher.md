# Persona: Dr. Maya Chen — Academic Researcher

## Profile
- **Age:** 31
- **Role:** PhD Candidate, Computational Biology
- **Environment:** MacBook Pro, home office + lab, reading 10–20 papers/week
- **Tech Comfort:** High — uses Zotero, Obsidian, terminal tools

## Goals
- Understand dense academic papers without losing context while multitasking
- Connect concepts across multiple papers in a research area
- Quickly locate definitions, methods, and citations within a document
- Build a mental map of how a field's concepts interrelate

## Frustrations
- Standard PDF readers offer no Q&A — she has to tab to ChatGPT constantly
- Can't ask "how does this compare to the Smith et al. paper I read last week?"
- Voice readers mangle mathematical notation and citation text
- Jumping between references breaks reading flow

## Behaviors
- Annotates heavily, but wants annotations to *link* not just highlight
- Listens to papers while commuting or doing lab work
- Often re-reads sections she didn't fully absorb on first pass
- Imports supplemental materials (appendices, cited papers) alongside main doc

## Key Scenarios
1. Listening to a methods section while pipetting — voice-asks "what reagent concentration was used?" and gets a quoted answer
2. Hits an unfamiliar concept, says "explain homology-directed repair" — gets context from the paper itself first, then general knowledge
3. Asks "show me everywhere Section 3 references Figure 2" to verify citation consistency

## Acceptance Criteria (rough)
- Semantic Q&A answers must cite page + paragraph
- Cross-document linking between supplemental PDFs and main doc
- Handles LaTeX-like notation gracefully in TTS (skips or paraphrases formulas)
