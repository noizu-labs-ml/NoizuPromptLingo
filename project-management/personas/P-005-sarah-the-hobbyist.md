# Sarah Kim — The Hobbyist Worldbuilder

| Field | Value |

## Bio

Sarah builds fictional worlds for fun — she's not writing a novel or running a game, she just loves the craft. She's active on r/worldbuilding and r/conlangs, maintains a personal wiki in Obsidian, and has been building the same sci-fi universe for three years. She has 300+ notes but no structured way to check them for consistency. She's price-sensitive and will exhaust the free tier before considering paid.

## Goals

1. Visualize how all the pieces of her universe connect — see the relationships she can't hold in her head
2. Get AI help filling gaps (e.g., "what would the economy of this space station look like given its location?")
3. Share polished universe entries with the r/worldbuilding community

## Frustrations

1. Obsidian has backlinks but no actual knowledge graph visualization with relationship types
2. She spends more time organizing notes than creating new content
3. AI tools generate generic sci-fi tropes instead of material that fits her specific universe's constraints

## Behaviors

- Spends 5-10 hours per week on worldbuilding as a creative hobby
- Posts universe entries on r/worldbuilding for feedback
- Uses Obsidian with 15+ plugins, has tried World Anvil and Campfire
- Consumes worldbuilding YouTube (Artifexian, Hello Future Me)

## Job to Be Done

> "When I've written 300 notes about my universe but can't see how they connect, I want a visual knowledge graph that shows me the relationships and gaps, so I can build deeper instead of wider."

## Relationship to Product

Sarah would discover Knowledge Base through Reddit or worldbuilding YouTube. The knowledge graph visualization is her hook — she's been wanting exactly this. She'd start on the free tier and hit the 50-entry limit quickly. She'd convert to Creator if the graph view and generation quality justify the cost. She'd churn if the free tier is too limited to evaluate the product properly.

## Scenarios

1. **Graph exploration** — Sarah imports her 300 Obsidian notes and explores the knowledge graph for the first time. She discovers that two factions she thought were unrelated actually share three common connections she'd forgotten about.
2. **Gap-filling generation** — Sarah asks the system to generate economic details for a space station, and the output correctly accounts for the station's location near an asteroid belt (from her canon) rather than giving generic space economy boilerplate.
