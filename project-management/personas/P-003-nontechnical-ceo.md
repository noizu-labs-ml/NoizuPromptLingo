# Sandra Okafor — Non-Technical CEO / Founder

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 32–50 |
| **Role** | CEO / Founder |
| **Technical Level** | Novice |
| **Industry** | Professional Services, HealthTech, Legal Tech, Real Estate Tech |
| **Location** | US or UK |

## Bio

Sandra is the CEO of a 15-person company that has quietly built a functional SaaS product using an outsourced development agency. Revenue is growing but she doesn't trust the agency's estimates, can't evaluate their technical decisions, and recently discovered they've locked her into proprietary infrastructure. She has strong business instincts and domain expertise but no technical background — every conversation with her dev team leaves her feeling like she's being managed rather than informed.

## Goals

1. Understand what she actually owns technically and what is at risk
2. Get a plain-language technology strategy aligned with her 12-month business goals
3. Evaluate whether her current development team or agency is the right long-term fit

## Frustrations

1. Engineers speak in jargon she can't parse, making every decision feel like a leap of faith
2. Vendor lock-in discovered too late — switching costs are now enormous and poorly understood
3. No one on her team translates technical decisions into business risk terms she can act on

## Behaviors

- Researches service providers by reading their website and LinkedIn; trusts referrals most
- Makes decisions based on gut fit + credentials, not deep technical evaluation
- Prefers phone/video discovery calls over written RFIs
- Will forward the website to a trusted technical advisor before signing
- Responds to testimonials, named case studies, and clear pricing signals

## Job to Be Done

> "When I feel like I'm flying blind on technology decisions that could sink my company, I want someone who can speak both languages — business and engineering — so I can make informed decisions and stop being at the mercy of people I can't evaluate."

## Relationship to Product

Discovers noizu.com via a warm referral from an investor, lawyer, or peer CEO. The homepage value proposition and the Fractional CTO service page must immediately communicate "I translate business strategy into technical execution" — not "I write Elixir code." The contact form is her primary entry point (she won't fill out a detailed RFI without a trust-building conversation first). Once engaged, the client dashboard must be non-technical by default — milestone names, not sprint velocity metrics.

## Scenarios

1. **Technical vendor evaluation** — Sandra's agency has proposed a $400K platform rebuild; she needs Keith to review the proposal and tell her in plain terms whether it's justified and what she's actually buying
2. **Technology strategy offsite** — Wants a half-day working session to produce a 12-month technology roadmap she can present to her board, written in business language with cost/risk annotations
