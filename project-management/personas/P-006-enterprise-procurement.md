# Christine Holt — Enterprise Procurement Manager

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 38–55 |
| **Role** | Procurement Manager / Vendor Management / IT Sourcing Lead |
| **Technical Level** | Novice |
| **Industry** | Financial Services, Healthcare, Insurance, Government Contractor |
| **Location** | US (major metro) |

## Bio

Christine manages the vendor evaluation and contracting process for a 1,200-person company whose engineering leadership has identified a need for external consulting on a security audit and platform modernization initiative. She doesn't evaluate technical merit herself — that's the engineering team's job — but she owns the process: RFI/RFP administration, vendor qualification, contract compliance, insurance certificates, and ultimately the purchase order. She moves slowly, requires documentation, and needs every box checked before she can advance a vendor.

## Goals

1. Complete vendor qualification for a shortlisted consulting engagement within her quarterly procurement cycle
2. Ensure the vendor meets her company's insurance, compliance, and confidentiality requirements
3. Produce a defensible vendor selection recommendation for her VP of Finance

## Frustrations

1. Small consultancies often lack the documentation infrastructure (W-9, COI, signed NDA templates, references) that enterprise procurement requires
2. Technical consultants who don't understand the procurement process and push back on standard contract terms
3. Engagements that stall because the vendor can't produce credentials or case studies on her timeline

## Behaviors

- Initiates contact via a formal RFI submission — does not call first
- Requires at minimum: company information, insurance certificate, W-9, 3 references, sample SOW
- Evaluates websites for professionalism and credibility markers (About page, credentials, logos)
- Tracks all vendor interactions in a procurement system; expects structured, documented responses

## Job to Be Done

> "When my engineering team has selected a consulting vendor I need to qualify, I want all required documentation and a cooperative, professional engagement process, so I can advance the contract without delays or compliance exceptions."

## Relationship to Product

Arrives via a hand-off from an internal engineering or IT stakeholder who has already vetted Keith technically. The website's professional presentation and the existence of a formal RFI submission workflow are critical conversion factors — a contact form that looks like a startup landing page will stall her process. The RFI dashboard is her primary tool: she needs to see structured submission, confirmation of receipt, and a clear response timeline. Post-engagement, she is unlikely to use the client dashboard herself but may monitor it to verify deliverable milestones.

## Scenarios

1. **Formal RFI submission** — Submits a structured RFI with 12 line-item questions about company structure, insurance coverage, security practices, and reference availability; expects a complete response within 5 business days
2. **Contract negotiation** — Forwards Keith's standard consulting agreement to her legal team; needs him to work with her procurement process rather than insisting on his own contract template
