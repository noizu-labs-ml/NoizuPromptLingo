# James Whitfield — Art Educator

**Type:** Secondary  
**Age:** 52 | **Location:** Edinburgh, UK  
**Occupation:** University lecturer in fine art, watercolor specialist  
**Platform:** iMac M1, classroom projector  

> "My students need to see *why* watercolor blooms happen, not just that they happen."

## Goals

- Demonstrate paint physics in real-time during lectures (flow, capillary action, drying)
- Let students experiment with media behavior without material costs
- Show how canvas properties (cold press vs hot press) affect wash behavior
- Create reproducible demonstrations that work the same way every time

## Frustrations

- Physical demos are one-shot — can't rewind or slow down a backrun forming
- Student access to quality materials is expensive and wasteful for learning
- Existing digital tools don't teach anything about real paint behavior
- Can't show the *process* of drying, only the before and after

## Usage Context

- Classroom demos projected at 1080p, needs to be visually clear at distance
- Would use slow-motion or step-through simulation if available
- Assigns exercises: "paint a graded wash on rough paper, then smooth paper"
- Needs to save and share canvas states as teaching materials

## Key Features He'd Use

- Canvas material properties (absorbency, roughness, porosity)
- Watercolor physics (granulation, backrun, flow dynamics)
- Drying kinetics visualization
- Zoom with canvas texture visibility

## Design Implications

- Simulation parameters should be inspectable (show wetness, flow vectors)
- A "slow simulation" or step-by-step mode would be high value
- Presets for paper types (Arches rough, Fabriano hot press) aid teaching
- Save/load canvas state for repeatable demos
