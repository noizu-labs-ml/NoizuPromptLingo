# Maya Chen — Digital Illustrator

**Type:** Primary  
**Age:** 28 | **Location:** Portland, OR  
**Occupation:** Freelance illustrator (editorial + book covers)  
**Platform:** MacBook Pro M3, Wacom Intuos Pro  

> "I want digital paint that actually *behaves* like paint — not a filter pretending to be watercolor."

## Goals

- Create editorial illustrations with authentic watercolor washes and controlled bleeds
- Layer wet-on-wet and wet-on-dry techniques the way she does on paper
- Export high-resolution work for print production (300 DPI+)
- Reduce the gap between her traditional and digital portfolios

## Frustrations

- Procreate's watercolor brushes look painted-on, not painted-with
- Photoshop's mixer brush has no concept of paper absorbency or drying
- Existing tools fake wetness with opacity; she wants actual flow dynamics
- Can't get backrun effects (pigment migration at wet/dry boundaries) digitally

## Usage Context

- 4-6 hour illustration sessions, often starting with pencil scan underlay
- Needs canvas texture to feel real under transparent washes
- Works in layers but wants paint to interact *across* layers (bleed-through)
- Frequently zooms to pixel level to control edge quality

## Key Features She'd Use

- Watercolor media with granulation and backrun
- Canvas material properties (absorbency, roughness)
- Multi-layer volume with cross-layer interaction
- Zoom + pan with impasto lighting feedback

## Design Implications

- Brush feel and responsiveness are make-or-break (< 16ms latency)
- Needs fine control over wetness, flow, and paper interaction
- Layer panel must surface paint state (wet/dry/depth) not just opacity
- Color mixing must be subtractive/absorptive, not RGB additive
