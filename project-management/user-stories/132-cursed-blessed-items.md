# US-132: Cursed and Blessed Items

**Persona:** Jamie — IF enthusiast (26, sighted, narrative quality)
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** Item Framework & Equipment

## Story
As Jamie, I want cursed and blessed items to emerge through narrative consequence rather than UI flags so that discovering an item's true nature feels like a story beat — not a database lookup.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Cursed items have one or more negative properties from a defined set: cannot unequip (slot-locked), stat drain (reduces one or more stats while worn), compulsion (periodically forces an action), narrative corruption (ambient descriptions darken), enemy attraction (aggressive mobs seek the bearer)
- [ ] Cursed item status is hidden until identification (US-130) or until the curse manifests; first manifestation narrated through the main prose channel: "Something feels wrong with the gauntlet — your hand clenches involuntarily."
- [ ] Blessed items have positive properties beyond their base stats: passive healing, fear aura against undead, luck bonus, or a unique triggered ability with prose narration
- [ ] Blessing and curse status are mutually exclusive per item (an item cannot be both)
- [ ] Curse removal requires a dedicated quest or NPC service (Priests of the Veil offer decursing at significant gold cost); the removal is narrated as a ritual scene
- [ ] Curse removal quests are procedurally seeded: each cursed item of rare tier or above gets a unique associated quest seed; the LLM expands it into a short quest chain on first trigger
- [ ] Players attempting to sell or trade a slot-locked cursed item receive a narrated refusal: "You reach for the ring, but your fingers refuse to obey."
- [ ] Blessed items discovered in consecrated locations have additional lore context explaining the blessing's origin, generated by the LLM with zone context as input

## Notes
The narrative corruption curse type is the most interesting for Jamie: it modifies the prose of the player's surroundings — descriptions become slightly darker, NPCs react with discomfort, environmental details shift. This is purely narrative and fully reversible on decursing. It requires the LLM narrative system to accept a "corruption level" context parameter.

The compulsion curse is the most mechanically disruptive: it periodically inserts a forced action into the player's action queue. For screen reader users this must be announced clearly via ARIA live region before it executes: "The blade pulls your arm — you attack [target] against your will." Forced actions should never target friendly players without an explicit opt-in safety setting (Carol's concern, US-099).

Curse discovery through consequence is the intended experience: players equip the ring because the stats look good, then something feels off, then they piece it together. The identification system (US-130) exists as an out — players who want to avoid the narrative discovery path can pay to know upfront.

The blessing origin lore (AC-8) is an important touch for Jamie: a blessed sword found in a ruined temple should have a blessing that references the temple's history. This requires the LLM to receive zone lore as context, not just the item template. This is a cross-system dependency between the lore database and the item generation pipeline.
