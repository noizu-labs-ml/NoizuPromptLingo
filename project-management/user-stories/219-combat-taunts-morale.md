# US-219: Combat Taunts and Morale

**Persona:** Tyler — MMO refugee, sighted, growth/clans focused
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** Advanced Combat & Tactics

## Story
As Tyler, I want to use taunt abilities to control enemy targeting in group fights and experience NPC morale breaks where enemies lose the will to fight so that combat has a psychological dimension beyond raw damage numbers.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Taunt ability forces 1–3 target enemies to prioritize the taunting player for 2 rounds; affected enemies announced: "The Orc Berserker snarls at you — you have his full attention"
- [ ] Taunt has a resource cost (stamina) and cooldown; cost and cooldown shown in ability menu
- [ ] Morale system tracks each NPC group's morale score; damage, ally deaths, and fear effects reduce morale; inspiring speeches, reinforcements, or home terrain bonus increase it
- [ ] Low morale behavior changes narrated through actions: enemies start retreating to cover, attacking more cautiously, calling for reinforcements, threatening to run
- [ ] Morale break: when morale reaches critical threshold, enemies attempt to flee combat; announced: "The remaining goblins break and run — their courage has fled with their captain"
- [ ] Morale break narration tailored to enemy type: goblins scatter chaotically, mercenaries demand payment before continuing, undead lose animation coherence when morale-equivalent breaks
- [ ] Player can exploit low morale before break with Intimidate ability: forces morale check immediately, potentially causing a break one round early
- [ ] Morale of enemy groups (not precise numbers) conveyed narratively: "The soldiers fight with desperate energy — they know they're losing" vs "The Orc warband presses in with brutal confidence"

## Notes
Tyler's tank-role orientation in MMOs means he naturally gravitates to taunt mechanics. In a text RPG with screen readers, taunt must have clear feedback about which enemies are taunted and for how long — this information can't be conveyed with a visual aggro meter. The morale system adds the psychological layer Tyler finds missing from simple HP-based combat: an enemy that breaks and runs is more satisfying than one that fights to 0 HP every time. Narrative morale expression (conveying state through behavior and prose rather than a number) respects the game's text-first design: "they fight with desperate energy" is more immersive than "Morale: 23%". Different enemy types should have distinct morale break behaviors generated by the LLM — goblin rout narration should feel different from mercenary negotiation. The Intimidate ability gives Tyler an active morale management tool alongside passive taunt, expanding his tactical palette. Morale-related narration should be integrated into the round summary, not a separate announcement, so it reads as organic behavior rather than system state change.
