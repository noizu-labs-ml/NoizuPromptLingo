# US-225: Wilderness Zones

**Persona:** Lena — Tabletop RPG player, sighted, editorial, short sessions
**Priority:** P1
**Epic:** World Depth & Exploration

## Story
As Lena, I want wilderness zones between cities to be genuine gameplay spaces with foraging, wildlife, camping, and varied encounters so that travel feels like part of the adventure rather than a loading screen with narrative text.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Wilderness zones have distinct environmental rules: no safe resting without making camp, wildlife encounters probabilistic based on terrain type, foraging and hunting available as time-consuming actions
- [ ] Each wilderness zone type (forest, desert, mountain, swamp, tundra) has a unique sensory vocabulary used consistently in narration: forest = rustling canopy, damp earth, filtered light-sound; desert = heat shimmer-sound, silence, distant wind
- [ ] Foraging action available in appropriate terrain: success yields herbs, berries, mushrooms with narrated discovery; failure yields nothing or disturbs wildlife
- [ ] Camping: player or party can make camp in wilderness for rest benefits; camp setup narrated, watch system available for party (guards rest, one member watches)
- [ ] Wildlife encounters are not all combat: deer startle and run, wolves circle and test, bears investigate noise then move on — non-combat wildlife resolvable through stillness, noise, or intimidation
- [ ] Wilderness travel narrated as a series of atmospheric vignettes rather than room-by-room descriptions: "An hour passes. The trees thin, and you hear the distant rush of the river you've been following"
- [ ] Random encounter table includes: combat, discovery (abandoned camp, old shrine), NPC meeting (traveling merchant, lost pilgrim), weather event, environmental hazard
- [ ] Wilderness zone danger level communicated narratively on entry: "The locals call this the Greyfen — their voices drop when they mention it" (dangerous) vs "The eastern road through the Millfields is well-traveled and safe" (low danger)

## Notes
Lena's tabletop RPG background means she expects wilderness travel to feel like a hex crawl — meaningful choices, resource expenditure, and narrative texture. The camping system ties directly to US-235 (wilderness survival) and should integrate with it. Non-combat wildlife encounters are a Lena-specific delight: the tense moment of standing still while wolves circle, deciding whether to draw a weapon, is pure tabletop RPG. Sensory vocabulary consistency is the worldbuilding layer: if the Thornwood always smells of pine and damp moss and the Dunemarsh always carries the sound of frogs and sulfur, the world becomes geographically coherent to blind players navigating by narration alone. Danger level communication through NPC dialogue and ambient narration (not a UI element) respects the fiction-first design. The vignette travel format (episodic description rather than room-by-room) is the right choice for wilderness: the space between cities should feel like distance, not a hallway. Lena will appreciate that foraging yields genuine items, not just flavor — the tangible reward reinforces the activity.
