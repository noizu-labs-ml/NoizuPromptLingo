# Lato

## Overview

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Designer** | Łukasz Dziedzic |
| **Foundry/Source** | tyPoland; released via Google Fonts with Google support |
| **Year** | Summer 2010 (designed); December 2010 (published on Google Fonts) |
| **Classification** | Humanist sans-serif |
| **Availability** | Google Fonts (free, SIL Open Font License); self-hostable |

## Character

Lato (Polish for "summer") was conceived as a corporate typeface with hidden warmth. Dziedzic designed it to appear "transparent" in body text — meaning it carries the reader through words without drawing attention to itself — while revealing subtle personality at display sizes. The letterforms use classical proportions in the uppercase and a slightly rounded quality in the details that prevents the coolness typical of grotesque sans-serifs. It balances professionalism and approachability with unusual finesse for a Google Fonts entry.

## Best Use Cases

- Corporate communications and reports
- Website body text and subheadings
- SaaS and B2B product interfaces
- Marketing and landing pages requiring professional friendliness
- Long-form reading in light weights (Lato Light, Lato Regular)

## Tone / Mood

Professional, warm, friendly, reliable. Projects corporate competence without rigidity.

## Demographics

Business and enterprise users, marketing teams, startup founders. Lato's corporate origins give it credibility in professional contexts while its humanist warmth makes it approachable for consumer-facing products. Third most-used Google Font as of 2018 with over 1 billion daily views.

## Notable Users

Numerous corporate websites, marketing agencies, SaaS platforms across Europe and North America. Its corporate-origin story makes it particularly common in professional services, consulting, and technology businesses.

## Pairing Recommendations

- **With serifs:** Merriweather or PT Serif for editorial warmth; Playfair Display for brand contrast
- **With display sans:** Raleway or Montserrat for headings over Lato body text
- **Complementary sans:** Open Sans or Nunito share similar openness for multi-family systems
- **Avoid:** Very geometric display fonts (Futura, Poppins) — the humanist warmth clashes with pure geometry

## Strengths

- Exceptional "invisible" body text performance — readers engage with content, not the typeface
- Classical proportions give authority that purely geometric fonts lack
- Strong weight range (100–900 with italics, 18 total styles)
- Excellent legibility across contexts from business cards to billboards
- Polish origins give subtle distinctions from US/UK typefaces that designers recognize

## Weaknesses

- "Generic professional" perception in some design communities
- Less distinctive than Gill Sans or Frutiger at equivalent sizes
- Heavy weights (Black, Heavy) lack the punch of more geometric alternatives
- Distinctiveness suffers when used as default without modification

## History and Context

Łukasz Dziedzic designed Lato for a large corporate client in summer 2010. When the client changed design direction and abandoned the project, Dziedzic released it publicly under the SIL Open Font License in December 2010 with support from Google. The typeface joined Google Fonts and rapidly became one of the most widely used web fonts on earth. The backstory — a corporate commission set free — lends Lato an interesting dual identity: the professional polish of a commissioned corporate face with the accessibility and open licensing of community projects. By 2018 it ranked third on Google Fonts, behind Roboto and Open Sans.
