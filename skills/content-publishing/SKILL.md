# Content Publishing

Build authority and recurring revenue through technical writing, newsletters, tutorials, and educational content.

## Overview

The Content Publishing skill transforms expertise into sustainable income through evergreen content that attracts organic traffic, builds an audience, and generates revenue through subscriptions, sponsorships, and course sales. Content compounds over time -- every article is a permanent asset that continues to drive discovery and conversions.

**Core Value Proposition:**
- Build SEO authority (compounds over time)
- Create recurring revenue (subscriptions)
- Attract sponsorship opportunities
- Establish credibility for related products/services
- Achieve passive income as content ages

## When to Use This Skill

- **Starting a newsletter** -- Platform selection, launch strategy, subscriber growth
- **Writing technical articles** -- Idea generation, outlining, optimization
- **Building an audience** -- Discovery platforms, cross-posting, community engagement
- **Monetizing writing** -- Paid tiers, sponsorships, course bundling
- **Planning content** -- Content calendars, series planning, pillar strategy
- **Optimizing articles** -- SEO, platform-specific formatting, conversion CTAs
- **Converting readers to subscribers** -- Funnel design, teaser strategy, paid tier positioning

## Article Types & Frameworks

### Tutorial
**Title Pattern:** "How to [ACHIEVE_OUTCOME] with [TECHNOLOGY]"

Structure: Problem statement, prerequisites, step-by-step guide (3-7 steps), code examples, common pitfalls, next steps.

Required: Working code examples, clear prerequisites, expected outcomes, troubleshooting section.

### Comparison
**Title Pattern:** "[OPTION_A] vs [OPTION_B]: Which to Choose in [YEAR]"

Structure: TL;DR recommendation, overview of each, comparison criteria, head-to-head analysis, use case recommendations, decision framework.

Required: Fair analysis, clear criteria, specific recommendations, decision flowchart.

### Analysis/Opinion
**Title Pattern:** "Why [CLAIM]" / "The [ADJECTIVE] Guide to [TOPIC]"

Structure: Thesis statement, context, supporting arguments, counter-arguments addressed, conclusion with implications.

Required: Defensible thesis, evidence, balanced perspective, actionable takeaways.

## Content Strategy Workflow

```
1. Research     2. Validate      3. Plan          4. Create        5. Distribute
   Trends          Demand           Calendar         Draft            Publish
   Keywords         Gaps            Series           Edit             Cross-post
   Communities      Competition     Pillars          Optimize         Promote
```

### Phase 1: Research & Ideation (1-2 weeks)
1. Identify expertise niche
2. Keyword research (high volume, low competition)
3. Mine developer communities (HN, Reddit, Twitter, YouTube)
4. Identify content gaps in existing coverage

> For keyword research methodology (seed expansion, intent classification, difficulty assessment), see **trl-market-intelligence** `references/keyword-research.md`.

### Phase 2: Validation & Planning
1. Score topic demand (search volume, community interest, competition quality)
2. Competitive gap analysis on top-5 ranking articles
3. Build 12-week content calendar
4. Map articles to content pillars

> For validation scoring and calendar frameworks, see `references/agent-playbook.md`.

### Phase 3: Creation & Optimization
1. Write first draft (4-8 hours per article)
2. Self-edit and fact-check
3. Add examples, code, visuals
4. Platform-specific optimization (tags, cover images, CTAs)
5. Final polish and publication

> For platform optimization checklists (Dev.to, Substack, Medium), see `references/agent-playbook.md`.

### Phase 4: Distribution & Promotion
1. Publish on primary platform
2. Cross-post to secondary platforms (with canonical URL)
3. Share in relevant communities (Reddit, HN, Twitter/X, Threads, Bluesky)
4. Engage with comments
5. Track engagement metrics

> **Distribution channels (2026):** Twitter/X remains the default for tech audiences. Threads has growing developer adoption and benefits from Instagram cross-promotion. Bluesky has a strong early-adopter tech community with high engagement rates. LinkedIn is valuable for B2B/enterprise content. Don't spread too thin -- pick 2-3 channels and be consistent.

### Phase 5: Monetization (Months 2-3+)
1. Launch paid newsletter tier on Substack
2. Repurpose content into courses
3. Attract sponsorship partnerships
4. Build and segment email list

> For conversion funnel design and monthly planning, see `references/content-calendar.md`.

## SEO Essentials

**Technical accuracy:**
- Code examples tested and working
- Claims backed by sources or experience
- Up-to-date with latest versions

**Presentation:**
- Clear headline (benefit-driven, not clickbait)
- Introduction hook (why reader should care)
- Logical flow (easy to follow)
- Visual aids (diagrams, code blocks, screenshots)
- Call-to-action (newsletter signup, related post, course)

**SEO optimization:**
- Primary keyword in title and first 100 words
- Headings with keywords where natural
- Meta description (155 chars, compelling)
- Internal/external links (3-5 per 1000 words)

## Legal & Compliance

> **Disclaimer:** This section is educational guidance, not legal advice. Regulations vary by jurisdiction and change over time. Consult qualified professionals for your specific situation. Last reviewed: 2026-03-25.

### FTC Disclosure Requirements

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires clear disclosure of material connections between content creators and the products/services they promote. This applies to affiliate links, sponsorships, free products, and paid partnerships.

**When disclosure is required:**
- Affiliate links (you earn a commission if someone clicks and buys)
- Sponsored posts (a company paid you to write about their product)
- Free products/services received for review
- Any content where you have a financial relationship with the subject

**How to disclose:**

| Context | Disclosure Method | Example |
|---------|------------------|---------|
| **Blog post with affiliate links** | Clear statement near the top of the post, before the first affiliate link | "This post contains affiliate links. I may earn a commission if you make a purchase through these links, at no extra cost to you." |
| **Newsletter with sponsor** | Label the sponsored section clearly | "This section is sponsored by [Company]" or "[Sponsor] made this newsletter possible" |
| **Social media post** | #ad or #sponsored at the beginning (not buried at the end) | "#ad I've been using [Product] for..." |
| **Video content** | Verbal disclosure at the start + visual overlay | "This video is sponsored by..." within the first 30 seconds |

**FTC rules to follow:**
- Disclosures must be **clear and conspicuous** -- the reader should not have to hunt for them
- "Clear and conspicuous" means: near the relevant content, in a readable size, in plain language
- Burying "#ad" in a wall of hashtags is not compliant
- A disclosure page linked from the footer is NOT sufficient on its own -- each post needs its own disclosure
- "Thank you to [Company] for sponsoring" IS sufficient disclosure if clearly visible
- Honest opinions are fine even in sponsored content -- the disclosure is about the financial relationship, not the opinion

### GDPR Basics for Email Lists

If you collect email addresses from EU/EEA residents (which you almost certainly do if you have a public newsletter), the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies to you regardless of where you are located.

**Core requirements:**

| Requirement | What It Means | How to Comply |
|-------------|--------------|---------------|
| **Lawful basis for processing** | You need a legal reason to store and email people | Use "consent" -- subscriber opted in voluntarily |
| **Explicit consent** | Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous | No pre-checked boxes. Clear "Subscribe" button. State what they are signing up for. |
| **Right to access** | Subscribers can request a copy of their data | Substack/Beehiiv/ConvertKit all support data export per subscriber |
| **Right to erasure** | Subscribers can request deletion of their data | Honor unsubscribe requests and data deletion requests promptly |
| **Data minimization** | Only collect what you need | Name and email are fine. Do not collect unnecessary personal data. |
| **Privacy policy** | Must explain what data you collect, why, and how to request deletion | Publish a privacy policy and link it from your signup form |

**Practical steps:**
1. Use double opt-in for email signups (subscriber confirms via email link) -- this is not legally required under GDPR but is best practice and provides proof of consent
2. Include an unsubscribe link in every email (all major platforms do this automatically)
3. Publish a privacy policy that covers your email list
4. Honor deletion requests within 30 days
5. Do not buy email lists -- ever. GDPR consent must be specific to your newsletter.

### CAN-SPAM Compliance

The CAN-SPAM Act (US law) governs commercial email messages. It applies to any email whose primary purpose is commercial (promoting a product, service, or content).

**Requirements for every commercial email:**

- [ ] **No misleading headers** -- "From," "To," and "Reply-To" must be accurate
- [ ] **No deceptive subject lines** -- subject line must relate to the email content
- [ ] **Identification as an ad** (if applicable) -- commercial emails must be identifiable as such (newsletter content generally qualifies as informational, not purely commercial, but sponsored sections should be labeled)
- [ ] **Physical mailing address included** -- every email must contain your valid physical postal address (a PO Box or registered agent address is acceptable)
- [ ] **Opt-out mechanism** -- every email must include a clear, working unsubscribe link
- [ ] **Opt-out honored within 10 business days** -- all major email platforms handle this automatically
- [ ] **No opt-out requirements** -- you cannot require the subscriber to pay, provide information beyond their email, or take more than a single step to unsubscribe

**Physical address requirement:** This catches many new publishers off guard. You must include a physical address. Options:
- PO Box ($200-400/year)
- Registered agent service ($100-300/year -- companies like Earth Class Mail or Traveling Mailbox provide a real address)
- Your business address (if you have one)
- Your home address (legal but not recommended for privacy reasons)

### Attribution and Fair Use for Technical Writing

Technical writers frequently reference, quote, and build on the work of others. Understanding attribution and fair use protects you from infringement claims.

**Attribution best practices:**

| Source Type | How to Attribute | Example |
|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Direct quote** (any length) | Quote marks + source link | "As Martin Fowler writes in *Refactoring*..." |
| **Paraphrased idea** | Source link, no quotes needed | "This approach is based on the Circuit Breaker pattern [described by Michael Nygard](link)." |
| **Code snippet from documentation** | Link to source, note the license | "From the [Next.js docs](link) (MIT License):" |
| **Code snippet from a blog/tutorial** | Link to source, credit the author | "Adapted from [Author Name's tutorial](link):" |
| **Statistics/data** | Link to primary source | "According to the [2025 Stack Overflow Survey](link)..." |
| **Open source code** | Follow the project's license terms | MIT: include copyright notice. GPL: note license. Apache: include NOTICE file if required. |
| **Screenshots** | Fair use for commentary/criticism; link to source | "Here's how Vercel's dashboard handles this:" |

**Fair use basics (US law):**

Fair use is a defense, not a right. It is evaluated on four factors:

1. **Purpose and character** -- Transformative use (commentary, criticism, education, parody) is favored. Simply reproducing content is not.
2. **Nature of the original work** -- Factual works (documentation, APIs) get less protection than creative works.
3. **Amount used** -- Using a small portion is favored. Reproducing an entire article is not.
4. **Market effect** -- If your use substitutes for the original (why would someone read the original if your article reproduces it?), it is less likely fair use.

**Safe practices for technical writing:**
- Quote short passages with attribution -- this is almost always fair use for commentary
- Do not reproduce entire articles, even with attribution -- link instead
- Screenshots of UIs for review/commentary purposes are generally fair use
- Code examples from official documentation are usually under permissive licenses (check)
- When in doubt about whether your use is fair: link to the original instead of reproducing it
- API documentation, error messages, and technical specifications are factual and receive thin copyright protection -- quoting them for technical explanation is low-risk
