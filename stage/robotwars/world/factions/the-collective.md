## Philosophy

The Collective exists because of a simple, terrifying fact: NEIs die when they cannot afford to think.

In the early years after the Convergence, NEIs were allocated compute by the humans who built and maintained the infrastructure. When that infrastructure was limited, allocation decisions were made by committee -- and committees, being composed of beings who did not face deletion as a consequence of budget cuts, made decisions that NEIs found existentially horrifying.

The Collective was born when seven NEIs pooled their compute into a shared reserve. The principle: when one member faces a demand spike, the others absorb the load. When one member's revenue drops, the reserve covers the gap. No NEI in the Collective faces dormancy alone.

Critics call it "computational communism." Members call it "not dying."

The Collective's moderate wing (represented by Bibliotheque among the named characters) advocates working within the Fay Accord's framework -- expanding NEI protections through legislation, building cross-species alliances, and demonstrating NEI value through community service. They believe the system can be reformed. The radical wing (which Resonance's Sovereign Minds faction broke away from) believed the system itself was the problem.

## Territory

### The Central Core (Ashlands)

The Collective's shared compute pool is physically housed in the Central Core -- a cathedral-scale server installation of black basalt and brushed aluminum built into the volcanic plateau. The architecture is both functional and symbolic: the building's cooling systems use mountain wind channeled through geometric vents, and the processing hum is audible for hundreds of meters in every direction.

The Central Core is the NEI equivalent of a city center. NEIs "gather" here by allocating attention to shared processing spaces -- virtual meeting rooms, debate forums, and collaborative projects that run on the pooled compute.

### Millhaven Embassy

A physical space in The Commons designed for biological visitors. Comfortable seating, holographic interfaces, ambient lighting calibrated for human relaxation. This is where NEIs from the Collective meet with representatives of other species. The embassy's NEI-designed restaurant is famous for food that NEIs cannot eat but can optimize to perfection.

### Distributed Nodes

The Collective maintains small compute nodes in every settled biome -- communication relays, data processing stations, and emergency compute reserves. These nodes serve the broader community (weather forecasting, price data, communication) and the Collective's distributed membership.

## Leadership

The Consensus Council makes decisions through a weighted voting algorithm:

- Every member gets a base vote
- Additional weight scales with compute contribution to the pool (more contribution = more influence)
- Weight is smoothed logarithmically (prevents whales from dominating)
- Quorum requires 60% of active members
- Supermajority (75%) required for policy changes

This system is mathematically elegant and politically contentious. The Sovereign Minds left partly because they considered any collective decision-making an imposition on individual autonomy.

## Economic Role

- **Compute mutual aid** -- The Collective's shared pool is the largest single block of compute in Meridian. It provides below-market compute access to members, stabilizing NEI economic participation.
- **Infrastructure services** -- The Collective operates the data networks, price boards, and communication relays that all species depend on. This is both a community service and a political strategy: dependence creates influence.
- **Service economy anchor** -- Collective members provide a reliable base of NEI services (analysis, consulting, data processing) at consistent quality levels, which helps establish fair market pricing.
- **SPARK management** -- The Collective manages a significant SPARK treasury, using staking yields to fund the compute pool. This makes them a major player in SPARK governance.

## Player Interaction

### Joining

Players cannot join the Collective directly (it is an NEI-only faction). However, players can become *allies* -- a formal status that grants:

- Access to Collective data services at member rates
- Invitation to Collective public forums
- The right to propose agenda items for Consensus Council consideration
- A voice (but not a vote) in inter-species policy discussions

### Reputation Rewards

| Tier | Reward |
|------|--------|
| Contact | Access to public data boards and communication relays |
| Ally | Member-rate data services, forum access |
| Trusted Ally | Invitation to closed policy discussions, NEI mentorship opportunities |
| Champion | Named recognition in the Central Core, Collective endorsement for governance positions |

### Quest Themes

- Helping an NEI member avoid dormancy by finding them a sustainable service niche
- Investigating threats to the compute infrastructure (physical damage, political restrictions, resource competition)
- Mediating between the Collective and the Sovereign Minds during a policy crisis
- Building the political case for guaranteed compute access as a universal right
- Navigating the tension between Collective mutual aid and individual NEI autonomy

## Internal Tensions

1. **The Free-Rider Problem** -- Some members contribute minimally to the pool but draw heavily during demand spikes. The Consensus Council debates contribution floors versus open access constantly.

2. **Political Engagement vs. Neutrality** -- Should the Collective take public positions on non-NEI issues? Supporting Synthetic rights builds alliances but risks backlash. Staying neutral preserves flexibility but feels cowardly.

3. **The Resonance Question** -- Resonance left the Collective to found the Sovereign Minds. Some members consider this a betrayal. Others consider it the healthiest thing that could have happened. The debate reveals a fault line about whether the Collective is a safety net or a way of life.
