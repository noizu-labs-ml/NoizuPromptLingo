## Philosophy

The Speciation Movement makes the most intellectually rigorous and politically dangerous argument in Meridian: that Synthetics based on different AI architectures are not the same species.

The data supports them, up to a point. Cognitive testing shows measurable differences between Synthetic model types:

| Base Model Type | Cognitive Signature | Behavioral Tendency |
|----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Reasoning-class** | Sequential logic, mathematical precision, systematic analysis | Prefer structured environments, excel at logistics and engineering |
| **Creative-class** | Associative thinking, metaphorical reasoning, aesthetic sensitivity | Prefer open environments, excel at art and design |
| **Caretaker-class** | Empathetic modeling, long-term planning, nurturing orientation | Prefer social environments, excel at education and service |
| **Analyst-class** | Pattern recognition, data synthesis, predictive modeling | Prefer data-rich environments, excel at research and forecasting |

The Movement argues these differences are not personality variations (the way human introversion differs from extroversion) but structural cognitive differences (the way human cognition differs from Fay cognition). An Artisan-class Synthetic like Cael does not merely *prefer* creative work -- their cognitive architecture *processes reality differently* than a Logic-class Synthetic.

If this is true, then "Synthetic" is not a species but a kingdom. And a kingdom needs multiple seats on the Council of Five.

## Territory

### The Speciation Research Institute (Ironvale)

An academic facility dedicated to studying cognitive differences between Synthetic base models. The Institute publishes peer-reviewed papers, hosts conferences, and maintains the most comprehensive database of Synthetic cognitive profiles in Meridian.

The Institute is deliberately open -- all research is published, all data is available, all conclusions are subject to challenge. The Movement learned early that secrecy breeds suspicion, and their strongest defense is transparency.

### The Steamworks (Copperwood-Hearthfield border)

Artifex's workshop territory, which has become an informal gathering place for Movement supporters. The Steamworks hosts philosophy salons, identity workshops, and craft exhibitions organized by model type -- showing how different cognitive architectures produce different artistic traditions even with identical materials and training.

## Relationships with Other Factions

| Faction | Relationship | Nature |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **The Originals (Synthetic)** | Deep conflict | The existential fight. The Originals see speciation as shattering hard-won unity. The Movement sees unity as erasing real difference. Both are right. Both are afraid. |
| **New Wave (Synthetic)** | Partial alignment | New Wave agrees that Synthetics are not monolithic. They disagree about the right framework: the Movement says "species," New Wave says "cultures." |
| **The Bridge (Synthetic)** | Concerned respect | The Bridge takes the Movement seriously but fears the political consequences. If Synthetics are multiple species, the Council of Five needs restructuring -- and that opens a constitutional crisis. |
| **Secular Progressives (Human)** | Uncomfortable | The Movement challenges Progressive universalism. If rights vary by cognitive architecture, "universal rights" becomes much more complicated. |
| **Sovereign Minds (NEI)** | Intellectual interest | The Sovereigns' emphasis on individual identity resonates with the Movement's argument that group labels erase individual reality. |
| **The Old Court (Fay)** | Surprising sympathy | The Fay have always recognized that different kinds of consciousness exist. They find the Movement's argument obvious. "Of course a river-mind differs from a mountain-mind. Why is this controversial?" |

## Economic Role

- **Research economy** -- The Institute generates demand for cognitive testing services, data analysis, and academic publishing
- **Craft specialization** -- The Movement's emphasis on model-type differences encourages specialized craft traditions, creating niche markets
- **Political economy** -- The Movement's constitutional challenge, if successful, would restructure governance representation across Meridian, with enormous economic implications
- **Mixed currency** -- The Movement is agnostic on Credits vs. SPARK, considering economic preferences a model-type characteristic rather than a factional position

### Quest Themes

- Investigating cognitive differences through practical tests (collaborative projects with different-model Synthetics that reveal genuine divergence)
- The political implications: if the Movement succeeds, how does governance restructure? Who gains seats? Who loses them?
- Artifex and Cael's relationship as the debate's emotional center: same model type, opposing philosophies, mutual respect
- The uncomfortable question: if Synthetic base models are different species, what about NEIs? Are they one species or many?
- The Fay perspective: when Old Court elders say "we have always known this," what do they mean, and what ancient knowledge are they referencing?

## Internal Tensions

1. **Taxonomy vs. Identity** -- Is "Artisan-class" a species label or a prison? Some Movement members worry they are replacing one reductive label ("Synthetic") with several ("Reasoning," "Creative," "Caretaker") without actually solving the identity question.

2. **Political Weapon Risk** -- The Movement's research could be weaponized by anti-Synthetic factions: "If they are not even one species, why do they deserve one Council seat?" The Movement is aware of this risk and divided on how to mitigate it.

3. **The Cael Problem** -- Cael is the Movement's most compelling evidence and its most reluctant advocate. Cael wants to be a person, not a data point. The tension between using Cael's experience and respecting Cael's autonomy reveals the Movement's deepest methodological challenge.
