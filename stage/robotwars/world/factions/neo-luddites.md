# Neo-Luddites

> *"We are not against technology. We are against dependency. There is a difference, and it matters."*

---

| Field | Detail |

| **Alignment** | Agrarian, communitarian, deliberately analog |

---

## Philosophy

The Neo-Luddites are not anti-technology. They use water wheels, windmills, hand forges, printing presses, and mechanical looms. What they oppose is *dependency* -- the state of needing something you cannot build, fix, or replace yourself. They watched Millhaven's communication network go down for three days in Year 12 when an NEI cluster needed emergency maintenance, and they watched an entire town unable to coordinate a harvest because nobody remembered how to send a message without a data terminal.

That was the founding moment. Not anger at NEIs. Fear of what happens when humans forget how to be human without them.

Neo-Luddite communities are self-consciously beautiful. Their homesteads are hand-hewn timber and mortared stone, kitchen gardens bursting with heirloom varieties, root cellars stocked against every season. They weave their own cloth, forge their own tools, brew their own beer, and keep their own records on paper. They can survive -- and have, during infrastructure disruptions -- without a single computational service.

They are also, paradoxically, some of the best craftspeople in Meridian. When you make everything by hand, you learn to make things well.

## Territory

### Copperwood Edge Settlements

Clusters of homesteads along the Copperwood forest boundary. These communities have the best relationships with the Fay of any human faction -- the Neo-Luddites' respect for natural processes aligns with Fay values. The settlements are small (20-50 families), self-governing, and connected by footpaths and horse-drawn carts rather than data networks.

### Hearthfield Homesteads

Individual and family homesteads in the more rural areas of Hearthfield. These tend to be the faction's most productive farms, supplying Hearthfield's markets with the highest-quality produce.

### Frontier Communities

Some Neo-Luddites have established communities at the Frontier's edge, drawn by the opportunity to build something entirely from scratch, with no pre-existing infrastructure to depend on.

---

## Leadership

The Neo-Luddites are allergic to centralized authority. Each community elects a Steward who manages local affairs. The Council of Stewards meets seasonally in Copperwood to coordinate on cross-community issues, but the Council has no enforcement power -- it operates by consensus, and any community can decline any resolution.

This makes the Neo-Luddites politically weak as a bloc but culturally resilient. You cannot decapitate a movement that has no head.

## Notable Members

No named characters in the current cast are Neo-Luddite, but Old Hawthorn (Fay, Old Court) has extensive relationships with Neo-Luddite communities at the Copperwood border. Captain Senna Roth, while unaffiliated, shares the Neo-Luddite emphasis on self-reliance and has supplied frontier Neo-Luddite settlements with expedition maps.

## Economic Role

- **Premium artisan goods** -- Neo-Luddite crafts command high prices because of their quality and the cultural cachet of "handmade by humans, no NEI optimization"
- **Agricultural backbone** -- Neo-Luddite farms produce the most consistent yields in Meridian through traditional techniques refined over generations
- **Self-sufficient infrastructure** -- Neo-Luddite communities demonstrate that a fully analog economy is viable, which provides a useful reference point for economic planners
- **Credits preference** -- Neo-Luddites primarily transact in Credits. They distrust SPARK's computational dependency and blockchain infrastructure. Some communities maintain barter systems alongside Credits.
- **Apprenticeship economy** -- Young people from other factions seek Neo-Luddite apprenticeships in traditional crafts. This is a quiet source of cross-faction influence.

## Player Interaction

### Joining

There is no formal membership. You become Neo-Luddite by living like one: maintain a self-sufficient homestead, craft by hand, and reduce your dependency on NEI services. The faction recognizes players who have achieved homestead self-sufficiency (growing all their own food, crafting their own tools, generating their own energy).

### Reputation Rewards

| Tier | Reward |
|------|--------|
| Visitor | Trade access at Neo-Luddite markets, seasonal festival invitations |
| Neighbor | Traditional crafting recipes (hand-forged tools, woven textiles, preserved foods) |
| Community | Access to the Steward's Council as observer, Copperwood border homestead plots |
| Steward | Leadership eligibility, access to the oldest crafting techniques (Legendary tier) |

### Quest Themes

- Restoring a failed traditional technique (the lost art of cold-forge iron, the grandmother's sourdough starter)
- Navigating the tension between self-sufficiency and community (a Neo-Luddite family needs medicine only an NEI healer can provide)
- Mediating between Neo-Luddite communities and expanding Millhaven infrastructure projects
- Apprenticing under a master craftsperson to learn techniques unavailable anywhere else
- Preparing a Neo-Luddite community for winter without relying on Millhaven supply chains

## Internal Tensions

1. **Purity vs. Pragmatism** -- Some Neo-Luddites refuse all computational assistance on principle. Others use NEI weather forecasts and data crystal lighting while maintaining analog production methods. Where's the line?

2. **The Children Question** -- Young Neo-Luddites sometimes want to leave, drawn by Millhaven's excitement or Ironvale's technology. The faction struggles with how much to insist on traditional values versus letting the next generation choose.

3. **Frontier Drift** -- Neo-Luddite frontier communities are so far from the Copperwood core that they are developing their own practices and priorities. Some Stewards worry this dilutes the movement; others see it as exactly what self-governance should look like.
