## Description

Threadweaver Spiders produce the finest natural fiber in Meridian -- Fay silk. Their webs are extraordinary: nearly invisible in daylight, they catch dew at dawn and moonlight at dusk, creating shimmering curtains that mark the boundaries between Copperwood's paths and its deep places. The silk is stronger than steel wire of equivalent thickness, naturally waterproof, and can be dyed to hold colors that no other fiber retains.

Threadweavers are not solitary spiders. They build in cooperative colonies of 20-50 individuals, creating web networks that span entire clearings. The webs serve dual purposes: catching insects for food and (the Fay claim) communicating information about forest conditions through vibration patterns. A Fay who can read a Threadweaver web knows what has passed through the area in the last 24 hours.

The spiders have a long co-evolutionary relationship with the Fay. Threadweavers build preferentially near Fay settlements, and the Fay maintain the canopy structures that support web-building. In return, the Fay harvest excess silk from abandoned or rebuilt webs without disturbing the colonies. This relationship is so old that the spiders recognize Fay by their magical resonance and will not bite them.

## Drops and Resources

|----------|--------|--------|-----|

| **Web pattern data** | Observation and documentation of web structures | Common | Exploration data; web patterns record animal movement and forest conditions |

**Important:** Harvesting silk from active webs or disturbing colonies is a Fay territorial violation. The Respect Mechanic applies: take only what is offered, ask when unsure.

## Economic Role

- **Luxury textile** -- Fay silk is the most valuable natural fiber in Meridian, commanding premium prices in Millhaven's markets
- **Fay trade anchor** -- Silk is the primary trade good through which Copperwood's economy interfaces with the broader Meridian market
- **Information service** -- Web pattern reading is a specialized skill that explorers and the Fay offer as a paid service
- **Artisan material** -- The most skilled weavers in Meridian (often Neo-Luddites or Fay-trained humans) produce Fay silk garments that are both functional and deeply beautiful

## Behavior

| Behavior | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Web construction** | Nightly rebuilding. Each spider replaces its web section every 2-3 days. Old silk is consumed and recycled. |
| **Colony coordination** | Colonies communicate through web vibrations, coordinating web placement and insect capture |
| **Fay recognition** | Spiders do not flee from Fay presence and allow Fay to harvest excess silk without defensive behavior |
| **Threat response** | Non-Fay who touch active webs trigger vibration alerts. The spider will investigate and, if the disturbance continues, deliver a warning bite. |
| **Seasonal cycle** | Largest webs in autumn (peak prey availability). Winter: reduced colony size, silk production drops. Spring: colony expansion, new web sites established. |
