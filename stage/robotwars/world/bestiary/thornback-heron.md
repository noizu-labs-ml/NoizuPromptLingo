## Description

Thornback Herons are tall, elegant wading birds with plumage that shifts between dark teal and deep purple depending on lighting conditions. Their name comes from a ridge of thorn-like feather quills running along their spinal crest -- decorative rather than defensive, these quills are displayed during courtship and territorial disputes.

In bioluminescent light, Thornback Herons are extraordinary. Their plumage reflects Thornmere's ambient glow, creating a silhouette edged in blue-green fire as they stand motionless in the shallows, waiting for fish. They are the most photographed (and painted, and sculpted) creature in Thornmere.

Thornbacks are solitary hunters and aggressive competitors. They claim fishing territories (a stretch of waterway roughly 50 meters long) and will drive away other herons -- and fishers of any species -- who attempt to fish their territory. This creates minor friction with human and Synthetic fishing operations, particularly along Millhaven's rivers during spawning season.

The Fay consider Thornbacks semi-sacred: they have inhabited Thornmere since before human settlement and are considered part of the swamp's ecological identity. Interfering with Thornback nesting sites is a pact violation.

---

## Economic Role

- **Fishing competition** -- Thornback territorial behavior creates genuine economic friction with commercial fishers, driving political discussions about wildlife management vs. species protection
- **Craft material** -- Thornback quills are premium writing instruments, naturally water-resistant and flexible
- **Cultural symbol** -- The Thornback's patient silhouette is a Thornmere icon, used in local signage, art, and merchandise
- **Ecological service** -- Thornbacks control fish populations, preventing overpopulation in Thornmere's waterways

## Behavior

| Behavior | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Still hunting** | Stands motionless for up to 30 minutes, then strikes with blinding speed to catch fish. Patience incarnate. |
| **Territory defense** | Raises spinal quills, spreads wings, and produces a deep croaking call. Will physically jab at intruders with beak. |
| **Bioluminescent fishing** | Uses Thornmere's ambient glow to attract prey; positions itself so its shadow creates a contrast zone where fish congregate |
| **Migration** | Some Thornbacks migrate to Brightsand Coast for winter, following fish populations. Others remain in Thornmere year-round. |
| **Courtship** | Paired male-female displays involving synchronized quill-raising, wing-spreading, and elaborate wading dances. Occurs in spring. |

## Narrative Hooks

- A Thornback has claimed a fishing territory that overlaps with a human fishing family's traditional grounds. Both have legitimate claims. The Wild Hunt considers this a matter for mediation, not enforcement.
- Thornback quills used as writing implements carry a faint bioluminescent residue that glows in Thornmere's dark. A letter written in Thornback quill ink becomes a beautiful artifact.
- Thornback migration patterns have shifted in recent years -- more birds are wintering in Thornmere rather than migrating to the coast. The Fay consider this a sign that something in the swamp is changing.
