## Description

Hearthstone Beetles are sturdy, iridescent insects with shells that shift between warm bronze, deep copper, and forest green depending on the angle of light. They are the workhorses of Hearthfield's soil ecosystem -- constantly burrowing, aerating soil, breaking down organic matter, and leaving behind castings that are among the best natural fertilizers in Meridian.

Each beetle maintains a small burrow territory (roughly 1 square meter) that it defends against other beetles through elaborate horn-butting displays that are more theatrical than violent. The displays are a minor spectator attraction for new players -- two beetles facing off, clicking mandibles, and pushing each other in slow, dignified circles until one decides the territory is not worth the effort and trundles away.

Beetles are completely indifferent to players and will continue their burrowing activities regardless of what is happening around them. This includes walking across player homestead gardens, which they improve by their passage.

## Drops and Resources

| Resource | Method | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|--------|-----|
| **Beetle casting** | Gathered from burrow entrances (beetle produces naturally) | Common | Premium fertilizer; increases crop quality by 0.5-1 star |
| **Shed shell fragment** | Found near burrows during molting season (spring) | Uncommon | Iridescent crafting material for jewelry, decoration, dye base |
| **Beetle wax** | Secreted on burrow walls; collect from abandoned burrows | Uncommon | Waterproofing agent, polish, alchemical ingredient |

---

## Economic Role

- **Agricultural foundation** -- Beetle-enriched soil produces higher-quality crops. Experienced farmers actively encourage beetle populations in their gardens.
- **Crafting material** -- Shed shell fragments are the first uncommon crafting material most players encounter, used for basic jewelry and iridescent dyes.
- **Soil assessment** -- Like Meadow Drifters, beetle population density indicates soil health. Their absence signals problems.

---

## Behavior

| Behavior | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Burrowing** | Constant. Beetles tunnel through topsoil, aerating and enriching it. |
| **Territory display** | Two beetles meeting at territory boundaries perform horn-butting rituals. Harmless, entertaining. |
| **Molting** | Spring event. Beetles shed old shells and grow larger ones. Shed shells are iridescent and collectible. |
| **Rain response** | Beetles surface during warm rain, forming slow processions across open ground. Neo-Luddites consider this good luck. |

## Narrative Hooks

- A farmer's beetle population has suddenly migrated away. Investigation reveals underground disturbance from a construction project.
- Beetle shell dyes can be mixed to create unique color palettes -- a crafting quest with aesthetic payoff.
- The Fay maintain that very old beetles (10+ years) develop a rudimentary awareness. The scientific community is skeptical. The beetles are not available for comment.
