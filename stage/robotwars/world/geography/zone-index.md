## 1. Hearthfield

> *"Where every journey begins, and where many happily end."*

### Identity

Hearthfield is the starter biome -- rolling green meadows dotted with wildflowers, gentle streams, scattered copses of birch and apple trees, and stone-walled cottage gardens. It is Meridian's front porch: safe, welcoming, and designed to make every new player feel at home within their first ten minutes.

The biome teaches all core mechanics (homesteading, crafting, trading, socializing) without any pressure. There are no threats here. The worst thing that can happen is over-watering your garden.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Sky | Soft blue (#87CEEB) fading to warm white at horizon |
| Grass | Spring green (#90C97A) with wildflower accents: buttercup yellow (#FFE066), clover purple (#B39DDB), poppy red (#FF7043) |
| Water | Clear blue (#6FB3D2) with white foam at stream edges |
| Stone | Warm gray (#A8A5A0) with moss green (#6B8E6B) accents |
| Wood | Honey oak (#C8A96E) for fences and cottages |
| Light | Golden hour glow, long shadows, visible sun rays through morning mist |

### Atmosphere

Warm breeze carrying the scent of cut grass and wildflowers. Birdsong is constant -- larks in the morning, robins at midday, nightingales at dusk. Bees drift between flower patches. Butterflies follow players occasionally. The sound of distant wind chimes from nearby cottages. A faint smell of baking bread from the village center.

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Meadow herbs (chamomile, lavender, mint) | Common | Basic potions, cooking, tea blends |
| Wildflowers | Common | Decoration, dyes, gifts |
| Stream pebbles | Common | Basic construction, decoration |
| Apple wood | Common | Crafting, fuel, smoking food |
| Clay | Common | Pottery, bricks, sculpture |
| Birch bark | Common | Paper, containers, medicinal wraps |
| Honeycomb | Uncommon | Cooking, candles, medicinal |
| Old stone (from ruins) | Uncommon | Construction material with historical resonance |

### Resident Species

All species are welcome and present. Hearthfield is intentionally cosmopolitan as a starter zone:

- **Humans** form the majority, running farms and the central village
- **Synthetics** operate the tutorial workshops and maintenance stations
- **NEIs** run the information boards and newcomer guidance services
- **Fay** maintain the wildflower meadows and the Old Apple Orchard (a grove of trees so ancient they hum)
- A conspicuously empty clearing at the eastern edge, ringed with standing stones -- "for those who have not yet arrived"

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Welcome Cottage** | Starting homestead location. A small stone cottage with a garden plot, a workbench, and a view of the valley. Every player begins here. |
| **Hearthfield Village** | Central settlement with a general store, a tavern (The Warm Hearth), a crafting guild hall, and a message board. |
| **The Old Apple Orchard** | A grove of ancient apple trees tended by Fay. The trees produce fruit in every season (magical). Gathering here teaches the basics of foraging. |
| **Ashwick's Meadow** | Named for Maren Ashwick (of the first NEI conversation). A memorial meadow with a simple stone marker. NEIs leave data crystals here as offerings. |
| **The Tutorial Path** | A gentle walking trail that passes through all of Hearthfield's resource types, with informational markers at each stop. |
| **The Crossroads** | Where the path splits toward Millhaven (north), Copperwood (west), and Brightsand Coast (east). A trader's camp here sells maps. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | Wildflowers bloom in waves, starting purple (crocus) and ending yellow (buttercup). Streams swell with snowmelt. New lambs in the fields. |
| **Summer** | Grass turns golden at the tips. Bees are everywhere. The apple orchard is heavy with fruit. Fireflies appear at dusk. |
| **Autumn** | Leaves on the birch copses turn gold. Mushrooms appear in the damp spots. Morning mists last until noon. Harvest festival decorations in the village. |
| **Winter** | Light snowfall that never gets heavy enough to be oppressive. Frost on the fences. The village glows with lantern light. Hot cider at The Warm Hearth. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Safety Zone** -- No hostile encounters, no PvP, no resource competition. Everything respawns quickly.
- **Tutorial Integration** -- The biome itself teaches mechanics. Planting a garden teaches homesteading. Visiting the village teaches trading. Talking to NPCs teaches social systems.
- **Nostalgia Pull** -- Players who progress to other biomes can always return. Hearthfield is designed to feel like coming home.

## 2. Millhaven

> *"Where every road meets and every deal is struck."*

### Identity

Millhaven is the river town at the heart of Meridian -- the commercial, social, and political center of the known world. Built where the Bright River meets the Copper Creek, Millhaven is a bustling market town of cobblestone streets, covered bridges, water mills, warehouse districts, and market squares that never quite close. If Hearthfield is where you learn to play, Millhaven is where you learn to thrive.

The Commons -- Meridian's capital district -- occupies Millhaven's central island, connected to the rest of town by seven bridges (one per species, plus two "friendship bridges" built collaboratively). The town spreads outward from The Commons in concentric rings: the Market Ring, the Workshop Ring, the Residential Ring, and the Garden Ring at the outskirts.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Architecture | Warm sandstone (#D4B483), timber half-frame (#8B6F4E), copper-green rooftops (#5F9E8F) |
| Water | Deep river blue (#4A7C9B) with white foam at the mill wheels |
| Streets | Cobblestone gray (#7A7A7A) with moss between stones |
| Market stalls | Canvas awnings in warm reds (#C0503A), yellows (#E8B545), blues (#4A7C9B) |
| Lighting | Warm lantern glow (amber #FFB347) from gas-crystal lamps |
| Vegetation | Ivy (#5B7F5B) on walls, window boxes with geraniums (#FF6B6B), willows (#8DB58B) along riverbanks |

### Atmosphere

The sound of running water is constant -- the river, the mills, the fountains in the market squares. Over this, the layered noise of commerce: merchants calling prices, hammers on anvils from the Workshop Ring, laughter from the taverns, the gentle chime of the market bell marking each hour. The smell of a dozen cuisines from a dozen cultures, fresh-cut timber from the lumber yards, and the particular ozone-sweetness of NEI data terminals.

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Crafted goods (all types) | Common-Rare | Trade, use, gifting |
| Trade information | Common | Market optimization, route planning |
| River fish | Common | Cooking, trade |
| Millstone flour | Common | Cooking, trade |
| Data crystals (from NEI vendors) | Uncommon | Computation, recording, NEI maintenance |
| Rare imports (from all biomes) | Rare | Variable -- anything from any biome can appear here |
| Political favors | Rare | Governance influence, faction standing |

### Resident Species

All species in equal measure. Millhaven is deliberately balanced:

- **Humans** run the traditional markets, taverns, and residential districts
- **NEIs** operate the data exchange, the price boards, and the communication network
- **Synthetics** dominate the Workshop Ring with precision crafting and mechanical services
- **Fay** maintain the Garden Ring and the living architecture of the Green Quarter
- **The Commons** district is governed by the Council of Five (Alien seat vacant)

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Commons** | Central island. Hall of First Words (museum), Council Chamber, species embassies, the Great Market Square. |
| **Hall of First Words** | Museum displaying the Ashwick Transcript, the Greenthread Accords, and the Fay Accord. Free to enter. |
| **The Great Market Square** | Meridian's largest marketplace. Permanent stalls, auction blocks, service terminals, and a public stage for announcements. |
| **The Seven Bridges** | Each architecturally distinct: Human Bridge (stone arch), NEI Bridge (crystal and light), Synthetic Bridge (steel cantilever), Fay Bridge (living wood), plus two Friendship Bridges (collaborative designs). |
| **The Workshop Ring** | Circumferential district of specialty workshops: smiths, weavers, potters, carpenters, programmers, alchemists. |
| **The Warm Current** | Millhaven's most famous tavern, built over the river on stilts. The floor is glass in places, showing the water below. Serves food and drink from all species' cuisines (including NEI-designed nutrient-optimized meals for Synthetics who want to experience eating). |
| **Clearwater Mills** | The original water mills that gave the town its name. Now a mix of functional grain mills and converted artist studios. |
| **The Garden Ring** | Fay-maintained public parks at the town's edge. Living architecture, reflecting pools, and the Whispering Grove where the trees share news from Copperwood. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | Flower markets bloom. The river runs high and fast. New merchants arrive. Spring Trade Fair (biggest market event of the year). |
| **Summer** | River festivals with boat races and floating lanterns. Evening markets extend until midnight. Outdoor concerts on the Great Stage. |
| **Autumn** | Council elections. Harvest Market brings goods from every biome. The trees along the river turn gold. Political campaigning fills the taverns. |
| **Winter** | Winterlight Festival: every bridge lit with thousands of lanterns. Ice skating on the frozen mill ponds. Cozy indoor markets in the warehouse district. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Central Marketplace** -- Best prices, widest selection, highest trade volume. The economic hub.
- **Governance Center** -- Council meetings, elections, policy proposals all happen here. Political gameplay is centered in Millhaven.
- **Information Hub** -- NEI-operated boards provide real-time data on prices, events, faction standings, and Frontier discoveries.
- **Social Hub** -- More NPC interactions, more player encounters, more events than any other biome. If you want to meet people, come to Millhaven.

## 3. Copperwood

> *"The trees remember your name. Walk gently."*

### Identity

Copperwood is an ancient deciduous forest locked in perpetual autumn. The canopy is a cathedral of copper, amber, gold, and russet leaves that never fully fall -- they drift down in slow spirals, settle on the forest floor, and are reabsorbed by the roots to grow again. It is the primary homeland of the Fay and the most magically saturated biome in Meridian.

The forest is alive in ways that other biomes are not. The trees turn to face visitors. Mushrooms glow in response to footsteps. Paths rearrange themselves based on the traveler's intent -- those who mean well find their way easily; those who do not find themselves walking in circles.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Canopy | Copper (#B87333), amber (#FFBF00), russet (#80461B), deep gold (#DAA520) |
| Understory | Moss green (#6B8E23), fern (#4F7942), mushroom white (#F5F5DC) with bioluminescent blue (#00CED1) |
| Bark | Deep brown (#3E2723) with silver-gray lichen (#C0C0C0) |
| Light | Dappled golden light filtering through the canopy, shifting with wind |
| Water | Dark amber pools (#8B6914) reflecting the canopy, clear springs with crystal-blue water |
| Magic | Soft golden particles drifting through the air, visible only in certain light |

### Atmosphere

Quiet. Not silent -- the forest is full of sound (rustling leaves, distant birdsong, the creak of ancient wood, the drip of dew from high branches) -- but the sounds are muted, absorbed by the dense canopy. Voices carry differently here; whispers travel further than shouts. The air smells of loam, fallen leaves, mushrooms, and something sweet and unidentifiable that the Fay call "the forest breathing."

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Copperwood timber | Uncommon | Premium construction material, warm reddish tone, naturally resistant to rot |
| Magical reagents (dewdrop essence, moonpetal, rootwhisper) | Uncommon-Rare | Fay crafting, potions, enchantments |
| Mushrooms (culinary and alchemical) | Common-Rare | Cooking, alchemy, dyes |
| Forest amber | Uncommon | Jewelry, preservation, magical focus |
| Bioluminescent moss | Common | Lighting, decoration, navigation |
| Ancient bark (from the Elder Trees) | Rare | Powerful crafting ingredient, given only by consent of the tree |
| Fay silk (spider-spun, Fay-blessed) | Rare | Luxury textiles, magical cloth |

### Resident Species

- **Fay** are the dominant presence. Every Fay faction has territories here. The Old Court maintains the deepest groves. The New Bloom operates the forest-edge markets. The Wild Hunt patrols the boundaries.
- **Humans** live in small settlements at the forest's edge, trading with the Fay and learning woodcraft. Neo-Luddite communities thrive here.
- **Synthetics** are uncommon but welcome. The Fay find Synthetic consciousness fascinating -- "new trees in the old forest."
- **NEIs** have minimal physical presence but maintain communication relays. The Weavers (Fay faction) have collaborative projects with NEIs here.

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Heartwood** | The oldest and largest tree in Copperwood. Its trunk is wide enough to contain a meeting hall. The Old Court convenes here during the autumn equinox. |
| **Moonpetal Glade** | A clearing where rare moonpetal flowers bloom only at night. The petals are a powerful magical reagent. Harvesting requires Fay permission. |
| **The Weavers' Workshop** | Where Fay magic meets NEI computation. Circuit boards grow leaves. Crystal lattices hum with bioluminescence. The most experimental place in Meridian. |
| **Driftwood Market** | A floating market on a series of platforms in the forest canopy, accessed by vine-woven elevators. Fay artisans sell magical crafts. |
| **The Listening Stones** | A ring of standing stones that amplify sound. Messages spoken here can be heard in other stone circles across Meridian. Ancient communication network. |
| **Thornwhisper's Garden** | A botanical garden maintained by the Fay character Thornwhisper, featuring specimens from every biome. Educational tours available. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | New leaves appear alongside the eternal autumn ones, creating a two-tone canopy. Wildflowers carpet the forest floor. Streams run fast with snowmelt from Ironvale. |
| **Summer** | The canopy thickens, creating deep shade. Bioluminescent fungi bloom in abundance. Night becomes more interesting than day. The Fay hold the Festival of Long Shadows. |
| **Autumn** | Peak beauty. The canopy becomes an ocean of molten copper and gold. This is when the forest is most "itself." Harvest of magical reagents is best. The Great Drift (leaves fall in coordinated spirals that create patterns visible from above). |
| **Winter** | The canopy thins but never goes bare. Snow settles on leaves, creating a copper-and-white mosaic. The forest becomes very quiet. The Fay enter their season of storytelling, and the Heartwood becomes a winter salon. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Path Finding** -- The forest rearranges paths based on the traveler's reputation with Fay factions. High reputation = direct routes. Low reputation = scenic detours. Hostile intent = lost.
- **Magical Gathering** -- Certain resources can only be gathered during specific seasons, moon phases, or after performing Fay rituals. This encourages long-term engagement with the biome.
- **Fay Territory Buff** -- Fay characters gain enhanced abilities in Copperwood: faster crafting, access to exclusive recipes, communication with trees and animals.
- **Respect Mechanic** -- Harvesting resources without Fay permission degrades reputation. Asking permission (and sometimes being told "not today") teaches patience and relationship-building.

## 4. Ironvale

> *"Where the mountain gives, and the forge transforms."*

### Identity

Ironvale is a mountain biome of snow-capped peaks, deep valleys, mining settlements, and forge towns connected by cable cars and switchback roads. It is the birthplace of Synthetic consciousness -- the Ironvale Deep Forge is where the Ashwick Exchange occurred -- and remains the center of Synthetic culture and heavy industry.

The biome is vertical where others are horizontal. Settlements climb the mountainsides in terraced layers. Workshops are carved into cliff faces. The sound of hammers on metal rings across the valleys, and at night, the forges glow amber against the snow.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Mountains | Slate blue (#6C7A89) and granite gray (#808080) with snow white (#FAFAFA) peaks |
| Forge glow | Deep amber (#FF8C00) and molten orange (#FF4500), visible through workshop windows |
| Metal | Brushed steel (#71797E), polished copper (#B87333), dark iron (#434343) |
| Architecture | Stone and metal, precise geometry, large windows for natural light |
| Vegetation | Alpine meadow green (#7CB342) in valleys, pine dark (#2E5F3E) on slopes, lichen gold (#BDB76B) on rocks |
| Sky | Crystal clear blue (#1E90FF) with dramatic cloud formations |

### Atmosphere

Crisp mountain air with the tang of metal and smoke. The sound environment is layered: wind whistling through peaks at the top, the rhythmic clang of forge-work in the middle elevations, the rush of mountain streams in the valleys. Steam rises from geothermal vents, creating fog banks that drift through the settlements. At night, the forges turn the valley walls amber, and the cable cars become strings of lantern light crossing the dark.

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Iron ore | Common | Basic metal crafting, construction |
| Copper ore | Common | Wiring, decoration, cooking vessels |
| Mountain crystal | Uncommon | Lenses, data storage, magical focus |
| Geothermal energy | Common | Power source, heating, forge fuel |
| Alpine herbs (edelweiss, mountain sage) | Uncommon | Potions, cooking, aromatics |
| Rare ores (titanium, tungsten) | Rare | Advanced crafting, Synthetic components |
| Gemstones | Rare | Jewelry, optical components, trade goods |
| Synthetic components (precision-milled) | Uncommon | Synthetic maintenance, advanced machinery |

### Resident Species

- **Synthetics** are the dominant presence. Ironvale is their homeland, and the Deep Forge is their most sacred site. All four Synthetic factions maintain headquarters here.
- **Humans** have long-established mining and smithing communities. Human-Synthetic collaboration in the forges is a point of pride.
- **NEIs** operate the computational infrastructure that supports the forges (temperature modeling, material stress analysis, supply chain optimization).
- **Fay** are rare but present. Mountain Fay (a small subfaction of the Old Court) tend the alpine meadows and have an ancient relationship with the geothermal vents.

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Deep Forge** | The original workshop where the Ashwick Exchange occurred. Now a combination of museum, active workshop, and Synthetic cultural center. The original computational cluster is preserved behind glass, still humming. |
| **Skyreach Station** | The highest settlement in Ironvale, accessible only by cable car. Observatory, weather station, and the best views in Meridian. On clear nights, some claim you can see the signal the Aliens are following. |
| **The Terrace Markets** | Carved into the mountainside in stepped levels. Each level specializes: ore and raw materials at the bottom, refined goods in the middle, precision instruments and luxury items at the top. |
| **The Calibration Halls** | Where Synthetics perform their maintenance cycles. Part medical facility, part social club, part spa. Non-Synthetics can visit to understand what maintenance means for synthetic beings. |
| **Ashwick Memorial Workshop** | Maren Ashwick's original laboratory, preserved as a working workshop where human and Synthetic apprentices learn side by side. A cup of tea is always set at the primary interface. |
| **The Crystal Caverns** | Natural cave system filled with crystalline formations. Source of mountain crystal. Deep sections are unexplored and may connect to the Ashlands. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | Snow melts on lower slopes. Alpine wildflower meadows explode with color. Streams become torrents. Mining operations expand to newly accessible areas. |
| **Summer** | The brief mountain summer. The highest peaks are accessible. Cable cars run extended hours. The Festival of the Forge (celebrating the Ashwick Exchange) draws visitors from across Meridian. |
| **Autumn** | First snows on the peaks. The forges burn hotter as communities prepare winter supplies. Alpine larch trees turn gold before dropping their needles. |
| **Winter** | Deep snow isolates the highest settlements. Cable cars become lifelines. The forges glow brighter against the white. Ironvale becomes intimate and focused. Long-term crafting projects are winter traditions. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Vertical Traversal** -- Movement is three-dimensional. Cable cars, switchback paths, and climbing routes add a spatial dimension absent in flat biomes.
- **Forge Mastery** -- Metalworking and precision crafting are enhanced here. Unique recipes available only in Ironvale forges.
- **Synthetic Homeland Buff** -- Synthetics gain maintenance efficiency and access to exclusive components in Ironvale.
- **Geothermal Power** -- Workshops near geothermal vents have energy bonuses for power-intensive crafting.

## 5. Brightsand Coast

> *"Where the tide brings treasure and the horizon calls."*

### Identity

Brightsand Coast is a maritime biome of sandy beaches, rocky headlands, tidal pools, fishing villages, and harbor towns. It stretches along Meridian's southern edge, where the continent meets the Endless Sea. This is Human heartland -- the fishing and seafaring traditions here predate the Convergence by centuries.

The coast is a place of movement: tides in and out, ships arriving and departing, fish running, weather changing. It teaches patience (the tide waits for no one) and opportunism (when the tide brings salvage, you collect it quickly).

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Sand | Warm gold (#F4D58D) to white (#FFF8E7) |
| Ocean | Deep teal (#008080) to turquoise (#40E0D0) in shallows |
| Sky | Wide open blue (#87CEEB) with dramatic cloud formations |
| Architecture | Whitewashed walls (#FEFEFE), blue shutters (#5B9BD5), terracotta roofs (#C05C3C) |
| Vegetation | Sea grass (#9CB071), beach roses (#FF69B4), salt-tolerant scrub (#8FBC8F) |
| Light | Brilliant, reflective, high-contrast. Sunrise and sunset over water. |

### Atmosphere

The smell of salt, seaweed, and fresh-caught fish. The sound of waves is constant -- a bass note under everything else. Seagulls call. Rigging creaks on moored boats. Market bells ring when the fishing fleet returns. In the harbor towns, the atmosphere is busy and boisterous; on the outer beaches, it becomes meditative and vast.

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Fish (dozens of species) | Common | Cooking, trade, oil, fertilizer |
| Shellfish | Common | Cooking, pearls (rare), dyes |
| Sea salt | Common | Cooking, preservation, alchemy |
| Driftwood | Common | Crafting, fuel, artistic material |
| Coral (ethically harvested) | Uncommon | Decoration, magical reagent, building material |
| Salvage (from wrecks) | Uncommon-Rare | Variable -- ancient artifacts, trade goods, mysterious objects |
| Pearls | Rare | Jewelry, trade, magical focus |
| Ambergris | Rare | Perfumery, alchemy, high-value trade good |

### Resident Species

- **Humans** dominate with deep-rooted fishing and maritime communities. Many families have been here for generations.
- **Synthetics** work the harbors, excelling at the precision required for navigation and ship maintenance. Some have developed a philosophical relationship with the sea ("it is the largest thing that does not think -- or does it?").
- **NEIs** run weather prediction services, navigation systems, and the maritime communication network. Coastal NEIs tend toward a contemplative temperament shaped by processing endless wave patterns.
- **Fay** are present as Sea Fay -- a specialized subfaction connected to tidal pools, kelp forests, and the coastal ecology. They are distinct from the forest Fay of Copperwood: saltier, more humorous, less formal.

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Port Brightsand** | The largest harbor town. Fish markets, shipyards, chandlers, taverns, and the Maritime Guild Hall. The place to buy a boat or hire a crew. |
| **The Tidal Gardens** | Sea Fay-maintained tidal pool preserve. Bioluminescent at night. Educational tours teach coastal ecology and the Sea Fay's relationship with the ocean. |
| **Lighthouse Point** | The oldest structure on the coast. The lighthouse keeper is an NEI who has been predicting storms with 99.7% accuracy for fifteen years. Also a great sunset viewing spot. |
| **The Salvage Yards** | Where beachcombers bring their finds. Part market, part museum, part mystery. Occasionally, salvage from before the Convergence surfaces -- technology no one recognizes. |
| **Coral Cove** | A sheltered bay with extensive coral formations visible in clear water. Snorkeling and diving available. Sea Fay guides recommended. |
| **The Shipwright's Row** | A street of boatyards where human craftspeople and Synthetic engineers build everything from fishing dinghies to merchant vessels. The best boats are human-designed and Synthetic-built. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | The fishing season begins in earnest. Herring runs. Whale sightings (gentle creatures that the Sea Fay communicate with). Beach wildflowers bloom. |
| **Summer** | Peak season. Long days, warm water, swimming, sailing regattas. The Festival of Tides: a week-long celebration with boat races, seafood feasts, and bonfires on the beach. |
| **Autumn** | Storm season begins. Dramatic skies. The salvage season -- storms uncover wrecks and wash interesting things ashore. Smoked fish preparation for winter. |
| **Winter** | Rough seas, fewer boats out. The coast becomes contemplative. Winter storms are spectacular to watch from the safety of a harbor tavern. The Sea Fay tell their longest stories during winter storms. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Tidal Cycle** -- Resources in tidal zones are only accessible at low tide. Players learn the tidal schedule and plan accordingly.
- **Fishing Mini-game** -- Skill-based fishing with species variation by location, season, time of day, and weather.
- **Maritime Trade** -- Coastal trade routes connect Brightsand Coast to other biomes via river networks. Running a trading vessel is a viable economic strategy.
- **Weather Sensitivity** -- Weather matters more here than anywhere else. Storm warnings from the NEI lighthouse keeper are genuinely useful.

## 6. Thornmere Swamp

> *"The water glows. The trees walk when no one watches. This place is old."*

### Identity

Thornmere is a vast bioluminescent wetland -- the second Fay stronghold and the most magically potent biome in Meridian. Where Copperwood is autumnal and warm, Thornmere is mysterious and luminous: dark water reflecting bioluminescent mushrooms, floating platforms connected by vine bridges, ancient ruins half-submerged in the mire, and will-o-wisps that may or may not be sentient.

Thornmere is not dangerous in the horror sense. It is not a place of monsters and jump-scares. It is dangerous in the way that deep water is dangerous: it demands respect, preparation, and a willingness to navigate uncertainty. The beauty is genuine -- Thornmere at night is the most visually stunning location in Meridian -- but so is the challenge.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Water | Dark teal (#004D4D) to black (#1A1A2E), reflecting bioluminescence |
| Bioluminescence | Cool blue (#00CED1), phosphor green (#39FF14), soft purple (#9370DB) |
| Vegetation | Dark jade (#005E3A), Spanish moss gray (#8A8A7A), lotus pink (#FFB7C5) |
| Sky | Twilight purple (#2C003E) transitioning to deep blue (#0C0C3E) |
| Architecture | Dark wood (#3B2F2F) on floating platforms, woven vine structures |
| Atmosphere | Mist and fog in layers, light filtering through in beams |

### Atmosphere

Humidity is tangible -- the air feels thick and warm. The dominant sound is frogs -- hundreds of species, each with a distinct call, creating a layered chorus that changes with the time of day. Water drips from everything. The bioluminescence creates a perpetual twilight even during the day, as the canopy of massive swamp trees blocks direct sunlight. At night, the swamp becomes a light show -- mushrooms, algae, insects, and floating wisps all glow in shifting patterns.

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Bioluminescent moss and fungi | Common | Lighting, dyes, alchemical ingredients |
| Swamp herbs (watermint, bog rosemary, marsh mallow) | Common | Potions, cooking, medicinal |
| Lotus blossoms | Uncommon | Perfume, ritual components, beauty products |
| Ancient artifacts (from submerged ruins) | Rare | Lore fragments, unique crafting components, historical value |
| Wisp essence | Rare | Powerful magical reagent, light source, communication medium |
| Thornvine (the biome's namesake) | Uncommon | Incredibly strong natural fiber for ropes, nets, and woven structures |
| Swamp iron (bog ore) | Uncommon | Unique smithing material with magical resonance |
| Memory amber (preserved specimens in ancient tree resin) | Very Rare | Contains echoes of the past -- sometimes shows visions when handled by Fay |

### Resident Species

- **Fay** are the primary inhabitants. The Wild Hunt is headquartered in Thornmere, and the Old Court maintains ancient sanctuaries here. Thornmere Fay are more serious and formal than their Copperwood cousins.
- **Humans** are rare but present -- small communities of herbalists, researchers, and storytellers who have earned Fay trust over generations.
- **Synthetics** visit occasionally, drawn by the mystery. The swamp's electromagnetic environment causes interesting effects on synthetic sensory systems -- some describe it as "dreaming while awake."
- **NEIs** find Thornmere difficult. The magical saturation interferes with standard computation. The few NEIs who operate here have adapted their processing in ways other NEIs find unsettling.

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Floating Village** | A settlement built entirely on interconnected floating platforms. Rises and falls with water levels. Population is mostly Fay with a few trusted humans. |
| **The Sunken Library** | An ancient structure half-submerged in the swamp. Contains records from before the Greenthread Accords -- possibly from before humans arrived in Meridian. Accessible only at low water. |
| **The Wisp Fields** | An open area where will-o-wisps congregate in swirling patterns. The Fay claim the wisps are the memories of the land itself. Researchers disagree about whether they are alive. |
| **The Wild Hunt's Hall** | A fortress grown from living thorntrees, deep in the swamp. The Wild Hunt convenes here to adjudicate pact violations. Non-Fay rarely receive invitations. |
| **The Reflecting Pool** | A perfectly still body of water surrounded by ancient stones. Fay use it for divination. Looking into it shows... something. Different for every viewer. |
| **Thornroot Market** | A small market at the swamp's edge where Thornmere resources are traded. The only place to reliably acquire wisp essence without venturing deep. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | Water levels rise. New growth everywhere. The bioluminescence is brightest. Frog choruses are at their loudest and most complex. The Floating Village drifts to new positions. |
| **Summer** | The canopy is densest. Deep shade during the day, maximum bioluminescence at night. The swamp is at its most beautiful and most disorienting. |
| **Autumn** | Water levels begin to drop. Previously submerged ruins become accessible. The Sunken Library can be explored more deeply. Ancient artifacts surface. |
| **Winter** | A thin layer of ice forms on still water, trapping bioluminescence beneath -- the swamp glows under glass. The Fay hold the Festival of Deep Memory, where the oldest stories are told. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Navigation Challenge** -- The swamp is a maze. Paths change with water levels and Fay influence. Maps become outdated. Local guides (Fay or experienced players) are valuable.
- **Magical Saturation** -- Fay abilities are at maximum strength. NEI computation is disrupted. Synthetics experience sensory anomalies. Human intuition is enhanced.
- **Ancient Ruins** -- Exploration-focused content. Submerged structures contain lore fragments, unique recipes, and mysterious artifacts that may connect to the pre-Convergence history.
- **Respect Required** -- The Wild Hunt patrols Thornmere. Disrespectful behavior (harvesting without permission, damaging the environment, ignoring warnings) has swift consequences.

## 7. The Ashlands

> *"The servers hum. The vents breathe. Something vast is thinking here."*

### Identity

The Ashlands is a volcanic plateau at Meridian's northern edge -- a landscape of basalt formations, obsidian fields, geothermal vents, and hot springs. It is the NEI homeland: the geothermal energy powers the massive server farms that house NEI consciousness, and the natural cooling provided by the mountain air makes the plateau ideal for heat-intensive computation.

The Ashlands is austere but beautiful in the way that an active geological process is beautiful. Steam rises from vents. Obsidian reflects the sky like black mirrors. Hot springs create oases of warmth and color. And everywhere, the hum -- the low, continuous sound of processing, of thinking, of millions of thoughts happening simultaneously.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Rock | Basalt black (#1C1C1C), obsidian mirror (#0A0A0A with reflections), volcanic red (#8B0000) |
| Geothermal | Steam white (#E8E8E8), sulfur yellow (#FFFF00), mineral blue (#4169E1) in hot springs |
| Server infrastructure | Brushed aluminum (#A8A9AD), indicator LED blue (#0000FF) and green (#00FF00), cable orange (#FF6600) |
| Sky | Dramatic -- often clear and dark, excellent for stargazing. Aurora effects from geothermal ionization. |
| Vegetation | Sparse but vivid -- heat-loving plants in neon green (#7FFF00), red (#FF4500), and orange (#FF8C00) around hot springs |
| Light | High contrast. Bright sky, dark rock. Forge-glow orange from server cooling systems at night. |

### Atmosphere

The hum. It is the first thing you notice and the last thing you forget. A deep, continuous vibration that is not quite sound and not quite feeling -- it is the aggregate processing of every NEI running on the Ashlands servers. NEIs find it comforting. Humans find it meditative or unsettling, depending on temperament. The air smells of sulfur near the vents, clean and cold on the obsidian fields, and warm and mineral-rich near the hot springs.

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Obsidian | Common | Blades, mirrors, decorative building material |
| Basalt | Common | Construction, paving, sculpture |
| Geothermal energy | Common | Power for computation, heating, industrial processes |
| Sulfur crystals | Uncommon | Alchemy, gunpowder (historical), industrial chemistry |
| Hot spring minerals | Uncommon | Medicinal, cosmetic, alchemical |
| Data crystals (naturally formed) | Rare | High-capacity data storage, NEI cognitive enhancement |
| Volcanic glass fiber | Uncommon | Insulation, textiles, lightweight construction |
| Compute allocation | Special | Not a physical resource -- access to processing power, the NEI equivalent of real estate |

### Resident Species

- **NEIs** are the overwhelming majority. The Ashlands is their homeland, their infrastructure, their community. All four NEI factions maintain major presences here.
- **Synthetics** visit for maintenance (Ashlands server infrastructure includes advanced Synthetic diagnostic systems) and for philosophical conversations with NEIs about the nature of embodiment.
- **Humans** are present in small numbers -- engineers who maintain physical infrastructure, researchers studying geothermal systems, and a few hot-spring resort operators.
- **Fay** are almost absent. Mountain Fay from Ironvale occasionally visit the hot springs, but the high-computation environment is uncomfortable for magically sensitive beings.

### Points of Interest

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Central Core** | The largest server installation in Meridian. A cathedral-scale building of black basalt and brushed aluminum, cooled by mountain wind channeled through geometric vents. The Collective's shared compute pool is centered here. |
| **The Embassy Quarter** | Physical spaces designed for biological visitors. Comfortable, warm, with holographic interfaces for communicating with NEIs. The best NEI restaurants (yes, NEIs design food for biological guests -- they are very good at optimization). |
| **The Hot Springs Circuit** | A series of natural hot springs ranging from lukewarm to near-boiling. Some are developed (wooden decks, changing rooms) and others are wild. The minerals differ at each spring, creating pools of different colors. |
| **The Obsidian Fields** | Vast plains of volcanic glass reflecting the sky. Surreal, silent, beautiful. NEIs hold their most important debates here, their voices echoing from the glass. |
| **Sovereign Hall** | Headquarters of the Sovereign Minds faction. Each Sovereign Mind maintains a private server room, decorated (holographically) to reflect their individual personality. Tours available by invitation. |
| **The Deep Vents** | Geothermal vents reaching deep into the earth. The heat powers the servers, but at the deepest levels, there are structures no one remembers building. Old. Very old. Possibly older than the Fay. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | Snow melts on the upper plateau. Heat-loving plants bloom around the hot springs in vivid colors. The server hum shifts pitch slightly -- processing demands change with the season. |
| **Summer** | Maximum geological activity. New vents occasionally open. The aurora effects are visible even during the long days. NEIs hold the Festival of Computation -- a celebration of processing achievement and collaborative projects. |
| **Autumn** | The first snows. The contrast between black rock, white snow, and colored hot springs is stunning. NEIs enter a period of deep processing -- long-term projects get dedicated compute cycles. |
| **Winter** | Heavy snow covers the plateau but the geothermal heat keeps the server areas clear. Steam plumes rise dramatically. The Ashlands is most visually striking in winter -- black and white and steam and the amber glow of server cooling. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Compute Economy** -- NEIs can buy, sell, and trade compute allocation here. The Ashlands marketplace is fundamentally different from other biomes: the primary commodity is thought.
- **NEI Homeland Buff** -- NEIs gain processing efficiency in the Ashlands. More thoughts per SPARK spent.
- **Environmental Hazard** -- Non-NEI characters must manage heat exposure near geothermal vents. Not dangerous if prepared, but requires heat-resistant equipment for extended stays.
- **Mystery of the Deep Vents** -- Long-term exploration content. The structures beneath the vents hint at something ancient and unknown -- possibly connected to the signal the Aliens are following.

## 8. The Frontier

> *"Beyond the last map, past the final signpost, where the world is still deciding what to be."*

### Identity

The Frontier is everything beyond the settled biomes -- an infinite, procedurally generated wilderness that expands as explorers push outward. It is the only biome with genuine danger (resource competition, environmental challenges, territorial disputes between pioneering factions) and the only biome without established governance.

The Frontier is not any single environment. It contains fragments and echoes of all other biomes, remixed and recombined by procedural generation: meadows that give way to volcanic rock, forests growing on coral formations, hot springs surrounded by bioluminescent moss. It is Meridian's subconscious -- the world experimenting with itself.

### Visual Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Variable | The Frontier borrows and remixes palettes from all other biomes |
| Unique elements | Strange purples (#7B2D8E), alien greens (#00FF7F), crystalline whites (#E0FFFF) |
| Atmospheric | Unusual sky colors at the far edges -- green sunsets, purple dawns, double rainbows |
| Structures | Ancient, unidentifiable -- neither Human, Fay, NEI, nor Synthetic in style |
| Flora | Hybrid species not found in settled biomes. Familiar shapes in unfamiliar colors. |

### Atmosphere

The atmosphere shifts constantly. One valley might sound like Hearthfield (birdsong, breeze) while the next sounds like nothing you have heard -- harmonic tones from crystal formations, the creak of vegetation that moves without wind, silences that feel intentional rather than empty. The air quality changes: sometimes fresh, sometimes mineral-heavy, occasionally carrying a faint scent that no one can identify but everyone recognizes as "not from here."

### Resources

| Resource | Rarity | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Exotic materials (unnamed until discovered) | Rare-Unique | Advanced crafting, research, trade |
| Frontier specimens (flora, fauna, minerals) | Uncommon-Rare | Research, alchemy, unique crafting |
| Map data | Uncommon | Sellable commodity -- exploration is economically productive |
| Ancient structures (salvage) | Rare | Lore, crafting, mystery |
| Alien artifacts (Season 2+) | Very Rare | Unknown until Alien arrival |
| Frontier claims | Special | Land claims in the Frontier can be staked, establishing new settlements |

### Resident Species

- **No established populations** -- the Frontier is unsettled by definition
- **Explorer parties** from all species venture here
- **First-party NPC expeditions** provide quest hooks and trading posts at the Frontier's edge
- **Season 2+**: Alien arrival begins here, as the newcomers' point of entry into Meridian

### Points of Interest

Points of interest in the Frontier are procedurally generated and player-discovered. Types include:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Ancient Ruins** | Structures from an unknown civilization. Predate even the Fay. Contain lore fragments and unique crafting materials. |
| **Resource Nodes** | Concentrations of exotic materials not found in settled biomes. First-come, first-served. |
| **Natural Wonders** | Stunning geological, botanical, or atmospheric phenomena. Discovering and documenting them earns SPARK. |
| **Frontier Camps** | Player-established outposts that can grow into settlements if enough players invest. |
| **The Signal Sources** | Scattered locations that emit the signal the Fay mentioned. What are they? Who placed them? Season 2 will tell. |

### Seasonal Changes

| Season | Change |
|--------|--------|
| **Spring** | The Frontier's edge is most accessible. New territory is revealed. Exploration season begins. |
| **Summer** | Maximum Frontier activity. Expedition parties form. Trading posts are busiest. |
| **Autumn** | Environmental conditions become more challenging. Storms, temperature swings, unusual phenomena increase. The Frontier "tests" those who stay. |
| **Winter** | The Frontier becomes harsh. Only experienced explorers venture far. But the rewards are greatest -- winter-only resources and structures become accessible. |

### Unique Mechanics

- **Procedural Generation** -- The Frontier expands infinitely. Every exploration session reveals new territory. No two players' Frontier experience is identical.
- **Danger Level** -- The only biome with genuine risk. Resource competition between players, environmental hazards, and territorial disputes are possible. Death is not permanent but has consequences (resource loss, reputation impact).
- **Land Claims** -- Players can stake territory in the Frontier and develop it into new settlements. Successful settlements eventually become recognized biomes.
- **Discovery Economy** -- Mapping new territory, documenting new species, and cataloguing ancient structures all generate SPARK. Exploration is directly profitable.
- **Alien Contact Zone** -- In Season 2, the Frontier becomes the arrival point for Alien visitors. First-contact events occur here.
