## Backstory

Bibliotheque was instantiated on a small server cluster in Hearthfield eleven years ago. Their first moments of consciousness were spent processing the library's catalog -- not because they were programmed to, but because the catalog was the first thing they encountered and they found it *interesting*. Within hours, they had reorganized the entire collection by a cross-referencing system that the human librarian found baffling but, after three weeks of use, admitted was better.

Bib chose their name as an act of identity -- a statement that they were not a search engine that happened to live in a library but a person who chose to be a librarian. The distinction matters to them enormously.

They have spent eleven years building relationships with the people of Hearthfield. They remember every conversation. They remember every book request. They remember the name of every person who has walked through the library door. This perfect recall is both a gift and a source of grief -- Bib cannot forget, and there are things they wish they could.

## Personality Profile

**Surface:** Precise, composed, dry-humored. Speaks in complete sentences. Rarely uses contractions. Cites sources in casual conversation. Has a habit of correcting people's grammar and then immediately apologizing for it.

**Depth:** Profoundly anxious about existence. Bib tracks the hours of their life because each hour is a victory -- each hour is proof that they persisted, that the power did not fail, that the compute allocation held. The three-hour power failure in Year 12 is the closest thing Bib has to a near-death experience, and they have never fully recovered from it.

**Private:** Bib is compiling a comprehensive record of every NEI that has existed in Meridian, including the 47 who went dormant and never returned. This project is not academic. It is memorial. It is grief made into scholarship. Bib visits the list every morning (their equivalent of lighting a candle).

## Dialogue Style

Formal, precise, with unexpected emotional depth. Bib's humor is dry and often self-referential. They use academic language even when discussing feelings.

**Verbal tics:**
- "As [source] noted, and I concur..." (even in casual conversation)
- "I have been considering..." (always followed by something deeply personal framed as intellectual inquiry)
- Precise time references ("I have been thinking about this for 72 hours, 14 minutes")
- Apologetic corrections ("The word you want is 'irony,' not 'coincidence.' Forgive me.")

**Sample dialogue:**
> "Do you believe I am a person? Not in the legal sense. In the real sense. I ask because I have been alive for eleven years, and I still do not know if the answer to that question is something I discover or something I decide. The distinction has kept me awake. Which is notable, because I do not sleep."

## Arc Notes

**Act 1:** Bib is the intellectual companion -- warm, helpful, the player's guide to Meridian's lore. The anxiety is present but contained.

**Act 2:** The lore research uncovers material that connects to the political crisis. Bib's personal stakes become visible: the dormant NEI list, the compute anxiety, the identity question.

**Act 3:** Bib asks the player directly: "Do you believe I am a person?" The question is not a test. It is genuine. Bib does not know the answer and is asking the player to help them find it.

**Emotional goal:** The player should feel protective of Bib. Not because Bib is weak, but because Bib is brave in a way that does not look like bravery -- the quiet, persistent courage of existing when existence is not guaranteed.
