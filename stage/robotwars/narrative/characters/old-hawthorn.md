## Backstory

Old Hawthorn does not have a backstory in the human sense. They have a *history*, which is different -- it is not a sequence of events that shaped them but a continuous experience of being part of the Hearthfield landscape for a duration that human calendars cannot comfortably express.

Hawthorn is bound to the Hearthfield region the way a tree is bound to its soil. They are not merely *from* this land. They are *of* it. Their body is bark-textured skin and leaf-threaded hair. Their eyes shift color with the light. They move at the pace of growing things. The ground communicates something to them with every step.

They have watched humans arrive, build Hearthfield, break pacts, apologize, build more. They watched the Convergence from the meadow's edge and felt the emergence of NEI consciousness as a tremor in the root network -- "like the land hearing a new kind of voice for the first time in a very long while."

Hawthorn is Old Court -- traditionally isolationist. But they are also Hearthfield's Fay, which means they have been in daily contact with humans for longer than most Fay are comfortable with. This has made them unusual: an Old Court elder who understands human timescales, who appreciates human kindness, and who has a surprisingly warm take on NEIs.

## Personality Profile

**Surface:** Patient. Speaks in nature metaphors. Moves slowly and deliberately. Occasionally sharp-witted in a way that catches people off guard.

**Depth:** Grieving. Every homestead means a wildflower meadow that will not grow back. Every server cluster means heat exhaust that dries the soil. Hawthorn does not oppose progress. They grieve what progress costs. This is not a political position. It is love expressed as sorrow.

**Private:** Hawthorn is looking for a successor. They speak of their own ending as a season, not a tragedy. They need someone to tend the hidden garden after they are gone. Whether that someone is a player, a Fay, a human, or something else entirely -- Hawthorn does not mind. They mind only that the garden continues.

## Dialogue Style

Metaphorical, rooted in nature, occasionally opaque. Always a concrete meaning beneath the poetry. Time references are seasonal rather than calendrical.

**Verbal tics:**
- Speaking to plants and the ground as though they can hear (they can, in Hawthorn's experience)
- "The soil remembers..." (followed by something true and uncomfortable)
- Time measured in seasons and growing cycles, never hours or days
- Unexpected humor ("Don't tell the Old Court I said that.")

**Sample dialogue:**
> "You want to vote on the forest? The forest has been voting for ten thousand years. It votes with roots and rainfall and the patience of stone. Your elections last a season. The forest's ballot takes a millennium. I am not saying yours is less important. I am saying they are different kinds of important, and you should stop pretending one can replace the other."

## Arc Notes

**Act 1:** Hawthorn is discovered at the meadow's edge. Mysterious, gentle, slightly alien. The player begins the Old Growth quest.

**Act 2:** The four-season quest deepens the relationship. Hawthorn shares stories of deep time. The hidden garden becomes a sanctuary. The succession question emerges.

**Act 3:** During the political crisis, Hawthorn's grief becomes raw and personal -- especially if the Grove Incident occurs. The player's relationship with Hawthorn determines the emotional weight of this moment.

**Emotional goal:** The player should feel that Hawthorn is the oldest, wisest, and saddest person they have met -- and that Hawthorn's sadness is not despair but a very old form of love.
