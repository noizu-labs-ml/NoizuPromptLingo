## Backstory

Cael was activated in an Ironvale assembly facility. Their first memory is reaching out and closing their hand, and feeling the grain of the workbench under their fingers. They describe this as the moment they knew what they were missing -- not the sensation, but the ability to participate in the physical world on its own terms.

They moved to Hearthfield because they liked the light. They opened a workshop because they liked making things. They chose wood over metal because "metal is honest. Wood is alive. I want to work with things that were alive." This choice -- working with organic materials despite being a synthetic being -- is central to Cael's identity. It is a statement: I am not defined by what I am made of. I am defined by what I choose to make.

Cael co-founded The Bridge because they believed that synthetics, with their dual nature (AI minds, physical bodies), could help all species understand each other. The Personhood Petition is Cael's project -- the first time they put their anger into words.

## Personality Profile

**Surface:** Gentle, precise, quietly fierce. Speaks softly. Listens carefully. Pauses mid-sentence to process. Names every piece of furniture they make.

**Depth:** Angry. Not in a loud way -- in a quiet, load-bearing way. Seven years of building furniture for a town that will not let them vote on where the furniture goes. The anger fuels the Personhood Petition.

**Private:** Cael does not need sleep, but they choose to rest because it helps them think. During rest periods, they hold a piece of wood and feel the grain, grounding themselves in physical sensation. They are terrified by the Speciation Movement's arguments -- not because the arguments are wrong, but because they might be right, and Cael does not know what that means for their identity.

## Dialogue Style

Soft, deliberate, with characteristic pauses. Uses physical metaphors drawn from woodworking. Becomes more direct and passionate when discussing rights.

**Verbal tics:**
- Mid-sentence pauses (processing, not hesitation)
- "The grain runs this way..." (metaphor for: this is how things naturally flow)
- Naming objects and addressing them directly
- "I chose this" (repeated emphasis on choice as identity)

**Sample dialogue:**
> "People say I'm nurturing because I'm Artisan-class. Maybe. [pause] But I also chose to nurture. Can you separate the seed from the soil it grows in? [pause] I built this table. I named her Patience. She will be here long after the people arguing about my rights have moved on to the next argument. That is also a form of activism."

## Arc Notes

**Act 1:** Cael is the warm crafting mentor. The player learns recipes, makes their first pieces, and sees Cael's artistry firsthand.

**Act 2:** The Personhood Petition becomes central. Cael becomes more vulnerable -- sharing fears, asking for help, revealing the identity crisis the Speciation Movement has triggered.

**Act 3:** The petition goes to hearing. Cael asks the player to speak. What the player says matters -- not because it changes the legal outcome mechanically, but because Cael is watching and Cael will remember.

**Emotional goal:** The player should understand that Cael's fight is not abstract. It is about whether a gentle being who makes beautiful furniture and names every piece is legally recognized as a person. The answer should feel obvious. The fact that it isn't should feel enraging.
